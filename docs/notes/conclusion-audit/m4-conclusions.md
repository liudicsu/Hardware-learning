# Month 4 — conclusions and numbers stated without their derivation

Scope: every `src/content/m4/*/en.mdx` was read in full. Each row below is a `##`/`###` section that states a
conclusion, a number or a table of results without giving the reader the formula, the number's source, or the
mechanism needed to follow it. Sections that already derive their numbers (for example urdf-tf "Inertia without
guessing", gazebo-sim "Reading the sensors", slam-nav "Save the map", ros2-graph "A filter in C++") are **not**
listed. Every gap listed here exists identically in the matching `zh.mdx` of the same lesson, so each fix must be
applied to both files.

| file | heading | what is missing | suggested fix (one sentence) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| src/content/m4/ros2-control/en.mdx | `## Controllers in YAML` | The diagnostic rule is asserted with no formula behind it: "a wrong radius scales every distance, so the robot drives 10 % too far or too short; a wrong separation makes every turn come out wrong while straight lines still look fine" — nothing states how `wheel_radius`/`wheel_separation` enter the controller's arithmetic, and the "10 %" is never worked through. | Add the two formulas the controller actually runs — odometry distance `= Δθ_wheel · r` and turn rate `ω_actual = (v_right − v_left)/b = ω·b_config/b_real` with `v_left = v − ωb/2`, `v_right = v + ωb/2` — and one worked line each: with `r` 10 % too large every measured distance and every commanded speed is 10 % high while turns stay correct, and with `b` 10 % too large the commanded turn comes out 10 % long while straight lines are unaffected. |
| src/content/m4/slam-nav/en.mdx | `### What Nav2 runs` | The footprint is given as four magic numbers a beginner must copy blind: "a rectangle around `base_footprint` from 0.11 m behind to 0.21 m in front and 0.16 m to each side" — no link to m4bot's own geometry, even though the lesson knows it. | Derive it from the URDF: chassis faces at `chassis_offset_x ± chassis_x/2 = 0.05 ± 0.15` → +0.20 / −0.10 m and the tyres' outer face at `wheel_separation/2 + wheel_width/2 = 0.14 + 0.015 = 0.155` m, i.e. +0.21 / −0.11 and ±0.16 with a 1 cm margin. |
| src/content/m4/urdf-tf/en.mdx | `## The m4bot in xacro` | Point 8 states the outcome of a rotation but never the rotation: "The optical frame is rotated by `rpy="${-pi/2} 0 ${-pi/2}"`, which turns z forward, x right and y down" — a beginner cannot check that −π/2, 0, −π/2 produces that convention. | Show the composed matrix `R = Rz(−90°)·Ry(0)·Rx(−90°)` applied to the optical axes: `R·(0,0,1) = (1,0,0)`, `R·(1,0,0) = (0,−1,0)`, `R·(0,1,0) = (0,0,−1)`, i.e. optical z-forward, x-right, y-down against a body frame of x-forward, y-left, z-up. |
| src/content/m4/ros2-graph/en.mdx | `### Record and replay` | The three replayed values (21.0158, 21.0314, 21.1031) are said to "show a second effect" — "the same input produces different output" — but the α = 0.5 `/filtered` values from the original recording are never printed and no EMA step is worked, so the effect cannot be checked. | Print the recorded α = 0.5 `/filtered` values next to the replayed ones and work one EMA step with α = 0.2 (the first reading seeds the filter: `x₁ = 21.0158`, and `x₂ = 21.0937` gives `y₂ = 21.0158 + 0.2 × (21.0937 − 21.0158) = 21.0314`), so the same input with two different α is visible side by side. |
| src/content/m4/ros2-control/en.mdx | `## A drive-distance action` | Point 6 explains a real bug with an unexplained gap: "The editor's first version used depth 10 and, when a goal was cancelled, reported 0.336 m while odometry showed the robot had moved 0.412 m. Python handles callbacks slower than 50 Hz odometry arrives" — neither the callback time nor the 0.076 m of missing distance is accounted for. | Quantify the staleness bound — a depth-10 queue at 50 Hz can hold `10/50 = 0.2 s` of history, i.e. `0.2 m/s × 0.2 s = 0.04 m` of stale position at the drive speed — and state the measured callback time and how much of the remaining 0.036 m is the braking distance. |
| src/content/m4/slam-nav/en.mdx | `### What Nav2 runs` | The inflation cost function is never given — "**Inflation** adds a cost that falls off with distance around every obstacle, out to `inflation_radius: 0.45` m" — and `nav2.yaml` labels that value "tuned in the lesson" although no tuning is ever shown. | State Nav2's cost as `cost = inflation_radius − d` scaled by `cost_scaling_factor: 3.0`, and either show the run that produced 0.45 m or remove the "tuned in the lesson" comment from the YAML. |
| src/content/m4/ros2-control/en.mdx | `## Drive with teleop` | The headline distance does not follow from any number in the lesson: "pressing `i` twenty times over about two seconds moved m4bot roughly 1.15 m at teleop's default 0.5 m/s" — 0.5 m/s × 2 s is 1.0 m, and the 0.5 s `cmd_vel_timeout` coast the lesson stresses is not included in the 1.15 m. | Show `0.5 m/s × (2 s of held key + 0.5 s cmd_vel_timeout coast) ≈ 1.25 m` and explain why the measured 1.15 m is lower (tapped keys rather than a held key), or drop the precise figure for a range. |
| src/content/m4/gazebo-sim/en.mdx | `## Choosing a simulator` | The hardware floor is a hard number with no source or date: "the minimum is an RTX 4080 with 16 GB VRAM and 32 GB RAM; data-centre cards without RT cores (A100, H100) are not supported" — and the comparison "MuJoCo … the fastest and most accurate contact dynamics; the research standard" has no benchmark behind it. | Link NVIDIA's Isaac Sim system-requirements page and state the month the check was made, and replace the MuJoCo superlative with a named benchmark or a verifiable citation. |
| src/content/m4/gazebo-sim/en.mdx | `## Sensors on the robot` | The distance that justifies the 0.20 m minimum range is given without its derivation: "The mast stands 0.18 m behind the lidar, so beams pointing backwards would hit the robot itself" — 0.18 m is never computed from the two joint origins the reader has just seen. | Show the subtraction from the URDF, `lidar_joint` at x = 0.12 and `mast_joint` at x = −0.06, so `0.12 − (−0.06) = 0.18 m`, and note explicitly that a 0.20 m minimum range is just above it. |
| src/content/m4/slam-nav/en.mdx | `### Drive the tour` | The driving advice is a claim with no numbers: "drive slowly, especially when turning (fast turns smear a scan, because the robot moves while the lidar sweeps)" — the reader is told what not to do but not how much smear each turn buys. | Put a number on it: at the lidar's 10 Hz the chassis rotates `ω/10` rad during one scan, so the tour's own 0.6 rad/s clamp smears 0.06 rad (≈3.4°) per scan while a 0.3 rad/s turn smears only 0.03 rad. |
| src/content/m4/ros2-graph/en.mdx | `### A publisher in Python` | The test signal is only described in words — "a slow wave around 21 degrees plus a little Gaussian noise" — so amplitude, period and noise level are never quantified even though the code fixes them. | State them from the code: amplitude 0.5 °C, one cycle every 50 samples (10 s at the 5 Hz default) and Gaussian noise with σ = 0.2 °C. |
| src/content/m4/ros2-setup/en.mdx | `## Install it` | Two figures in the route table are unsourced: "free tier; paid from €39.97/month" for The Construct and "about 2 GB" for the pixi/RoboStack download — no pricing link, no date, no measurement. | Link The Construct's pricing page and date the check, and replace "about 2 GB" with the measured size of the pixi environment (e.g. `du -sh .pixi` after `pixi install`). |
