## Why vision alone falls short on precise insertion

In plug-into-socket or peg-into-hole tasks, the last few millimetres happen where the gripper and the object block the camera:

- At contact the camera mostly sees the back of the gripper; a lateral offset is a few pixels in the image.
- Visual pose estimates often carry a fixed bias (calibration, occlusion, lighting) that does not go away by "looking again".
- Touch gives a signal exactly at contact: the direction of shear tells you which way you are off.

AnySkin (arXiv 2409.08276) plug-insertion experiment (Table III, take the paper as source of truth): cameras only ≈ **1.7**/10; cameras + AnySkin ≈ **6.7**/10; with a fresh skin and no policy retraining ≈ **5.3**/10.

## How touch enters a policy

A common structure is **modality-specific encoders + a shared trunk**:

1. Camera image → vision encoder → vision tokens;
2. Tactile reading (magnetometer vector or optical tactile image) → small encoder → tactile tokens;
3. Proprioception → MLP → state token;
4. All tokens go through one Transformer trunk, whose output conditions the action head — a BC regression head, a diffusion head or [10.1](/en/month/month-10/flow-matching-policies)'s flow-matching head all work.

BAKU (arXiv 2406.07539) is one readable example of this "observation trunk + multi-sensory observations + action chunking + action head" combination.

<Resource id="baku-paper" />

Two practical points:

- **Gating / a contact flag.** Out of contact, tactile tokens are mostly noise; a common fix is a contact flag, or letting the network learn to ignore them when not touching. The toy below shows the same intuition with an explicit threshold.
- **Synchronisation.** Tactile sensors often sample faster than cameras; align by timestamp when recording (see [8.1 dataset engineering](/en/month/month-8/dataset-engineering)), or tactile tokens and actions drift apart.

## Replaceability is the precondition for scale

Elastomers wear out and get replaced. If a new skin changes the signal, policies trained on old data lose performance and tactile data never accumulates. AnySkin Table III: AnySkin **6.7**/10 → **5.3**/10 (about **14** percentage points; relative drop (6.7−5.3)/6.7 ≈ **21 %**); ReSkin **6.0**/10 → **1.7**/10 (about **43** percentage points; relative drop ≈ **72 %**). Percentage points are not relative drops — do not mix them; take the paper counts as source of truth. So when choosing a tactile sensor, cross-instance signal consistency matters as much as sensitivity; without it you need a quick re-calibration after each swap, or training data from several skins.

## Gated insertion toy

`tactile_gate_toy.py`: 1-D insertion with the hole at 0 mm; the vision estimate has a fixed 1.5 mm bias plus noise, and insertion succeeds only within 0.5 mm. All three policies share the same vision draws:

1. **Vision only**: insert once at the vision estimate.
2. **Vision + gated touch**: on contact the skin reports a shear proportional to the lateral error (plus noise); only when |shear| exceeds a threshold does the policy shift by −shear / assumed gain; at most 2 contacts per trial.
3. **Swapped skin**: same policy, but the new skin's true gain is 0.6 while the policy still assumes 1.0 (no re-calibration).

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""tactile_gate_toy.py: 1-D peg insertion, vision-only vs vision + gated tactile (stdlib).

The hole is at 0 mm. A vision policy aims with a fixed lateral bias plus noise.
Insertion succeeds if the lateral error is within TOL_MM.

  - vision only: one try at the vision estimate
  - + tactile (gated): on contact the skin reports a shear proportional to the
    lateral error (plus noise). Only if |shear| > GATE the policy shifts by
    -shear / ASSUMED_GAIN; up to MAX_PROBES contacts per trial
  - + tactile, swapped skin: same policy, but the replacement skin's true gain
    differs from the gain the policy was trained with (no re-calibration)

Fixed seed; all trials share the same vision draws. This is a toy, not AnySkin.
Also prints AnySkin (arXiv 2409.08276) insertion constants used in the lesson
prose — paper numbers, not toy measurements. Take the paper as source of truth.
"""
import random

SEED = 17
TRIALS = 200
BIAS_MM = 1.5
VISION_NOISE_MM = 0.6
TOL_MM = 0.5
SHEAR_NOISE = 0.3
GATE = 0.4
ASSUMED_GAIN = 1.0
SWAPPED_GAIN = 0.6
MAX_PROBES = 2

rng = random.Random(SEED)
vision = [BIAS_MM + rng.gauss(0.0, VISION_NOISE_MM) for _ in range(TRIALS)]
shear_noise = [[rng.gauss(0.0, SHEAR_NOISE) for _ in range(MAX_PROBES)] for _ in range(TRIALS)]


def run(true_gain, use_touch):
    ok, probes = 0, 0
    for i in range(TRIALS):
        err = vision[i]  # if |err| <= TOL_MM the peg slides in and we stop
        if use_touch:
            for k in range(MAX_PROBES):
                if abs(err) <= TOL_MM:
                    break
                shear = true_gain * err + shear_noise[i][k]
                probes += 1
                if abs(shear) > GATE:
                    err -= shear / ASSUMED_GAIN
        if abs(err) <= TOL_MM:
            ok += 1
    return ok / TRIALS, probes / TRIALS


succ_vis, _ = run(ASSUMED_GAIN, False)
succ_tac, probes_tac = run(ASSUMED_GAIN, True)
succ_swap, probes_swap = run(SWAPPED_GAIN, True)

print(f"seed={SEED}")
print(f"trials={TRIALS}")
print(f"tol_mm={TOL_MM}")
print(f"success_vision_only={succ_vis:.3f}")
print(f"success_tactile_gated={succ_tac:.3f}")
print(f"success_tactile_swapped={succ_swap:.3f}")
print(f"mean_probes_tactile={probes_tac:.2f}")
print(f"mean_probes_swapped={probes_swap:.2f}")
# AnySkin paper constants (arXiv 2409.08276, insertion, successes out of 10)
print("anyskin_plug_cam=1.7")
print("anyskin_plug_skin=6.7")
print("anyskin_plug_swap=5.3")
```

## Contact residual and a slip proxy

The gate toy above took "in contact" as given. On a real arm you have to build such signals yourself, and the first question is what you are allowed to compare with what.

### Compare like with like

A residual only means something between two estimates of the **same quantity, in the same frame and the same unit**. In `contact_slip_residual_toy.py`:

- `v_pred = J(q_meas) · qdot_meas` — the fingertip velocity predicted from joint encoders through forward kinematics of a planar 2-link arm (`L1 = 0.30 m`, `L2 = 0.25 m`), world frame, m/s. This is the same contact-Jacobian idea as `J_c qdot` in [11.6 humanoid WBC](/en/month/month-11/humanoid-wbc-overview); the floating-base nullspace projector is not redone here.
- `v_meas` — a synthetic measured object/contact velocity in the same world frame, m/s. In a lab it might come from motion capture or object tracking; that is not run on this page.
- `r = v_meas − v_pred`, then split along the table tangent `t = (1, 0)` and normal `n = (0, 1)`.

<Formula label="Velocity residual (same frame, m/s)">r = v_meas − J(q) qdot,   r_t = r · t,   r_n = r · n</Formula>

A force in N is **never** subtracted from a velocity in m/s. The toy's `Vel` class carries a unit and a frame and refuses mixed subtraction: `unit_guard: refuse N@world - m/s@world`.

### Sensing assumption for contact

The toy **assumes** a separate normal-force channel `f_n` (N), thresholded into a contact flag `f_n > 2.0 N`. This is a written assumption about the sensor, not something derived from velocities. Contact **break** is decided only from that flag. The normal-velocity residual `r_n` is printed but never used to decide break: a large `r_n` can also come from an encoder glitch, a kinematic model error or a moving table, and without a declared sensor you cannot tell them apart.

### Slip is a toy proxy

While the contact flag is true, the toy raises a slip alarm when the mean of `|r_t|` over 5 ticks exceeds 0.02 m/s for 3 ticks in a row. That is this page's definition, not a GelSight or AnySkin slip classifier; real slip detectors are sensor-specific (see [11.3](/en/month/month-11/tactile-sensing)). Stick, slip and break share one seeded noise stream (joint angle, joint rate, measured velocity and force noise), so differences between rows come from the scenario, not from luck.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""contact_slip_residual_toy.py: same-frame velocity residual + slip proxy (stdlib).

A planar 2-link arm (L1, L2 in m) pushes an object along a table. Every
residual compares LIKE quantities: the fingertip velocity predicted from
proprioception, v_pred = J(q_meas) @ qdot_meas (forward kinematics, world
frame, m/s), against a synthetic measured object/contact velocity v_meas
(world frame, m/s). Both are then projected on the table tangent t and normal
n. A force (N) is never subtracted from a velocity (m/s): the Vel guard below
refuses mixed units or frames.

Contact break is read ONLY from an explicit assumed sensor channel: a normal-
force reading f_n (N) thresholded into a contact flag (toy assumption written
in the lesson). The normal velocity residual is printed but never used to
decide "break".

Slip is a toy PROXY: while the contact flag is true, the tangential residual
(mean over SLIP_WINDOW ticks) above SLIP_THRESH for SLIP_HOLD ticks raises a
slip alarm. It is not a GelSight / AnySkin slip classifier.

Scenarios stick / slip / break share one seeded noise stream (joint, velocity
and force noise). The contact-Jacobian idea is the same as 11.6's J_c qdot;
this toy does not redo the floating-base projector.
usage: python3 contact_slip_residual_toy.py
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

SEED = 117
DT = 0.01                 # s
HORIZON = 200             # ticks
L1, L2 = 0.30, 0.25       # m
ONSET = 100               # tick where slip or break begins
SLIP_RAMP = 20            # ticks to reach full slip speed
SLIP_SPEED = 0.05         # m/s tangential lag at full slip
F_CONTACT = 5.0           # N while pressing
F_THRESH = 2.0            # N: assumed contact-flag threshold
Q_NOISE = 0.001           # rad
QD_NOISE = 0.01           # rad/s
V_NOISE = 0.004           # m/s on the measured velocity
F_NOISE = 0.3             # N
SLIP_WINDOW = 5           # ticks
SLIP_THRESH = 0.02        # m/s
SLIP_HOLD = 3             # consecutive ticks over threshold
TANGENT = (1.0, 0.0)      # table tangent, world frame
NORMAL = (0.0, 1.0)       # table normal, world frame


class Vel:
    """A 2-D quantity tagged with unit and frame; only m/s in 'world' here."""

    def __init__(self, x: float, y: float, unit: str, frame: str) -> None:
        self.x, self.y, self.unit, self.frame = x, y, unit, frame

    def __sub__(self, other: "Vel") -> "Vel":
        if self.unit != other.unit or self.frame != other.frame:
            raise TypeError(f"refuse {self.unit}@{self.frame} - {other.unit}@{other.frame}")
        return Vel(self.x - other.x, self.y - other.y, self.unit, self.frame)

    def along(self, axis: tuple[float, float]) -> float:
        return self.x * axis[0] + self.y * axis[1]


def joints(k: int) -> tuple[float, float, float, float]:
    t = k * DT
    w = 2 * math.pi * 0.5
    q1 = 0.5 + 0.25 * math.sin(w * t)
    q2 = 1.1 + 0.15 * math.sin(2 * w * t)
    return q1, q2, 0.25 * w * math.cos(w * t), 0.15 * 2 * w * math.cos(2 * w * t)


def fk_velocity(q1: float, q2: float, qd1: float, qd2: float) -> Vel:
    """v = J(q) qdot for the fingertip of a planar 2-link arm (world, m/s)."""
    s1, c1 = math.sin(q1), math.cos(q1)
    s12, c12 = math.sin(q1 + q2), math.cos(q1 + q2)
    vx = (-L1 * s1 - L2 * s12) * qd1 - L2 * s12 * qd2
    vy = (L1 * c1 + L2 * c12) * qd1 + L2 * c12 * qd2
    return Vel(vx, vy, "m/s", "world")


def shared_noise() -> list[tuple[float, ...]]:
    rng = random.Random(SEED)
    return [tuple(rng.gauss(0.0, s) for s in
                  (Q_NOISE, Q_NOISE, QD_NOISE, QD_NOISE, V_NOISE, V_NOISE, F_NOISE))
            for _ in range(HORIZON)]


def run(scenario: str, noise: list[tuple[float, ...]], gated: bool = True) -> dict:
    window: list[float] = []
    over = 0
    slip_alarm_at = break_flag_at = None
    false_slip = 0
    pre, post = [], []
    max_normal_resid_post = 0.0
    for k in range(HORIZON):
        q1, q2, qd1, qd2 = joints(k)
        nq1, nq2, nqd1, nqd2, nvx, nvy, nf = noise[k]
        v_true = fk_velocity(q1, q2, qd1, qd2)
        v_pred = fk_velocity(q1 + nq1, q2 + nq2, qd1 + nqd1, qd2 + nqd2)
        # Synthetic object velocity in the same world frame (m/s).
        lag = 0.0
        if scenario == "slip" and k >= ONSET:
            lag = SLIP_SPEED * min(1.0, (k - ONSET + 1) / SLIP_RAMP)
        if scenario == "break" and k >= ONSET:
            obj = Vel(0.0, 0.0, "m/s", "world")   # object left behind
            f_n = 0.0
        else:
            obj = Vel(v_true.x - lag * TANGENT[0], v_true.y - lag * TANGENT[1], "m/s", "world")
            f_n = F_CONTACT
        v_meas = Vel(obj.x + nvx, obj.y + nvy, "m/s", "world")
        f_meas = f_n + nf                          # N: separate channel
        contact = f_meas > F_THRESH                # assumed sensor channel
        r = v_meas - v_pred                        # m/s - m/s, same frame
        r_t, r_n = r.along(TANGENT), r.along(NORMAL)
        (post if k >= ONSET else pre).append(math.hypot(r.x, r.y))
        if k >= ONSET:
            max_normal_resid_post = max(max_normal_resid_post, abs(r_n))
        if not contact and gated:
            if break_flag_at is None and k >= ONSET:
                break_flag_at = k
            window.clear()
            over = 0
            continue                               # slip proxy suspended
        window.append(abs(r_t))
        if len(window) > SLIP_WINDOW:
            window.pop(0)
        mean_t = sum(window) / len(window)
        over = over + 1 if mean_t > SLIP_THRESH else 0
        if over >= SLIP_HOLD:
            if scenario != "stick" and k >= ONSET:
                if slip_alarm_at is None:
                    slip_alarm_at = k
            else:
                false_slip += 1
    rms = lambda xs: math.sqrt(sum(x * x for x in xs) / len(xs))
    return {"pre": rms(pre), "post": rms(post), "slip_at": slip_alarm_at,
            "break_at": break_flag_at, "false_slip": false_slip,
            "max_rn_post": max_normal_resid_post}


def main() -> None:
    noise = shared_noise()
    print(f"seed={SEED} dt_s={DT} horizon={HORIZON} onset_tick={ONSET} "
          f"slip_speed_mps={SLIP_SPEED} ramp_ticks={SLIP_RAMP}")
    print("residual r = v_meas - J(q_meas) qdot_meas  [m/s, world frame; t=(1,0) n=(0,1)]")
    print(f"contact flag = f_n > {F_THRESH} N (assumed sensor channel); "
          f"slip proxy: mean|r_t| over {SLIP_WINDOW} > {SLIP_THRESH} m/s for {SLIP_HOLD} ticks")
    try:
        _ = Vel(5.0, 0.0, "N", "world") - fk_velocity(*joints(0))
        guard = "accepted (BUG)"
    except TypeError as exc:
        guard = str(exc)
    print(f"unit_guard: {guard}")
    print(f"{'scenario':<8} {'rms_r_pre':>10} {'rms_r_post':>11} {'slip_alarm_tick':>15} "
          f"{'slip_latency_ms':>15} {'flag_drop_tick':>14} {'false_slip_ticks':>16}")
    res = {}
    for sc in ("stick", "slip", "break"):
        r = res[sc] = run(sc, noise)
        lat = "-" if r["slip_at"] is None else f"{(r['slip_at'] - ONSET) * DT * 1000:.0f}"
        print(f"{sc:<8} {r['pre']:>10.4f} {r['post']:>11.4f} "
              f"{'-' if r['slip_at'] is None else r['slip_at']:>15} {lat:>15} "
              f"{'-' if r['break_at'] is None else r['break_at']:>14} {r['false_slip']:>16d}")
    ungated = run("break", noise, gated=False)
    print(f"break_ungated: slip_alarm_tick={ungated['slip_at']} "
          "(without the contact flag the proxy would call a break 'slip')")
    print(f"break: max|r_n| after onset = {res['break']['max_rn_post']:.4f} m/s "
          "(printed, not used to decide break)")
    assert res["stick"]["false_slip"] == 0 and res["slip"]["slip_at"] is not None
    assert res["break"]["break_at"] == ONSET and res["break"]["slip_at"] is None
    assert res["break"]["false_slip"] == 0 and ungated["slip_at"] is not None
    assert all(abs(res[s]["pre"] - res["stick"]["pre"]) < 1e-12 for s in res)
    print("ok: shared noise (identical pre-onset residuals); break from contact flag only; "
          "slip = toy proxy, not a GelSight/AnySkin classifier")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### How to read it

- **Shared noise:** the residual RMS before tick 100 is **0.0081** m/s in all three scenarios — the same draws, as the final assertion checks.
- **stick:** after tick 100 the RMS stays at **0.0082** m/s and there are **0** false slip ticks.
- **slip:** from tick 100 the object lags the fingertip by a tangential speed that ramps to 0.05 m/s over 20 ticks. RMS rises to **0.0484** m/s; the alarm fires at tick **111**, a latency of **110** ms, with **0** false alarms. The latency includes the ramp, the 5-tick window and the 3-tick hold: lower thresholds alarm sooner but start firing on noise (with `SLIP_THRESH = 0.008` the alarm comes at 60 ms, but stick shows 3 false slip ticks and the final assertion fails on purpose).
- **break:** at tick 100 the object stops and `f_n` drops; the flag drops at tick **100** (the toy force channel has no filter, so 0 ms — a real sensor is slower). RMS jumps to **0.3088** m/s and max `|r_n|` is **0.2635** m/s, yet there is no slip alarm, because the flag suspends the proxy.
- **break_ungated:** the same residual without the flag raises a slip alarm at tick **103**: the proxy would call a break "slip". This is why the contact channel and its assumption are written down.

- Residual toy: compare `v_meas` with `J(q) qdot` in one frame and unit (m/s), never N − m/s; break only from the assumed force-flag channel; slip is a toy proxy (alarm at tick 111, 110 ms, 0 false slip ticks); without the flag a break would be called slip (tick 103).
- No tactile sensor purchase required; on-robot steps not run on this page; no new capstone track.

**Common mistakes**

- Leaving the tactile channel ungated, so out-of-contact noise steers the policy.
- Recording without timestamp-aligning touch and camera.
- Skipping re-calibration after a skin swap and blaming the policy.
- Subtracting a force from a velocity, or comparing velocities expressed in different frames.
- Inferring contact break from the normal velocity residual without a declared contact sensor.
- Calling a residual-threshold proxy a slip classifier.
