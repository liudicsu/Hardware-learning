## Why embodiment pools skew

Open X-Embodiment (OXE) pools many robot datasets into one ecosystem so a single policy can see many arms and grippers. The project page reports **1M+** real trajectories, **22** embodiments and about **60** datasets. Pooling alone does not make the mixture balanced: OXE-AugE (arXiv 2512.13100) notes that in the original OXE real data the **top-4** embodiments account for **more than 85 %** of trajectories. A fine-tune that ignores that skew mostly learns the majority arms.

## OXE-AugE in one paragraph (take the paper as source of truth)

OXE-AugE applies robot-appearance augmentation on **16** common subsets and reports about **4.4M** trajectories — more than **3×** the original OXE pool. ### Staged augmentation and what it does not transfer
We opened [OXE-AugE HTML §4.2 and Appendix A.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13100v1); take the paper as source of truth. Read the pipeline as four checkpoints, not "recolour an arm and declare transfer":

| Stage | Input → output | Audit / failure case |
|---|---|---|
| Mask fusion | Learned SAM2 mask + simulated robot mask → aligned, pruned, smoothed mask | Inspect gripper boundaries and missing background; a wrong mask erases objects. |
| Inpaint | Masked source video → reconstructed background (E2FGVI) | Check temporal flicker and hallucinated object edges. |
| Base-position tune | Source end-effector path + target kinematics → feasible base translation | Replay tracking error controls accept/reject; discard unreachable paths rather than inventing actions. |
| Replay/composite | Target-robot replay/render + reconstructed scene → augmented video | Inspect robot/object occlusion and pose/action alignment. |

The stage names are documentation constants in the CPU toy, **not implementations** of SAM2, inpainting, IK or rendering. Appearance augmentation preserves task/scene intent, not contact dynamics; differing grippers may need different strategies. Keep source/augmented/held-out robot identities explicit and split original trajectories before augmentation to avoid source-derived train/test leakage. This is complementary to 8.1's single-arm audit, not a replacement.

### A.5: partial score is not binary success

| Highest milestone reached | Score |
|---|---:|
| Moves toward the object and attempts a grasp | 0.3 |
| Grasps the object | 0.5 |
| Carries it toward the correct destination | 0.8 |
| Places it at the target | 1.0 |

Use one milestone score per trial, not a sum of stages. A.5 evaluates **10 trials × 4 tasks = 40** per policy per embodiment and reports **mean ± standard error of scores**. This can differ from the fraction of fully completed placements. Our synthetic no-milestone outcome is 0 by explicit toy convention; A.5 does not explicitly define that case. The toy computes SE as sample standard deviation / sqrt(40), assuming independent trials; shared task conditions or correlations need more careful uncertainty treatment. It does not recreate the paper's outcomes or error bars.

The abstract and §6.2 call the **24–45 %** improvement "success"; A.5's scored mean is **not binary success rate**. The denominator / absolute-vs-relative interpretation of that lift is **uncertain here**: do not silently turn it into percentage points or recompute a relative lift without specifying baseline and aggregation. Figures and paper definitions remain authoritative.

## UniVLA: task-centric latent actions, decoded per embodiment

The skew above is about **how many** trajectories each arm contributes. This section asks something different: how can one policy learn from video of **many** arms when only a few steps of the target arm carry motor labels? It is not another OXE-AugE count or score toy. It is also not the LAPA / IDM pseudo-label curve in [9.9](/en/month/month-9/world-models-overview), which labels generated video for one robot. Here the question is cross-embodiment transfer through a shared latent action space.

<Resource id="univla-paper" />

**What the paper does (opened at arXiv 2505.06111; numbers 以论文为准).** Other projects also use the name UniVLA; this section means only the paper by Bu et al. (arXiv 2505.06111). UniVLA (Bu et al., RSS 2025) learns latent actions from pairs of video frames in DINOv2 feature space, with a VQ-VAE codebook. Training has two stages:

- **Stage 1:** the instruction (T5 embeddings) is fed to the latent model, so its codes absorb the **task-irrelevant** dynamics, such as other agents or camera shake.
- **Stage 2:** that codebook is reused and a new set of **task-centric** latent actions is learned.

The policy is Prismatic-7B, with the vocabulary extended by |C| action tokens and N = 4 tokens per step (|C| = 16, so 16⁴ choices instead of OpenVLA's 256⁷). Small per-embodiment decoder heads (10.8M parameters) turn latents into robot actions. The paper notes that LAPA-style latents encode every visual change, and that training on task-irrelevant latents gives near-zero success on LIBERO-Long. It reports 960 A100-hours of pretraining against 21,500 for OpenVLA, and gains over OpenVLA of 18.5 % on LIBERO, 29.6 % in navigation and 36.7 % on real robots. None of this was reproduced here: the 7B pretraining, the GPUs and the robots are **本页未运行**.

### Fair setup

`univla_latent_action_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. An end-effector must reach the goal named by the instruction ("left" or "right") and go around a round obstacle; touching it is a failure. A second blob, the **wanderer**, moves on its own. Two embodiments see the same scenes, but **B** maps a motor command differently: its axes are swapped, one is flipped and the gains differ. Video episodes, starts, wanderer paths and evaluation starts are shared per seed, over five seeds.

| Arm | Trained on | Output |
|---|---|---|
| A_raw_bc_A | A's labelled motor commands only | motor command, used as-is on A and B |
| B_latent_lang | latent codes of the **EE's** frame change, from unlabelled video of A and B | code → per-embodiment linear decoder |
| C_latent_no_lang | latent codes of the **whole** frame change (EE and wanderer), same video | code → per-embodiment linear decoder |
| D_raw_bc_small | only the 20 labelled steps that the decoders get | motor command |

**Equal budgets.** B and C use the same 12-entry codebook, 10 Lloyd iterations, video, tabular policy grid and 20 labelled decoder steps per embodiment. The decoder input is the code's centroid: 2 numbers for B and 4 for C (labelled). Language is modelled as a known mask that says which blob is the actor. Real UniVLA learns that split from DINOv2 features in two stages, which is **not** modelled here. Metrics are success and collision rates on each embodiment over 40 episodes, mean±sd over five seeds. There are two scenarios: **wanderer** (σ 0.06 per step) and **still_scene** (the wanderer never moves).

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""univla_latent_action_toy.py: task-centric latent actions vs raw motor BC (stdlib).

Synthetic 2-D world in [0, 1]^2: an end-effector (EE) must reach the goal named
by the instruction ("left" or "right") and go around a round obstacle in the
middle; touching it ends the episode as a failure. A second blob (another agent,
the task-irrelevant dynamics) wanders on its own. Frames show both blobs.

Two embodiments see the SAME scenes but map a motor command m to an EE step
differently: A: step = m; B: step = (-0.6 * m_y, 1.4 * m_x) (axes swapped, one
flipped, different gains). Shared per seed: video episodes, starts, wanderer
paths and evaluation starts (common random numbers). Arms:
  A_raw_bc_A         tabular BC from EE cell + instruction to motor, trained on
                     A's labelled episodes only
  B_latent_lang      latent action = codebook index of the EE's frame-to-frame
                     change (the instruction marks which blob is the actor; real
                     UniVLA learns this split from DINOv2 features in two stages,
                     not modelled); policy on unlabelled video of A AND B; a
                     per-embodiment linear decoder from the code's centroid
  C_latent_no_lang   the same, but the code covers the whole frame change (EE and
                     wanderer), as in latent models without language conditioning
  D_raw_bc_small     tabular BC trained only on the decoder's labelled steps
Equal budgets: the same codebook size, Lloyd iterations, video, grid and decoder
labels (DEC_LABELS labelled steps per embodiment) for B, C and D; decoder input
is 2 numbers for B and 4 for C (labelled). Not UniVLA, no VLM, no GPU.
usage: python3 univla_latent_action_toy.py
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
import statistics

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
STEP = 0.05
OBST, OBST_R = (0.5, 0.5), 0.17
GOALS = {"left": (0.12, 0.88), "right": (0.88, 0.88)}
GRID = 10
VIDEO_EPISODES = 80        # per embodiment
DEC_LABELS = 20            # labelled steps per embodiment for decoders (and D)
CODES = 12
LLOYD_ITERS = 10
T_MAX = 40
N_EVAL = 40
SCENARIOS = (("wanderer", 0.06), ("still_scene", 0.0))
ARMS = ("A_raw_bc_A", "B_latent_lang", "C_latent_no_lang", "D_raw_bc_small")


def to_step(emb, m):
    return (m[0], m[1]) if emb == "A" else (-0.6 * m[1], 1.4 * m[0])


def to_motor(emb, d):
    return (d[0], d[1]) if emb == "A" else (d[1] / 1.4, -d[0] / 0.6)


def blocked(a, b):
    """Does the segment a->b pass within OBST_R + 0.04 of the obstacle centre?"""
    vx, vy = b[0] - a[0], b[1] - a[1]
    t = max(0.0, min(1.0, ((OBST[0] - a[0]) * vx + (OBST[1] - a[1]) * vy) / (vx * vx + vy * vy + 1e-12)))
    return math.hypot(a[0] + t * vx - OBST[0], a[1] + t * vy - OBST[1]) < OBST_R + 0.04


def expert_step(ee, goal):
    """Straight to the goal, or first to a waypoint below and beside the obstacle."""
    tgt = goal
    if blocked(ee, goal):
        side = 1.0 if goal[0] > OBST[0] else -1.0
        tgt = (OBST[0] + side * 0.3, min(ee[1], 0.26))
        if math.hypot(tgt[0] - ee[0], tgt[1] - ee[1]) < 1e-9:
            tgt = goal
    dx, dy = tgt[0] - ee[0], tgt[1] - ee[1]
    dist = math.hypot(dx, dy)
    s = min(STEP, dist)
    return (s * dx / dist, s * dy / dist)


def clip(p):
    return (min(max(p[0], 0.0), 1.0), min(max(p[1], 0.0), 1.0))


def hit(p):
    return math.hypot(p[0] - OBST[0], p[1] - OBST[1]) < OBST_R


def start(rng):
    while True:
        p = (rng.uniform(0.05, 0.95), rng.uniform(0.05, 0.4))
        if not hit(p):
            return p


def cell(ee, task):
    return (min(GRID - 1, int(ee[0] * GRID)), min(GRID - 1, int(ee[1] * GRID)), task)


def wander(w, rng, sigma):
    return clip((w[0] + rng.gauss(0.0, sigma), w[1] + rng.gauss(0.0, sigma)))


def video(emb, rng, sigma):
    """Expert episodes: (cell, motor, ee_change, wanderer_change) per step."""
    rows = []
    for _ in range(VIDEO_EPISODES):
        task = rng.choice(("left", "right"))
        ee, w = start(rng), (rng.random(), rng.random())
        for _ in range(T_MAX):
            d = expert_step(ee, GOALS[task])
            m = to_motor(emb, d)
            m = (m[0] + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.002), m[1] + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.002))
            st = to_step(emb, m)
            nee = clip((ee[0] + st[0], ee[1] + st[1]))
            nw = wander(w, rng, sigma)
            rows.append((cell(ee, task), m, (nee[0] - ee[0], nee[1] - ee[1]), (nw[0] - w[0], nw[1] - w[1])))
            ee, w = nee, nw
            if math.hypot(ee[0] - GOALS[task][0], ee[1] - GOALS[task][1]) < 0.06:
                break
    return rows


def d2(a, b):
    return sum((x - y) ** 2 for x, y in zip(a, b))


def kmeans(data, k, rng):
    cent = [list(p) for p in rng.sample(data, k)]
    for _ in range(LLOYD_ITERS):
        sums, cnt = [[0.0] * len(data[0]) for _ in cent], [0] * k
        for p in data:
            i = min(range(k), key=lambda c: d2(p, cent[c]))
            cnt[i] += 1
            sums[i] = [s + v for s, v in zip(sums[i], p)]
        cent = [[s / cnt[i] for s in sums[i]] if cnt[i] else cent[i] for i in range(k)]
    return cent


def lstsq(xs, ys):
    """Least squares y = W x (no bias), solved with normal equations + tiny ridge."""
    n = len(xs[0])
    a = [[sum(x[i] * x[j] for x in xs) + (1e-6 if i == j else 0.0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
    W = []
    for k in range(len(ys[0])):
        b = [sum(x[i] * y[k] for x, y in zip(xs, ys)) for i in range(n)]
        m = [row[:] + [bb] for row, bb in zip(a, b)]
        for c in range(n):
            piv = max(range(c, n), key=lambda r: abs(m[r][c]))
            m[c], m[piv] = m[piv], m[c]
            for r in range(n):
                if r != c:
                    f = m[r][c] / m[c][c]
                    m[r] = [u - f * v for u, v in zip(m[r], m[c])]
        W.append([m[i][n] / m[i][i] for i in range(n)])
    return W


def table(pairs):
    """cell -> mean value (motor) or majority value (code)."""
    acc = {}
    for c, v in pairs:
        acc.setdefault(c, []).append(v)
    return acc


def lookup(tab, key, reduce):
    if key in tab:
        return reduce(tab[key])
    near = min((k for k in tab if k[2] == key[2]), key=lambda k: (k[0] - key[0]) ** 2 + (k[1] - key[1]) ** 2)
    return reduce(tab[near])


def mean_vec(vs):
    return tuple(sum(v[i] for v in vs) / len(vs) for i in range(len(vs[0])))


def majority(vs):
    return max(sorted(set(vs)), key=vs.count)


def rollout(emb, act, rng, sigma):
    task = rng.choice(("left", "right"))
    ee, w = start(rng), (rng.random(), rng.random())
    for t in range(T_MAX):
        m = act(cell(ee, task))
        s = to_step(emb, m)
        n = math.hypot(s[0], s[1])
        if n > 2 * STEP:                       # actuator saturation, same for every arm
            s = (s[0] * 2 * STEP / n, s[1] * 2 * STEP / n)
        ee = clip((ee[0] + s[0], ee[1] + s[1]))
        w = wander(w, rng, sigma)
        if hit(ee):
            return 0, 1
        if math.hypot(ee[0] - GOALS[task][0], ee[1] - GOALS[task][1]) < 0.06:
            return 1, 0
    return 0, 0


def run_seed(seed, sigma):
    vids = {e: video(e, random.Random(100 * seed + (1 if e == "A" else 2)), sigma) for e in ("A", "B")}
    feats = {"B_latent_lang": lambda r: list(r[2]), "C_latent_no_lang": lambda r: list(r[2]) + list(r[3])}
    pol, books = {}, {}
    for arm, f in feats.items():
        allrows = vids["A"] + vids["B"]
        book = kmeans([f(r) for r in allrows], CODES, random.Random(500 + seed))
        books[arm] = book
        code = lambda r, f=f, book=book: min(range(CODES), key=lambda c: d2(f(r), book[c]))
        pol[arm] = table([(r[0], code(r)) for r in allrows])
    raw_A = table([(r[0], r[1]) for r in vids["A"]])
    out = {}
    for emb in ("A", "B"):
        lab = random.Random(700 + seed).sample(vids[emb], DEC_LABELS)
        dec = {}
        for arm, f in feats.items():
            book = books[arm]
            xs = [book[min(range(CODES), key=lambda c: d2(f(r), book[c]))] for r in lab]
            dec[arm] = lstsq(xs, [r[1] for r in lab])
        small = table([(r[0], r[1]) for r in lab])
        acts = {
            "A_raw_bc_A": lambda c: lookup(raw_A, c, mean_vec),
            "B_latent_lang": lambda c: tuple(sum(w * x for w, x in zip(row, books["B_latent_lang"][lookup(pol["B_latent_lang"], c, majority)])) for row in dec["B_latent_lang"]),
            "C_latent_no_lang": lambda c: tuple(sum(w * x for w, x in zip(row, books["C_latent_no_lang"][lookup(pol["C_latent_no_lang"], c, majority)])) for row in dec["C_latent_no_lang"]),
            "D_raw_bc_small": lambda c: lookup(small, c, mean_vec),
        }
```

## X-VLA: a soft prompt for each embodiment

UniVLA above shares a **latent action** space and decodes it per embodiment. X-VLA keeps ordinary actions and instead gives each data source a small set of **learnable embeddings**, a soft prompt, that tells one shared backbone which hardware it is driving. This section is **not** OXE-AugE's appearance augmentation and **not** UniVLA's latent actions.

<Resource id="xvla-paper" />

**Source actually opened (arXiv abstract page + PDF body read with pdftotext; numbers 以论文为准).**

- **X-VLA (Zheng, Li et al., Tsinghua AIR / Shanghai AI Lab / Peking University, arXiv 2510.10274):** the paper compares four ways to handle heterogeneous robot data under one aligned training recipe: (a) domain-specific action projection heads at the output, (b) HPT-style domain-specific projections of the inputs, (c) language prompts that describe the hardware, and (d) **soft prompts**, per-source learnable parameters `p_i ≈ Φ(h_i)` that start random, are trained end to end and are injected early in action generation. It reports that soft prompts train most stably (Fig. 4) and, in its ablation path (Table 1), lower the pretraining validation error from 0.053 to 0.041 and raise adaptation accuracy on Simpler-WidowX from 64.6 to 73.8.
- **Two phases.** Phase I pretrains X-VLA-0.9B, a flow-matching model built from soft-prompted Transformer encoders, on 290K episodes from Droid, RoboMind and Agibot (seven hardware setups, five robot types). Phase II adapts to a new robot in two steps: a **new** prompt is first warmed up with the pretrained backbone **frozen**, then backbone and prompt are trained jointly.
- **Reported numbers (以论文为准):** tuning only 9M parameters (about 1 %) reaches 93 % on LIBERO and 54 % on Simpler-WidowX, comparable to the 3B π0. A t-SNE plot shows the learned prompts clustering by hardware, with the two Franka setups that differ only in main camera view mixed together; frozen pretrained UR5 prompts help early adaptation to the unseen WidowX, but the domain gap limits the final result. Training X-VLA on GPUs: **本页未运行**.

### Shared bilinear stand-in, three regimes

`xvla_soft_prompt_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** task features `x ~ N(0, I6)`; each embodiment e has a hidden hardware vector `h_e` with R factors, and its demo action is `a = (Σⱼ h_e[j] T_j) x` plus noise (sd 0.10), where `T_1 … T_R` are fixed 2 × 6 maps. So an embodiment changes **how** the task features map to actions. Cameras, action-space layouts, flow matching and training stability are **not** modelled.

| Arm | How the embodiment enters |
|---|---|
| A_pooled | not at all: one linear map for every embodiment |
| B_hard_id | the one-hot ID is the prompt, fixed, one slot per embodiment; in this stand-in that is a separate linear head per embodiment, nothing shared |
| C_soft_prompt | a learned prompt `p_e` of length K = 2 per embodiment and a shared backbone, `a = (Σⱼ p_e[j] B_j) x`, fitted jointly by alternating least squares (60 sweeps) |

**Adaptation to a held-out embodiment** (a Phase II stand-in) gives every arm the same 4 demos: A fine-tunes its pooled map (ridge toward it, 12 numbers), B fits a fresh head (ridge toward zero, 12 numbers), and C freezes the backbone and fits only a new prompt (2 numbers). Only the paper's first step, the prompt warm-up, is modelled; the joint fine-tune is not.

**Common random numbers:** per (regime, seed), the hardware vectors, the maps `T_j`, the training demos and their noise, the 4 adaptation demos and the 100 test inputs are drawn once and fed to all three arms; C's prompt initialisation comes from its own fixed seed. Five seeds; every entry is mean±sd over seeds. Metrics: action RMSE against the noiseless action, averaged over the seen embodiments (**seen_rmse**) and on the held-out one after adaptation (**new_emb_rmse**). Both are **blind** to cameras, compute, training stability and closed-loop success.

**How pairs are labelled** (computed from unrounded per-seed values): **tie** only if the two arms are identical on every seed; **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** if the mean paired difference is no larger than its sd. Otherwise the gap is **consistent**, which means `|paired mean| > sd`; it is called **small but consistent gap, not a tie** if it is under 20 % of the smaller arm's mean, and **clear gap** otherwise. The 20 % rule only decides whether a consistent gap is small; it never decides consistency. "Consistent" means only `|paired mean| > sd`; it does **not** mean every seed agrees in sign. So every consistent or clear print line also carries a sign count: **same sign on all 5 seeds** is the stronger claim, and **k of 5 seeds has / have the opposite sign** marks a gap that some seeds reverse.

Regimes: **single_embodiment** (one embodiment, R = 2, 24 demos); **shared_low_rank** (six embodiments whose hardware lies on R = 2 shared factors, only 8 demos each: the paper's premise); **unrelated_full_rank** (six embodiments with one private factor each, R = 6, 30 demos each: the premise fails).

```python
"""Synthetic X-VLA-style embodiment soft prompts. Stdlib only.

Model (stated in the lesson prose too): a linear / bilinear stand-in, not the
0.9B flow-matching Transformer.
  task features   x ~ N(0, I_D), D = 6        (what the shared backbone sees)
  embodiment e    hidden hardware vector h_e in R^R
  demo action     a = (sum_j h_e[j] T_j) x + N(0, SIGMA^2 I_2)
  T_1..T_R are fixed 2 x D maps; R is the true number of hardware factors.
Arms, all fitted on the same demos:
  A_pooled       one linear map W x for every embodiment (no embodiment input)
  B_hard_id      the one-hot ID is the prompt (fixed, length = number of
                 embodiments): in this stand-in that is one separate linear head
                 per embodiment, nothing shared between embodiments
  C_soft_prompt  a = (sum_j p_e[j] B_j) x with a learned prompt p_e in R^K, K = 2,
                 per embodiment and a shared backbone B_1..B_K, fitted jointly by
                 alternating least squares (ALS_ITERS sweeps)
Adaptation to a held-out embodiment with N_NEW demos (Phase II stand-in):
  A_pooled       ridge fine-tune of W toward the pooled W        (2 D numbers)
  B_hard_id      a fresh head, ridge toward zero                 (2 D numbers)
  C_soft_prompt  backbone frozen, only a new prompt p_new fitted (K numbers);
                 the paper's second step (joint fine-tune) is not modelled.
Common random numbers: per (regime, seed) one set of hardware vectors, maps T_j,
train demos, demo noise, adaptation demos and test inputs is drawn once and fed
to all three arms; C's prompt initialisation comes from its own fixed seed.
Metrics per seed: action RMSE against the noiseless action on N_TEST test inputs
per seen embodiment, and on the held-out embodiment after adaptation. They are
blind to camera / action-space differences, to compute and to training stability.
No presumed winner. Benchmark numbers 以论文为准; GPU X-VLA run 本页未运行.
"""
import math
import random

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
D, K, SIGMA, LAM, ALS_ITERS, N_TEST, N_NEW = 6, 2, 0.10, 1e-3, 60, 100, 4
# name, number of seen embodiments, true hardware factors R, demos per embodiment
REGIMES = (("single_embodiment", 1, 2, 24), ("shared_low_rank", 6, 2, 8), ("unrelated_full_rank", 6, 6, 30))
ARMS = ("A_pooled", "B_hard_id", "C_soft_prompt")


def solve(a, b):
    n = len(b)
    m = [row[:] + [b[i]] for i, row in enumerate(a)]
    for c in range(n):
        p = max(range(c, n), key=lambda r: abs(m[r][c]))
        m[c], m[p] = m[p], m[c]
        for r in range(n):
            if r != c:
                f = m[r][c] / m[c][c]
                for j in range(c, n + 1):
                    m[r][j] -= f * m[c][j]
    return [m[i][n] / m[i][i] for i in range(n)]


def ridge(rows, ys, prior=None, lam=LAM):
    """argmin sum (y - w.r)^2 + lam * n * |w - prior|^2."""
    d, n = len(rows[0]), len(rows)
    prior = prior or [0.0] * d
    a = [[sum(r[i] * r[j] for r in rows) + (lam * n if i == j else 0.0) for j in range(d)] for i in range(d)]
    b = [sum(r[i] * y for r, y in zip(rows, ys)) + lam * n * prior[i] for i in range(d)]
    return solve(a, b)


def apply(w, x):
    return [sum(w[o][i] * x[i] for i in range(D)) for o in range(2)]


def rmse(w, xs, truth):
    err = [(p - t) ** 2 for x, tr in zip(xs, truth) for p, t in zip(apply(w, x), tr)]
    return math.sqrt(sum(err) / len(err))


def shared_draws(seed, n_emb, rank, n_demo):
    """Common random numbers: one draw per (regime, seed), shared by every arm."""
    rng = random.Random(1000 * seed + 10 * n_emb + rank)
    t = [[[rng.gauss(0, 1 / math.sqrt(D)) for _ in range(D)] for _ in range(2)] for _ in range(rank)]
    if rank == n_emb:  # unrelated: one private hardware factor per embodiment
        hs = [[1.0 if j == e else 0.0 for j in range(rank)] for e in range(n_emb)]
    else:
        hs = [[rng.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(rank)] for _ in range(n_emb)]
    h_new = [rng.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(rank)]

    def true_map(h):
        return [[sum(h[j] * t[j][o][i] for j in range(rank)) for i in range(D)] for o in range(2)]

    def demos(w, n):
        xs = [[rng.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(D)] for _ in range(n)]
        return xs, [[v + rng.gauss(0, SIGMA) for v in apply(w, x)] for x in xs]

    maps = [true_map(h) for h in hs]
    train = [demos(w, n_demo) for w in maps]
    new_map = true_map(h_new)
    adapt = demos(new_map, N_NEW)
    test_x = [[rng.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(D)] for _ in range(N_TEST)]
    return maps, train, new_map, adapt, test_x


def fit_pooled(train):
    xs = [x for d in train for x in d[0]]
    ys = [y for d in train for y in d[1]]
    return [ridge(xs, [y[o] for y in ys]) for o in range(2)]


def fit_head(xs, ys, prior=None):
    return [ridge(xs, [y[o] for y in ys], prior[o] if prior else None) for o in range(2)]


def prompt_map(p, b):
    return [[sum(p[j] * b[j][o][i] for j in range(K)) for i in range(D)] for o in range(2)]


def fit_prompt(xs, ys, b):
    rows, tgt = [], []
    for x, y in zip(xs, ys):
        feats = [apply(b[j], x) for j in range(K)]
        for o in range(2):
            rows.append([feats[j][o] for j in range(K)])
            tgt.append(y[o])
    return ridge(rows, tgt)


def fit_soft(train, seed):
    rng = random.Random(7919 + seed)  # C's own prompt initialisation
    prompts = [[rng.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(K)] for _ in train]
    b = None
    for _ in range(ALS_ITERS):
        b = [[[0.0] * D for _ in range(2)] for _ in range(K)]
        for o in range(2):
            rows = [[p[j] * xi for j in range(K) for xi in x] for p, d in zip(prompts, train) for x in d[0]]
            ys = [y[o] for d in train for y in d[1]]
            w = ridge(rows, ys)
            for j in range(K):
                b[j][o] = w[j * D:(j + 1) * D]
        prompts = [fit_prompt(d[0], d[1], b) for d in train]
    return prompts, b


def run(seed, n_emb, rank, n_demo):
    maps, train, new_map, adapt, test_x = shared_draws(seed, n_emb, rank, n_demo)
    truth = [[apply(w, x) for x in test_x] for w in maps]
    truth_new = [apply(new_map, x) for x in test_x]
    pooled = fit_pooled(train)
    heads = [fit_head(d[0], d[1]) for d in train]
    prompts, b = fit_soft(train, seed)
    seen = {
        "A_pooled": sum(rmse(pooled, test_x, tr) for tr in truth) / n_emb,
        "B_hard_id": sum(rmse(h, test_x, tr) for h, tr in zip(heads, truth)) / n_emb,
        "C_soft_prompt": sum(rmse(prompt_map(p, b), test_x, tr) for p, tr in zip(prompts, truth)) / n_emb,
    }
    new = {
        "A_pooled": rmse(fit_head(adapt[0], adapt[1], pooled), test_x, truth_new),
        "B_hard_id": rmse(fit_head(adapt[0], adapt[1]), test_x, truth_new),
        "C_soft_prompt": rmse(prompt_map(fit_prompt(adapt[0], adapt[1], b), b), test_x, truth_new),
    }
    return seen, new


def ms(v):
    m = sum(v) / len(v)
    return m, math.sqrt(sum((x - m) ** 2 for x in v) / (len(v) - 1))


def fmt(x, nd=4):
    if x != 0 and abs(x) < 0.5 * 10 ** -nd:
        return f"{x:.2e}"
    return f"{x:.{nd}f}"


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    """How many seeds disagree in sign with the paired mean (zeros listed apart)."""
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def label(diffs, mean_a, mean_b):
    """tie: identical on every seed; near-tie: |paired mean| <= paired sd;
    otherwise consistent (|mean| > sd), called small if |mean| < 20 % of the
    smaller arm mean. The 20 % rule only decides "small". "Consistent" means
    only |mean| > sd; the appended sign count (seeds whose paired difference
    has the opposite sign to the mean) separates sign-consistent gaps."""
    if all(d == 0 for d in diffs):
        return "tie: identical on every seed"
    m, sd = ms(diffs)
    if abs(m) <= sd:
        return f"near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (indistinguishable from 0 within +-{sd:.4f})"
    base = "small but consistent gap, not a tie" if abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_a), abs(mean_b)) else "clear gap, not a tie"
    return base + "; " + sign_note(diffs, m)


def main():
    print(f"seeds={len(SEEDS)} D={D} K={K} sigma={SIGMA} lam={LAM} als_iters={ALS_ITERS} n_test={N_TEST} n_new={N_NEW}")
    print(f"numbers tuned to adapt to a new embodiment: A_pooled {2 * D}, B_hard_id {2 * D}, C_soft_prompt {K}")
    print("regime arm seen_rmse new_emb_rmse  (mean+-sd over seeds)")
    for name, n_emb, rank, n_demo in REGIMES:
        res = [run(seed, n_emb, rank, n_demo) for seed in SEEDS]
        for a in ARMS:
            s, n = ms([r[0][a] for r in res]), ms([r[1][a] for r in res])
            print(f"{name} E={n_emb} R={rank} demos={n_demo} {a} {s[0]:.4f}+-{s[1]:.4f} {n[0]:.4f}+-{n[1]:.4f}")
        for x, y in (("C_soft_prompt", "B_hard_id"), ("C_soft_prompt", "A_pooled"), ("B_hard_id", "A_pooled")):
            for i, met in ((0, "seen_rmse"), (1, "new_emb_rmse")):
                d = [r[i][x] - r[i][y] for r in res]
                m, sd = ms(d)
                print(f"{name} paired {x[0]}-{y[0]} {met} {fmt(m)}+-{sd:.4f}: {label(d, ms([r[i][x] for r in res])[0], ms([r[i][y] for r in res])[0])}")
    print("No winner declared: K = 2 prompts help only when embodiments share a few hardware factors")
    print("Benchmark success rates 以论文为准; GPU X-VLA run 本页未运行")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

- **single_embodiment:** on seen data A and B are a **tie: identical on every seed** (0.0651±0.0179): with one embodiment, the one-hot head is the pooled map. C gives 0.0643±0.0180, and paired C−B −0.0007±0.0009 is a **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise**; the bilinear fit is only regularised differently. On the held-out embodiment nothing has taught the backbone how hardware varies, so **C loses to B**: 0.8406±0.5117 against the fresh head's 0.5999±0.3989 (paired C−B 0.2406±0.2119). That counts as a clear gap because it exceeds 20 % of B's mean, but `|paired mean|` exceeds the sd by only about 0.03 and **1 of 5 seeds has the opposite sign** (C is better on that seed), so it is weakly consistent. A's fine-tuned pooled map is worst at 1.0589±0.3141 (paired B−A −0.4590±0.3328, clear gap): pulling toward another robot's map is a bad prior.
- **shared_low_rank:** C wins both metrics. Seen: 0.0690±0.0118 against B 0.1812±0.0351 and A 1.2427±0.1303 (paired C−B −0.1122±0.0401, clear gap). B against A on the new embodiment (paired −0.1169±0.1065) is labelled clear because it exceeds 20 % of B's mean, though `|paired mean|` exceeds the sd by only about 0.01 (its sign is the same on all 5 seeds).
