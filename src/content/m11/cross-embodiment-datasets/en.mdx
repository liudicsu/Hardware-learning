## Why embodiment pools skew

Open X-Embodiment (OXE) pools many robot datasets into one ecosystem so a single policy can see many arms and grippers. The project page reports **1M+** real trajectories, **22** embodiments and about **60** datasets. Pooling alone does not make the mixture balanced: OXE-AugE (arXiv 2512.13100) notes that in the original OXE real data the **top-4** embodiments account for **more than 85 %** of trajectories. A fine-tune that ignores that skew mostly learns the majority arms.

## OXE-AugE in one paragraph (take the paper as source of truth)

OXE-AugE applies robot-appearance augmentation on **16** common subsets and reports about **4.4M** trajectories — more than **3×** the original OXE pool. ### Staged augmentation and what it does not transfer
We opened [OXE-AugE HTML §4.2 and Appendix A.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13100v1); take the paper as source of truth. Read the pipeline as four checkpoints, not "recolour an arm and declare transfer":

| Stage | Input → output | Audit / failure case |
|---|---|---|
| Mask fusion | Learned SAM2 mask + simulated robot mask → aligned, pruned, smoothed mask | Inspect gripper boundaries and missing background; a wrong mask erases objects. |
| Inpaint | Masked source video → reconstructed background (E2FGVI) | Check temporal flicker and hallucinated object edges. |
| Base-position tune | Source end-effector path + target kinematics → feasible base translation | Replay tracking error controls accept/reject; discard unreachable paths rather than inventing actions. |
| Replay/composite | Target-robot replay/render + reconstructed scene → augmented video | Inspect robot/object occlusion and pose/action alignment. |

The stage names are documentation constants in the CPU toy, **not implementations** of SAM2, inpainting, IK or rendering. Appearance augmentation preserves task/scene intent, not contact dynamics; differing grippers may need different strategies. Keep source/augmented/held-out robot identities explicit and split original trajectories before augmentation to avoid source-derived train/test leakage. This is complementary to 8.1's single-arm audit, not a replacement.

### A.5: partial score is not binary success

| Highest milestone reached | Score |
|---|---:|
| Moves toward the object and attempts a grasp | 0.3 |
| Grasps the object | 0.5 |
| Carries it toward the correct destination | 0.8 |
| Places it at the target | 1.0 |

Use one milestone score per trial, not a sum of stages. A.5 evaluates **10 trials × 4 tasks = 40** per policy per embodiment and reports **mean ± standard error of scores**. This can differ from the fraction of fully completed placements. Our synthetic no-milestone outcome is 0 by explicit toy convention; A.5 does not explicitly define that case. The toy computes SE as sample standard deviation / sqrt(40), assuming independent trials; shared task conditions or correlations need more careful uncertainty treatment. It does not recreate the paper's outcomes or error bars.

The abstract and §6.2 call the **24–45 %** improvement "success"; A.5's scored mean is **not binary success rate**. The denominator / absolute-vs-relative interpretation of that lift is **uncertain here**: do not silently turn it into percentage points or recompute a relative lift without specifying baseline and aggregation. Figures and paper definitions remain authoritative.

## UniVLA: task-centric latent actions, decoded per embodiment

The skew above is about **how many** trajectories each arm contributes. This section asks something different: how can one policy learn from video of **many** arms when only a few steps of the target arm carry motor labels? It is not another OXE-AugE count or score toy. It is also not the LAPA / IDM pseudo-label curve in [9.9](/en/month/month-9/world-models-overview), which labels generated video for one robot. Here the question is cross-embodiment transfer through a shared latent action space.

<Resource id="univla-paper" />

**What the paper does (opened at arXiv 2505.06111; numbers 以论文为准).** Other projects also use the name UniVLA; this section means only the paper by Bu et al. (arXiv 2505.06111). UniVLA (Bu et al., RSS 2025) learns latent actions from pairs of video frames in DINOv2 feature space, with a VQ-VAE codebook. Training has two stages:

- **Stage 1:** the instruction (T5 embeddings) is fed to the latent model, so its codes absorb the **task-irrelevant** dynamics, such as other agents or camera shake.
- **Stage 2:** that codebook is reused and a new set of **task-centric** latent actions is learned.

The policy is Prismatic-7B, with the vocabulary extended by |C| action tokens and N = 4 tokens per step (|C| = 16, so 16⁴ choices instead of OpenVLA's 256⁷). Small per-embodiment decoder heads (10.8M parameters) turn latents into robot actions. The paper notes that LAPA-style latents encode every visual change, and that training on task-irrelevant latents gives near-zero success on LIBERO-Long. It reports 960 A100-hours of pretraining against 21,500 for OpenVLA, and gains over OpenVLA of 18.5 % on LIBERO, 29.6 % in navigation and 36.7 % on real robots. None of this was reproduced here: the 7B pretraining, the GPUs and the robots are **本页未运行**.

### Fair setup

`univla_latent_action_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. An end-effector must reach the goal named by the instruction ("left" or "right") and go around a round obstacle; touching it is a failure. A second blob, the **wanderer**, moves on its own. Two embodiments see the same scenes, but **B** maps a motor command differently: its axes are swapped, one is flipped and the gains differ. Video episodes, starts, wanderer paths and evaluation starts are shared per seed, over five seeds.

| Arm | Trained on | Output |
|---|---|---|
| A_raw_bc_A | A's labelled motor commands only | motor command, used as-is on A and B |
| B_latent_lang | latent codes of the **EE's** frame change, from unlabelled video of A and B | code → per-embodiment linear decoder |
| C_latent_no_lang | latent codes of the **whole** frame change (EE and wanderer), same video | code → per-embodiment linear decoder |
| D_raw_bc_small | only the 20 labelled steps that the decoders get | motor command |

**Equal budgets.** B and C use the same 12-entry codebook, 10 Lloyd iterations, video, tabular policy grid and 20 labelled decoder steps per embodiment. The decoder input is the code's centroid: 2 numbers for B and 4 for C (labelled). Language is modelled as a known mask that says which blob is the actor. Real UniVLA learns that split from DINOv2 features in two stages, which is **not** modelled here. Metrics are success and collision rates on each embodiment over 40 episodes, mean±sd over five seeds. There are two scenarios: **wanderer** (σ 0.06 per step) and **still_scene** (the wanderer never moves).

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""univla_latent_action_toy.py: task-centric latent actions vs raw motor BC (stdlib).

Synthetic 2-D world in [0, 1]^2: an end-effector (EE) must reach the goal named
by the instruction ("left" or "right") and go around a round obstacle in the
middle; touching it ends the episode as a failure. A second blob (another agent,
the task-irrelevant dynamics) wanders on its own. Frames show both blobs.

Two embodiments see the SAME scenes but map a motor command m to an EE step
differently: A: step = m; B: step = (-0.6 * m_y, 1.4 * m_x) (axes swapped, one
flipped, different gains). Shared per seed: video episodes, starts, wanderer
paths and evaluation starts (common random numbers). Arms:
  A_raw_bc_A         tabular BC from EE cell + instruction to motor, trained on
                     A's labelled episodes only
  B_latent_lang      latent action = codebook index of the EE's frame-to-frame
                     change (the instruction marks which blob is the actor; real
                     UniVLA learns this split from DINOv2 features in two stages,
                     not modelled); policy on unlabelled video of A AND B; a
                     per-embodiment linear decoder from the code's centroid
  C_latent_no_lang   the same, but the code covers the whole frame change (EE and
                     wanderer), as in latent models without language conditioning
  D_raw_bc_small     tabular BC trained only on the decoder's labelled steps
Equal budgets: the same codebook size, Lloyd iterations, video, grid and decoder
labels (DEC_LABELS labelled steps per embodiment) for B, C and D; decoder input
is 2 numbers for B and 4 for C (labelled). Not UniVLA, no VLM, no GPU.
usage: python3 univla_latent_action_toy.py
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
import statistics

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
STEP = 0.05
OBST, OBST_R = (0.5, 0.5), 0.17
GOALS = {"left": (0.12, 0.88), "right": (0.88, 0.88)}
GRID = 10
VIDEO_EPISODES = 80        # per embodiment
DEC_LABELS = 20            # labelled steps per embodiment for decoders (and D)
CODES = 12
LLOYD_ITERS = 10
T_MAX = 40
N_EVAL = 40
SCENARIOS = (("wanderer", 0.06), ("still_scene", 0.0))
ARMS = ("A_raw_bc_A", "B_latent_lang", "C_latent_no_lang", "D_raw_bc_small")


def to_step(emb, m):
    return (m[0], m[1]) if emb == "A" else (-0.6 * m[1], 1.4 * m[0])


def to_motor(emb, d):
    return (d[0], d[1]) if emb == "A" else (d[1] / 1.4, -d[0] / 0.6)


def blocked(a, b):
    """Does the segment a->b pass within OBST_R + 0.04 of the obstacle centre?"""
    vx, vy = b[0] - a[0], b[1] - a[1]
    t = max(0.0, min(1.0, ((OBST[0] - a[0]) * vx + (OBST[1] - a[1]) * vy) / (vx * vx + vy * vy + 1e-12)))
    return math.hypot(a[0] + t * vx - OBST[0], a[1] + t * vy - OBST[1]) < OBST_R + 0.04


def expert_step(ee, goal):
    """Straight to the goal, or first to a waypoint below and beside the obstacle."""
    tgt = goal
    if blocked(ee, goal):
        side = 1.0 if goal[0] > OBST[0] else -1.0
        tgt = (OBST[0] + side * 0.3, min(ee[1], 0.26))
        if math.hypot(tgt[0] - ee[0], tgt[1] - ee[1]) < 1e-9:
            tgt = goal
    dx, dy = tgt[0] - ee[0], tgt[1] - ee[1]
    dist = math.hypot(dx, dy)
    s = min(STEP, dist)
    return (s * dx / dist, s * dy / dist)


def clip(p):
    return (min(max(p[0], 0.0), 1.0), min(max(p[1], 0.0), 1.0))


def hit(p):
    return math.hypot(p[0] - OBST[0], p[1] - OBST[1]) < OBST_R


def start(rng):
    while True:
        p = (rng.uniform(0.05, 0.95), rng.uniform(0.05, 0.4))
        if not hit(p):
            return p


def cell(ee, task):
    return (min(GRID - 1, int(ee[0] * GRID)), min(GRID - 1, int(ee[1] * GRID)), task)


def wander(w, rng, sigma):
    return clip((w[0] + rng.gauss(0.0, sigma), w[1] + rng.gauss(0.0, sigma)))


def video(emb, rng, sigma):
    """Expert episodes: (cell, motor, ee_change, wanderer_change) per step."""
    rows = []
    for _ in range(VIDEO_EPISODES):
        task = rng.choice(("left", "right"))
        ee, w = start(rng), (rng.random(), rng.random())
        for _ in range(T_MAX):
            d = expert_step(ee, GOALS[task])
            m = to_motor(emb, d)
            m = (m[0] + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.002), m[1] + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.002))
            st = to_step(emb, m)
            nee = clip((ee[0] + st[0], ee[1] + st[1]))
            nw = wander(w, rng, sigma)
            rows.append((cell(ee, task), m, (nee[0] - ee[0], nee[1] - ee[1]), (nw[0] - w[0], nw[1] - w[1])))
            ee, w = nee, nw
            if math.hypot(ee[0] - GOALS[task][0], ee[1] - GOALS[task][1]) < 0.06:
                break
    return rows


def d2(a, b):
    return sum((x - y) ** 2 for x, y in zip(a, b))


def kmeans(data, k, rng):
    cent = [list(p) for p in rng.sample(data, k)]
    for _ in range(LLOYD_ITERS):
        sums, cnt = [[0.0] * len(data[0]) for _ in cent], [0] * k
        for p in data:
            i = min(range(k), key=lambda c: d2(p, cent[c]))
            cnt[i] += 1
            sums[i] = [s + v for s, v in zip(sums[i], p)]
        cent = [[s / cnt[i] for s in sums[i]] if cnt[i] else cent[i] for i in range(k)]
    return cent


def lstsq(xs, ys):
    """Least squares y = W x (no bias), solved with normal equations + tiny ridge."""
    n = len(xs[0])
    a = [[sum(x[i] * x[j] for x in xs) + (1e-6 if i == j else 0.0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
    W = []
    for k in range(len(ys[0])):
        b = [sum(x[i] * y[k] for x, y in zip(xs, ys)) for i in range(n)]
        m = [row[:] + [bb] for row, bb in zip(a, b)]
        for c in range(n):
            piv = max(range(c, n), key=lambda r: abs(m[r][c]))
            m[c], m[piv] = m[piv], m[c]
            for r in range(n):
                if r != c:
                    f = m[r][c] / m[c][c]
                    m[r] = [u - f * v for u, v in zip(m[r], m[c])]
        W.append([m[i][n] / m[i][i] for i in range(n)])
    return W


def table(pairs):
    """cell -> mean value (motor) or majority value (code)."""
    acc = {}
    for c, v in pairs:
        acc.setdefault(c, []).append(v)
    return acc


def lookup(tab, key, reduce):
    if key in tab:
        return reduce(tab[key])
    near = min((k for k in tab if k[2] == key[2]), key=lambda k: (k[0] - key[0]) ** 2 + (k[1] - key[1]) ** 2)
    return reduce(tab[near])


def mean_vec(vs):
    return tuple(sum(v[i] for v in vs) / len(vs) for i in range(len(vs[0])))


def majority(vs):
    return max(sorted(set(vs)), key=vs.count)


def rollout(emb, act, rng, sigma):
    task = rng.choice(("left", "right"))
    ee, w = start(rng), (rng.random(), rng.random())
    for t in range(T_MAX):
        m = act(cell(ee, task))
        s = to_step(emb, m)
        n = math.hypot(s[0], s[1])
        if n > 2 * STEP:                       # actuator saturation, same for every arm
            s = (s[0] * 2 * STEP / n, s[1] * 2 * STEP / n)
        ee = clip((ee[0] + s[0], ee[1] + s[1]))
        w = wander(w, rng, sigma)
        if hit(ee):
            return 0, 1
        if math.hypot(ee[0] - GOALS[task][0], ee[1] - GOALS[task][1]) < 0.06:
            return 1, 0
    return 0, 0


def run_seed(seed, sigma):
    vids = {e: video(e, random.Random(100 * seed + (1 if e == "A" else 2)), sigma) for e in ("A", "B")}
    feats = {"B_latent_lang": lambda r: list(r[2]), "C_latent_no_lang": lambda r: list(r[2]) + list(r[3])}
    pol, books = {}, {}
    for arm, f in feats.items():
        allrows = vids["A"] + vids["B"]
        book = kmeans([f(r) for r in allrows], CODES, random.Random(500 + seed))
        books[arm] = book
        code = lambda r, f=f, book=book: min(range(CODES), key=lambda c: d2(f(r), book[c]))
        pol[arm] = table([(r[0], code(r)) for r in allrows])
    raw_A = table([(r[0], r[1]) for r in vids["A"]])
    out = {}
    for emb in ("A", "B"):
        lab = random.Random(700 + seed).sample(vids[emb], DEC_LABELS)
        dec = {}
        for arm, f in feats.items():
            book = books[arm]
            xs = [book[min(range(CODES), key=lambda c: d2(f(r), book[c]))] for r in lab]
            dec[arm] = lstsq(xs, [r[1] for r in lab])
        small = table([(r[0], r[1]) for r in lab])
        acts = {
            "A_raw_bc_A": lambda c: lookup(raw_A, c, mean_vec),
            "B_latent_lang": lambda c: tuple(sum(w * x for w, x in zip(row, books["B_latent_lang"][lookup(pol["B_latent_lang"], c, majority)])) for row in dec["B_latent_lang"]),
            "C_latent_no_lang": lambda c: tuple(sum(w * x for w, x in zip(row, books["C_latent_no_lang"][lookup(pol["C_latent_no_lang"], c, majority)])) for row in dec["C_latent_no_lang"]),
            "D_raw_bc_small": lambda c: lookup(small, c, mean_vec),
        }
```

### Read the trade-off

**Raw BC from A** is the best arm on A (**0.890**) and scores **0.000** on B: the same motor numbers move B's end-effector sideways, and it hits the obstacle **0.340** of the time.

**The task-centric latent** reaches **0.840** on A and **0.865** on B. The policy learned from both arms' unlabelled video, and 20 labelled steps were enough to fit each linear decoder.

**Without the actor split**, the codebook spends its 12 entries on the wanderer's motion. The policy then cannot predict codes from the scene, so C falls to **0.125** on B. In still_scene the two latents are identical (**0.865** on B), so the bleed comes from the task-irrelevant motion, not from the latent idea itself.

**The 20 labels alone** (D) give **0.355** on B: enough to fit a small decoder, but not enough to cover the whole route.

**The toy does not pick a winner.** On its own embodiment, raw BC with plenty of labels is best. The latent route pays off only when the target arm has few labels and the codes ignore what the task does not control.
