## Reading a schematic: connections, not physical layout

A schematic describes who connects to whom, not where parts sit on the physical board. On a real PCB parts are packed tight and traces run across several layers, but the schematic only cares about one thing: the electrical connection. So don't read it as a spatial map ("input on the left, output on the right"); read it as a list of "which pin goes to which".

Learning the symbols is step one. You will meet these over and over:

| Symbol name | What it does | How it's labelled |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resistor | Limits current, divides voltage | R1, R2…; drawn as a zigzag or a box |
| Capacitor | Stores charge, smooths voltage | C1, C2…; a polarised electrolytic carries a + mark |
| Diode | Lets current flow one way | D1, D2…; the bar side is the cathode |
| LED | Emits light when current flows | Labelled D or LED, with two arrows pointing away |
| NPN transistor | A small current controls a big current | Q1…; the emitter's arrow points **out** of the symbol |
| Switch / button | Opens or closes a circuit | SW or S |
| Ground | The 0 V reference for everything | GND |
| Supply labels | Name the power nets | VCC, VDD, 5V, 3V3, VBAT |

Three reading rules; keep them handy and you avoid most misreads:

- **Junction dot**: a filled dot where wires cross means those wires are actually connected.
- **Crossing without a dot = not connected**: the wires merely cross; electrically they may live on different layers.
- **Same net label = connected**: two places marked `3V3` or `SDA` are one net even if no wire is drawn between them; big schematics stay tidy thanks to this.

Bench tip: do not try to read the whole sheet at once. Pick one net (say GND or a signal), trace every pin on it, understand that one fully, then move to the next. Read a schematic one net at a time.

## Capacitors: storing charge, resisting sudden change

A <Term id="capacitor">capacitor</Term> is two metal plates placed very close together; charge "sits" between them. Two behaviours are enough to carry with you:

- It **resists sudden voltage change**: to jump the voltage across a capacitor instantly you would need an infinite current. More current charges it faster, but "instant" is impossible.
- Hence the classic motto: **block DC, pass changes**. A steady DC eventually cannot get through, while fast changes pass. Run it once in the simulator to observe it directly.

How fast it charges or discharges is set by the <Term id="rc-time-constant">time constant (τ)</Term>, the product of the resistance and the capacitance:

<Formula label="RC time constant">τ = R × C</Formula>

Rules of thumb (charging from zero):

- After **1 τ** the capacitor reaches **≈ 63 %** of its target voltage;
- After **5 τ** it reaches **≈ 99 %** — engineering-wise, "charged".

Both numbers come from the charging curve V(t) = V_final × (1 − e^(−t/τ)). At t = τ the fraction is 1 − e^(−1) = 0.632; at t = 5τ it is 1 − e^(−5) = 0.993.

**Worked example**: the embedded Falstad RC example uses R = 180 Ω and C = 33 µF.

```text
τ = 180 Ω × 33 µF = 5940 µs ≈ 5.9 ms
```

So roughly 5.9 ms after charging starts it is at 63 %, and about 5 × 5.94 ≈ 29.7 ms gets it to 99 %.

<Falstad title="RC charge/discharge (official Falstad example)" circuit="capac" height={420} />

How to play with it:

1. A square-wave source repeatedly charges and discharges the capacitor. Watch the scope trace: the voltage climbs and decays.
2. Compare against the numbers above: each climb passes 63 % around 1 τ and 99 % around 5 τ — the curve is steep at first, then flattens as the voltage approaches its final value exponentially.
3. **Predict, then act**: double-click the capacitor and make it 10× bigger (330 µF). Write your prediction first, then run: the time constant grows 10× and charging visibly slows. A whole ×10 is chosen so the change is obvious, not a subtle shift.

## Decoupling capacitors: supplying transient current locally

Inside a digital IC, gates switch extremely fast, and each switching event draws a short current pulse from the supply (a current spike). Supply traces have resistance and inductance; a changing current produces an L·di/dt drop. If the pulse must come from a distant source, the current loop is large and its inductance is high, so the voltage at the chip pins dips and can cause glitches or resets.

The fix is **a 100 nF ceramic capacitor (<Term id="decoupling-capacitor">decoupling capacitor</Term>) directly across each IC's supply pin (VCC) and GND, as physically close to the chip as possible**. The capacitor is a local charge store: the supply charges it continuously, and during a switching event the pulse flows through the capacitor and ground in a short loop with low inductance, so the L·di/dt voltage dip stays small. Hobby boards and volume production alike are wired this way.

- **100 nF ceramic**: one per chip, hugging its supply pins. If the board has multiple supply domains (say 3.3 V and 5 V coexisting), each domain needs its own.
- **Bulk electrolytic (100–470 µF)**: placed near motor drivers and other loads that draw large transient currents, providing a larger charge reserve for the whole board.

A real capacitor is not ideal: in series with its capacitance it has a small resistance, the <Term id="esr">equivalent series resistance (ESR)</Term>. When the capacitor supplies a current pulse I, the ESR alone drops ΔV = I × ESR, before any charge has been used. Multilayer ceramic capacitors have a much lower ESR than aluminium electrolytics, which is one reason the 100 nF next to each chip is ceramic, while the electrolytic provides the bulk charge. Capacitor datasheets list ESR (or the dissipation factor from which it follows); for a motor driver, multiply it by the current step you expect, for example the stall current from the brownout section below.

A phrase worth knowing: 100 nF is often quoted as “0.1 µF”; when a schematic or parts list says “add a 0.1”, that is what it means.

## Diodes and LEDs: one-way conduction and current limiting

A <Term id="diode">diode</Term> is a PN junction: with forward bias the barrier lowers and current flows from the anode to the cathode; with reverse bias the barrier rises and only a small leakage current flows. The bar in the symbol is the cathode. Conduction begins after the <Term id="led-forward-voltage">forward voltage (Vf)</Term> is overcome:

- Ordinary silicon diode: **≈ 0.6–0.7 V**;
- An LED's Vf depends on colour: **red ≈ 1.8–2.2 V**, **green/blue/white ≈ 2.8–3.3 V**.

An LED is a current-driven device: push its voltage just a little above Vf and the current shoots up steeply — a few millivolts too much can destroy it. That is why **an LED must always have a series current-limiting resistor**, the number-one cause of beginner LED failures. The resistor follows straight from Ohm's law:

<Formula label="LED current-limiting resistor">R = (Vsupply − Vf) / I</Formula>

where Vsupply is the supply voltage, Vf the LED's forward voltage, I the target current.

**Worked example**: 5 V supply, red LED (Vf = 2 V), target 10 mA.

```text
R = (5 V − 2 V) / 10 mA = 3 V / 0.01 A = 300 Ω
```

300 Ω is not an E12 standard value (…270, 330, 390…), so pick **up** to the nearest: **330 Ω**. Choose the larger value: a larger resistor only dims the LED, while a smaller one over-currents it and shortens its life until it fails.

<Falstad title="LED current limiting: 5 V, 330 Ω, red LED" circuit="ledLimit" height={380} />

The ammeter in series reads about 9.8 mA. Falstad's default LED model drops about 1.78 V rather than the 2 V used in the worked example, so the current is a little higher than the 9.1 mA you get from (5 − 2) / 330. That difference is normal — real LEDs vary too, which is why we design with margin. Try it: double-click the resistor, change 330 Ω to 100 Ω, predict the new current before looking, and notice how quickly the current rises.

### Inductors: why switching off a coil produces a spike

An <Term id="inductor">inductor</Term> (a coil; every motor and relay winding is one) opposes changes in its current. Its voltage is

<Formula label="Inductor voltage">V = L × dI/dt</Formula>

L in henries (H), dI/dt in amperes per second. Opening a switch tries to make the current fall to zero almost instantly, so dI/dt is huge and so is V.

<Falstad title="Inductive kick (official Falstad example)" circuit="inductkick" height={400} />

The circuit file (`inductkick.txt`) contains a 5 V source, a 1 H inductor, a 100 Ω resistor and a switch with 0.5 nF across its contacts.

1. Close the switch. The current settles at 5 V / 100 Ω = 50 mA.
2. Open the switch and watch the voltage across it. The 50 mA can only flow into the 0.5 nF capacitor, so the voltage rises until the inductor's energy has moved into it. The theoretical peak is I × √(L/C) = 0.05 A × √(1 H / 0.5 nF) ≈ 2.24 kV.
3. Repeat with the second example below, the same circuit with a diode across the inductor. When the switch opens, the current circulates through the diode, and the switch sees only the 5 V supply plus one diode drop.

<Falstad title="Inductive kick with a flyback diode (official Falstad example)" circuit="inductkickBlock" height={400} />

This is what the flyback diode described next does for motors and relays.

One more protective use: the **<Term id="flyback-diode">flyback diode</Term>**. Motors and relays are inductive loads; the instant you switch them off, the inductor — which cannot change its current abruptly — produces a high-voltage spike (tens of volts are common) that can damage the driving transistor. Wire a diode anti-parallel across the load; when the switch opens, the inductor current continues through the diode and the spike is limited to the supply voltage plus one diode forward drop. Month 2 applies this to motors.

## The transistor as a switch: small current controls a large current

Why a transistor? A microcontroller GPIO pin can **safely source only about 10–20 mA** (exact figure: read the datasheet), and its logic level is often just 3.3 V (ESP32 and most modern chips; the classic Arduino Uno is 5 V). Motors, LED strips and relays want hundreds of milliamps or more; wired straight to a pin, the pin or the chip can be damaged. The idea: **the GPIO provides only a control signal; the transistor carries the load current**.

The most common arrangement is the NPN low-side switch: the load sits between the supply and the collector, the emitter goes to ground, and the GPIO feeds a small current into the base through a base resistor. Transistor on, load circuit complete.

Sizing the base resistor: say target collector current Ic = 20 mA (lighting a small LED strip), and your NPN's current gain <Term id="beta-hfe">β (hFE)</Term> ≈ 100.

```text
Minimum base current: Ib(min) = Ic / β = 20 mA / 100 = 0.2 mA
```

But a switch must be **fully on** (saturated) — half-on wastes heat. Practice is to overdrive the base 5–10×; taking ×10:

```text
Ib ≈ 10 × 0.2 mA = 2 mA
Rb = (Vgpio − Vbe) / Ib = (3.3 V − 0.7 V) / 2 mA ≈ 1.3 kΩ
```

1.3 kΩ isn't standard either, so go **down** to 1 kΩ: a bit more base current, deeper <Term id="transistor-saturation">saturation</Term>, safer margin. Direction matters: when sizing the base resistor, err small (more saturation), not large.

**The same switch from a 5 V Uno pin.**

```text
Ib ≈ 2 mA (same Ic = 20 mA, β = 100, ×10 overdrive)
Rb = (5 V − 0.7 V) / 2 mA = 2.15 kΩ
```

Going down to the next E12 value gives 1.8 kΩ, Ib = 4.3 V / 1.8 kΩ = 2.4 mA.

**Operating regions of an NPN**

| Region | Condition | Vce | Power in the transistor P = Vce × Ic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cut-off | Vbe below about 0.6 V, Ib ≈ 0 | close to the supply | ≈ 0 (Ic ≈ 0) |
| Active (linear) | Ic = β × Ib, less than the load needs | several volts | large: Vce × Ic |
| Saturation | Ib well above Ic / β | about 0.2 V | small: at 20 mA, 0.2 V × 20 mA = 4 mW |

A switch must sit in cut-off or saturation; step 3 of the practice circuit below, with a 100 kΩ base resistor, leaves it in the active region.

Circuit: on the left a 3.3 V source through a switch (playing the role of a GPIO output HIGH) and a 1 kΩ base resistor drive the NPN; the collector side is 5 V → 220 Ω → LED. Steps:

1. **Click the switch** — the GPIO "goes HIGH" — and watch the LED light up.
2. Note the currents the sim reports: the **base branch carries only about 2–3 mA while the collector branch carries well over 10 mA** — a small current controls a large one.
3. **Break it**: double-click the 1 kΩ base resistor and set it to 100 kΩ. The LED dims or goes out. Why? ## Pull-up and pull-down resistors: holding a defined level

An input pin with nothing attached is floating: its input impedance is very high and its leakage current is typically below 1 µA, so a small charge coupled from nearby signals or from a hand can move its voltage across the VIH/VIL thresholds and the reading flips at random — high, low, high — and the program behaves erratically. This is a common cause of a circuit that "sometimes works and sometimes recovers when touched".

The cure: with a large resistor (10 kΩ is the habit) hold the input at a known level —

- **Pull-up**: 10 kΩ from the pin to VCC; it reads HIGH at rest. Add a button from the pin to GND: **pressed**, the pin is dragged straight to GND and reads LOW; released, the <Term id="pull-up-pull-down">pull-up</Term> restores HIGH. This "reads low when pressed" wiring is called **active low**.
- **Pull-down**: the complementary arrangement. 10 kΩ from the pin to GND; it reads LOW at rest; a button from the pin to VCC makes it read HIGH when pressed.

Why 10 kΩ? The resistor only needs to hold the level, not supply current. Too large and noise can push the pin around; too small and pressing the button wastes current for nothing. 10 kΩ is the decades-old compromise.

A digital input does not read every voltage as a clean 0 or 1. The datasheet gives two <Term id="logic-thresholds">logic thresholds</Term>: VIH, the lowest voltage guaranteed to read HIGH, and VIL, the highest voltage guaranteed to read LOW. Between them the result is undefined, and a floating pin can sit there.

```text
ATmega328P (Uno), VCC = 5 V:   VIH = 0.6 × VCC = 3.0 V   VIL = 0.3 × VCC = 1.5 V
ESP32-S3, VDD = 3.3 V:          VIH = 0.75 × VDD = 2.475 V   VIL = 0.25 × VDD = 0.825 V
ESP32-S3 output HIGH:            VOH ≥ 0.8 × VDD = 2.64 V (datasheet minimum, high-impedance load)
ESP32-S3 HIGH into an Uno input: 2.64 V < VIH = 3.0 V, so the Uno is not guaranteed to read HIGH
```

An unloaded ESP32-S3 output is typically close to 3.3 V, but the datasheet guarantees only 2.64 V, and the HIGH level falls further as the pin sources current. Under load the HIGH may therefore not register. Use a 3.3 V → 5 V level shifter for every ESP32-S3 signal into an Uno input. The other direction needs one too: a 5 V Uno output exceeds the ESP32-S3's maximum input voltage of VDD + 0.3 = 3.6 V.

The pull-up's other job is to keep the pin far from that band. On the Uno at 5 V, with the ATmega328P's maximum input leakage of 1 µA, a 10 kΩ pull-up drops only 1 µA × 10 kΩ = 10 mV. The pin sits at 4.99 V, 1.99 V above VIH, and pressing the button costs 5 V / 10 kΩ = 0.5 mA. A 1 MΩ pull-up drops up to 1 µA × 1 MΩ = 1 V, so the pin sits at 4.0 V. It still reads HIGH, but the margin to VIH halves to 1.0 V, and with 100 times the source resistance, the same coupled noise current shifts the pin 100 times further (ΔV = I × R). 10 kΩ keeps the pin within 10 mV of the rail for 0.5 mA while pressed.

Sources: ATmega48A/PA/88A/PA/168A/PA/328/P datasheet (DS40002061B), Table 30-1, Common DC characteristics; ESP32-S3 datasheet v2.2, Table 5-4, DC characteristics (3.3 V, 25 °C).

Many MCUs have configurable internal pull-ups, enabled with one setting (`INPUT_PULLUP` on Arduino), which removes the external resistor — but this also means "pressed reads LOW".

## Batteries and LiPo safety

The lithium-polymer (LiPo) packs powering DIY robots come with parameters and rules you simply memorise:

- **One cell is 3.7 V nominal, 4.2 V full**; discharge **no lower than ≈ 3.0–3.3 V per cell**. Over-discharge damages the cell permanently — and a damaged cell is also a hazardous one to recharge.
- **"S" is the count of cells in series**: 2S = 2 × 3.7 V = 7.4 V nominal, 3S = 11.1 V. Check the S-count first when matching a battery to a design.
- **Capacity in mAh**: 2200 mAh means it can theoretically deliver 2200 mA for an hour (a manufacturer's estimate; treat it as such).
- **C rating × capacity = maximum continuous current**. Example: a 2200 mAh, 25C pack → 25 × 2200 mAh = 55 A.

<Quiz id="q6" question="A 1500 mAh, 20C LiPo: what is the maximum continuous current in A?" answer={30} unit="A" explanation="20 × 1500 mAh = 30 000 mA = 30 A." />

<Callout type="danger" title="LiPo safety rules">
**Never** charge unattended; only use a balance charger and charge inside a **LiPo-safe bag**. Overcharge, short circuit, puncture or mechanical damage can start a fire that is difficult to extinguish. Retire any **puffy** pack immediately — do not repair it, sell it, or continue using it. For long storage, hold each cell at **≈ 3.8 V** (many chargers have a one-button storage mode). These rules address the thermal-runaway risk of LiPo cells.
</Callout>

## Why a stalling motor resets your microcontroller (brownout)

A typical sequence: the robot catches a wall corner, the motor stalls, and in that instant the MCU resets and the program starts over. This follows directly from Ohm's law and the internal resistance of the supply.

**Step 1: how big is the stall current?** While spinning, a motor generates <Term id="back-emf">back-EMF</Term> that cancels part of the applied voltage, so current stays moderate. Stall the rotor completely and back-EMF vanishes; the motor becomes a small resistance and draws its **<Term id="stall-current">stall current</Term>, often 5–10× the running current**. The no-load (free-run) current is what the motor draws when its shaft turns freely with nothing attached. The course's Romi gearmotor, for example, draws 130 mA with no load at 4.5 V, and Pololu gives its stall current as 1.25 A, a value Pololu marks as an extrapolation; that is 9.6 times the no-load current.

**Step 2: how does that collapse the rail?** The battery has internal resistance; wires and connectors add more; all of it sits **in series** with the motor in the same loop. By Ohm's law, V = I × R — whatever voltage those resistances drop is voltage your devices never receive.

**Worked example**: 2 A stall current, 0.5 Ω total series resistance (battery + wires + connectors).

```text
Drop = I × R = 2 A × 0.5 Ω = 1 V
```

A nominal 5 V rail sags to 4 V in that instant. If the 3.3 V regulator's input is already near its drop-out, its output falls right behind.

**Step 3: the MCU defensively resets.** MCUs contain a **brown-out detector**: when VCC dips below a safe threshold it forces a reset rather than letting the CPU execute incorrectly. So the "reboot" you saw was the protection working. Worse is a case where it does not reset but executes incorrectly: drifting sensor readings, garbled serial, random freezes.

**Fixes (roughly in order of effectiveness):**

1. Give the logic its **own regulated supply**: the MCU runs off its own <Term id="ldo">LDO</Term> or buck, not the motor's noisy supply rail.
2. **Bulk electrolytic near the motor driver** (say 220–470 µF): absorbs the surge locally and buys the regulator time.
3. **Thicker, shorter power wires**: shrink that series resistance R directly.
4. **Common ground**: digital ground and power ground must meet at one point — but don't route motor current through the logic ground path.
5. **Soft-start / current limiting**: ramp <Term id="pwm">PWM</Term> from zero or cap current in software, avoiding the shock at its source.

## Common mistakes and a before-you-move-on checklist

Common mistakes, in no particular order:

- No current-limiting resistor on an LED, or one chosen too small — it overheats and fails quickly.
- Electrolytic reversed — the case can swell and rupture, which is hazardous.
- Decoupling cap placed centimetres away — the trace inductance of a few centimetres already produces a significant L·di/dt drop during transients.
- Base resistor too large; the transistor never saturates and the load voltage never arrives.
- Button pin left floating; the input level changes at random and program behaviour becomes erratic.
- 5 V signals into 3.3 V-only inputs — check the absolute maximum ratings first; "directly compatible" is not universal.
- Digital and power grounds never tied — the circuit appears connected but has no electrical path.
- Exceeding the C rating, continuous over-discharge — the pack keeps heating up, causing overheating and permanent damage.

Before moving on, you should be able to answer "yes" to all of these:

- I can point at each symbol — resistor, capacitor, diode, LED, NPN transistor, GND — and state its job.
- I can explain junction dots vs dot-less crossings, and why two identical net labels are the same wire.
- I have computed τ = R × C by hand and know the 63 % and 99 % milestones.
- I can say why the decoupling cap must hug the chip, and what 100 nF vs bulk electrolytic each handle.
- I have sized an LED resistor and know to round up to the standard value.
- I clicked the Falstad transistor switch, and I can explain why the LED dimmed with Rb = 100 kΩ.
- I know why a floating input is dangerous and what `INPUT_PULLUP` does.
- I can recite the LiPo voltage windows, the meaning of S and C, and the safety rules.
- I can explain, with V = I × R, why a stall can reset the MCU.

## Supply dip: series ESL + ESR + C and placement
The earlier RC charging and local-bypass advice remain. The corrected model is a **series ESL+ESR+C capacitor path**, not L·di/dt alone. Compare **equal total capacitance** while changing placement and capacitor values; distinguish impedance versus frequency from the transient minimum rail. This is not [11.1 round-trip IR/fuse sizing](/en/month/month-11/power-battery-emc) or an FCC/CE test.

<Resource id="ti-sn74hc14-decoupling" />

The opened real **SN74HC14 SCLS085L** Power Supply Recommendations recommends **0.1 µF** near each VCC and mentions parallel 1 µF. That is a device-specific reading pointer, not the source of the toy's parts. **All numerical model inputs are synthetic**: total C = **10 µF** for every bank, each ESR/ESL, placement loop L, pulse train and source impedance. No commercial component is characterized here.

### Equal C does not mean equal impedance

For N identical capacitors in parallel, `C_total=N C_part`, `ESR_eq=ESR_part/N`, `ESL_eq=ESL_part/N`; the **shared placement-loop L is added once, never divided by N**. The resulting branch is `Zcap(ω)=ESR_eq + j[ω(L_loop+ESL_eq) − 1/(ω C_total)]`. This aggregation assumes identical branches with negligible unequal connection/mutual inductance, not arbitrary mixed-value banks.

The mounted-path **self-resonant frequency (SRF)** is `1/[2π sqrt((L_loop+ESL_eq) C_total)]`, not the isolated component's datasheet SRF. Below SRF the imaginary part is negative (capacitive); at SRF reactances cancel but ESR remains; above SRF it becomes positive (inductive). At **10×SRF**, `X_L/|X_C| = 100`, so adding capacitance alone does not remove the high-frequency inductance. The extra **20 MHz** table separates X_loop, X_ESL, X_C and ESR and shows **loop inductance dominating** above all four SRFs.

| Bank | Parts (each C / ESR / ESL) | Shared loop L | Total C |
|---|---|---|---|
| far_bulk | 1 × (10 µF / 0.20 Ω / 5 nH) | 40 nH | 10 µF |
| local_single | 1 × (10 µF / 0.02 Ω / 1 nH) | 3 nH | 10 µF |
| local_split | 10 × (1 µF / 0.04 Ω / 0.8 nH) | same 3 nH | 10 µF |
| long_trace | same 10 × (1 µF / 0.04 Ω / 0.8 nH) | 100 nH | 10 µF |

**Placement-only control:** local_split versus long_trace has exactly the same parts and total C, changing only the common loop L. **Value/bank comparison:** local_single versus local_split keeps placement and total C fixed but changes individual C and the declared ESR/ESL; it does not prove “more parallel parts always win”. Far bulk also changes part parasitics, so that contrast is not placement-only.

The **intrinsic capacitor-bank SRFs**, before adding the shared loop, are **0.711763 / 1.591549 / 5.626977 / 5.626977 MHz**. They differ from the mounted-path SRFs below; **20 MHz exceeds both sets**, so the loop-dominance example is also above the capacitor's own SRF, not just a relabelled low-frequency mounting resonance.

### A circuit for the same pulse train

An ideal 3.3 V source feeds the pin through a shared synthetic **Rfeed = 0.5 Ω**; the series capacitor branch goes from pin to return. The current i is positive from stored capacitor charge toward the pin. With `u=Vc−3.3 V`, `L=L_loop+ESL_eq`, the KCL/KVL model is:

- `du/dt = −i/C_total`;
- `L di/dt = u − (Rfeed+ESR_eq)i + Rfeed I_load`;
- `Vpin = 3.3 V − Rfeed(I_load−i)`.

Thus ESR, ESL, capacitor discharge **and recharge** affect the result. Rfeed represents the common source impedance for this circuit, not a new harness-wire sizing lesson. Initially the capacitor is charged to 3.3 V and i=0. All arms receive the **same pulse train**: increments 0.10 / 0.16 / 0.08 A, linear rise/fall times 20 / 40 / 10 ns, 500 ns plateaus, starts 10 µs apart; three cycles 100 µs apart. There are **9 rising and 9 falling ramps**. A variable-step implicit trapezoid solves the two states: at most 0.5 ns near pulses, 50 ns in long idle intervals; refinement is tested rather than assuming the grid is exact. Overshoot is reported, not clipped.

The impedance table describes the **capacitor branch**, not the full driving-point impedance of the source in parallel. Pin response also depends on Rfeed and the workload spectrum. #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Synthetic PDN: source through shared Rfeed, load, series ESL+ESR+C shunt.
All arms have EXACTLY 10 uF total and consume the SAME piecewise-linear pulses.
Identical parallel caps combine as C=N*Cpart, ESR=ESRpart/N, ESL=ESLpart/N;
shared placement-loop inductance is added ONCE, never divided by N.
Frequency impedance, SRF and time-domain pin voltage are different metrics.
No real component/PCB characterization, FCC/CE or fuse certification. Stdlib only.
"""
from math import ceil, pi, sqrt
from typing import NamedTuple

VRAIL = 3.3
RFEED = 0.5  # ohm, shared synthetic regulator/source impedance, not 11.1 wire sizing
HF_HZ = 20e6
TOTAL_C = 10e-6
PULSES = ((0.10, 20e-9), (0.16, 40e-9), (0.08, 10e-9))


class Bank(NamedTuple):
    name: str
    count: int
    each_uf: float
    each_esr: float  # ohm
    each_esl_nh: float
    loop_nh: float


BANKS = (Bank("far_bulk", 1, 10., .20, 5., 40.),
         Bank("local_single", 1, 10., .02, 1., 3.),
         Bank("local_split", 10, 1., .04, .8, 3.),
         Bank("long_trace", 10, 1., .04, .8, 100.))


def equivalent(bank):
    return (bank.count * bank.each_uf * 1e-6, bank.each_esr / bank.count,
            (bank.loop_nh + bank.each_esl_nh / bank.count) * 1e-9)


def impedance(bank, frequency):
    c, r, inductance = equivalent(bank)
    omega = 2 * pi * frequency
    return complex(r, omega * inductance - 1 / (omega * c))


def srf(bank):
    """Mounted-path resonance includes the shared loop, not bare-part SRF."""
    c, _, inductance = equivalent(bank)
    return 1 / (2 * pi * sqrt(inductance * c))


def component_srf(bank):
    """Intrinsic identical-bank SRF before the shared placement-loop L."""
    c, _, _ = equivalent(bank)
    esl = bank.each_esl_nh / bank.count * 1e-9
    return 1 / (2 * pi * sqrt(esl * c))


def pulse_segments():
    """(duration, I_start, I_end, max_step); same linear ramps and hold in all arms."""
    segments = []
    now = 0.
    for cycle in range(3):
        for index, (amplitude, rise) in enumerate(PULSES):
            start = cycle * 100e-6 + index * 10e-6
            if start > now:
                segments.append((start - now, 0., 0., 50e-9))
            segments.extend(((rise, 0., amplitude, .5e-9),
                             (500e-9, amplitude, amplitude, .5e-9),
                             (rise, amplitude, 0., .5e-9),
                             (1000e-9, 0., 0., .5e-9)))
            now = start + 2 * rise + 1500e-9
    segments.append((300e-6 - now, 0., 0., 50e-9))
    return tuple(segments)


def step(bank, u, current, load0, load1, dt):
    """Trapezoid on u=Vc-Vrail, i=capacitor-to-node current; KCL+KVL.
    du/dt=-i/C; L di/dt=u-(Rfeed+ESR)i+Rfeed*Iload.
    Vpin=Vrail-Rfeed*(Iload-i). Includes charge/recharge, not L*di/dt only.
    """
    c, r, inductance = equivalent(bank)
    a = dt / (2 * c)
    b = dt / (2 * inductance)
    damping = b * (RFEED + r)
    rhs0 = u - a * current
    rhs1 = b * u + (1 - damping) * current + b * RFEED * (load0 + load1)
    next_i = (rhs1 + b * rhs0) / (1 + damping + a * b)
    next_u = rhs0 - a * next_i
    return next_u, next_i


def simulate(bank, segments, refinement=1):
    u = current = 0.
    minimum = maximum = VRAIL
    for duration, load0, load1, max_step in segments:
        n = ceil(duration / (max_step / refinement))
        dt = duration / n
        for k in range(n):
            before = load0 + (load1 - load0) * k / n
            after = load0 + (load1 - load0) * (k + 1) / n
            u, current = step(bank, u, current, before, after, dt)
            pin = VRAIL - RFEED * (after - current)
            minimum, maximum = min(minimum, pin), max(maximum, pin)
    return minimum, VRAIL - minimum, maximum


def main():
    segments = pulse_segments()
    print("Synthetic series ESL+ESR+C capacitor path; shared source Rfeed=0.5ohm")
    print("Equal total C=10uF; shared 9 rising/9 falling ramps; 500ns plateaus; 3 cycles")
    print("arm count each_uF total_uF ESR_eq_ohm Ltotal_nH cap_SRF_MHz mounted_SRF_MHz min_rail_V dip_V max_rail_V")
    for bank in BANKS:
        c, r, inductance = equivalent(bank)
        minimum, dip, maximum = simulate(bank, segments)
        print(f"{bank.name} {bank.count} {bank.each_uf:.1f} {c * 1e6:.1f} {r:.4f} {inductance * 1e9:.3f} {component_srf(bank) / 1e6:.6f} {srf(bank) / 1e6:.6f} {minimum:.6f} {dip:.6f} {maximum:.6f}")
    print("arm Z_0.1SRF_ohm Z_SRF_ohm Z_10SRF_ohm ImZ_0.1SRF_ohm ImZ_10SRF_ohm XL_over_XC_at_10SRF")
    for bank in BANKS:
        f = srf(bank)
        print(f"{bank.name} {abs(impedance(bank, .1 * f)):.6f} {abs(impedance(bank, f)):.6f} {abs(impedance(bank, 10 * f)):.6f} {impedance(bank, .1 * f).imag:.6f} {impedance(bank, 10 * f).imag:.6f} 100.000")
    print("20MHz above all SRFs: arm Xloop_ohm Xesl_eq_ohm Xc_ohm ESR_eq_ohm")
    for bank in BANKS:
        c, r, _ = equivalent(bank)
        omega = 2 * pi * HF_HZ
        print(f"{bank.name} {omega * bank.loop_nh * 1e-9:.6f} {omega * bank.each_esl_nh / bank.count * 1e-9:.6f} {1 / (omega * c):.6f} {r:.6f}")
    print("SRF cancels reactances, not ESR; above SRF the path becomes inductive")
    print("Shared loop L is not divided by capacitor count; not a universal part/layout winner")
    print("No DC-bias/tolerance/mutual-L/distributed-PCB/regulator dynamics; no FCC/CE or fuse claim")
    print("Real board / scope / EMC test: 本页未运行")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

## ESD and TVS clamp literacy

Decoupling keeps a rail stiff against load steps. ESD is a different threat: a short, high-voltage pulse into a pin. This section is about that pulse and a TVS-style clamp path. It is **not** the supply-dip ESL+ESR+C toy above, and it is **not** a fuse IR or certification exercise.

**Sources actually opened (PDF body verified with pdftotext).**

- **TI SSYA010A (*Electrostatic Discharge (ESD)*):** the Human Body Model (HBM) is a component-level stress that simulates a person discharging through a device; it uses a series RC of **100 pF** and **1500 Ω**. The short-circuit peak current of that network is about **V / 1500 Ω** (about **1.33 A at 2 kV**). The protection discussion describes clamps that discharge ESD current safely to ground, with a series resistor isolating a secondary clamp that protects a gate oxide.
- **TI SLVAE37 (*How to select a Surge Diode*):** a TVS diode should be invisible at nominal voltage, then **shunt** surge current to ground while **clamping** the voltage (`VCLAMP`) to a safe level. Key datasheet ideas include `VRWM` (max working voltage with low leakage), `VBR` (breakdown / turn-on, typically at 1 mA), `RDYN` (dynamic resistance), and `IPP` (peak pulse current). The PDF states **`VCLAMP = VBR + ISURGE × RDYN`**, so `VCLAMP` rises with surge current, and **`VBR` is always higher than `VRWM`** — during a surge, **`VRWM < VBR < VCLAMP`**. Pick `VRWM` above the nominal rail; do not trust `VBR` alone for leakage.
- **TI SLYT492 (*Design considerations for system-level ESD circuit protection*):** HBM / MM / CDM are **device-level** manufacturing stresses; **IEC 61000-4-2** is the **system-level** end-user test, with **contact** and **air-gap** discharge methods (table up to ±8 kV contact / ±15 kV air). Do not confuse an HBM rating with an IEC rating. A TVS also presents a **parasitic capacitance** shunt to ground that must sit in the signal-chain bandwidth budget; high-speed paths use lower parasitic capacitance.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Synthetic ESD / TVS clamp literacy toy. Stdlib only.

Shared HBM-style pulse (TI SSYA010A: 100 pF + 1500 ohm). Short-circuit peak
is about V/1500 ohm (~1.33 A at 2 kV). Arms share that source; protection
topology differs:

  A_unprotected  connector/IC pin are the same node; C_pin only (no clamp)
  B_tvs          TVS at the connector (= IC pin); clamp VBR + I*RDYN
  C_series_tvs   TVS at the connector, then series R, then an IC-pin node
                 with its own internal clamp (SSYA010-style primary/secondary)

Integration: backward Euler with DT=1 ps (clamp tau = RDYN*C_pin = 7.5 ps).
Window T_END >= 5*R_HBM*C_HBM. Most HBM energy dissipates in the 1500 ohm;
clamp energy is a small slice. Not an IEC 61000 certification.
ESD gun / real TVS lab: 本页未运行.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

# HBM network (SSYA010A)
C_HBM = 100e-12
R_HBM = 1500.0
V_HBM0 = 2000.0  # synthetic precharge; not a pass/fail claim
E_HBM0 = 0.5 * C_HBM * V_HBM0 * V_HBM0  # 200 uJ at nominal

# Synthetic clamp / pin (lesson values, not a datasheet part)
C_PIN = 5e-12
C_CONN = 5e-12  # connector node capacitance on arm C
R_SERIES = 33.0
VBR = 8.0  # synthetic breakdown / knee (SLVAE37 VBR)
RDYN = 1.5  # synthetic dynamic resistance (SLVAE37 RDYN)
# IC secondary clamp (arm C): same knee model, synthetic
VBR_IC = 8.0
RDYN_IC = 1.5

DT = 1e-12  # 1 ps; clamp tau = RDYN*C_PIN = 7.5 ps
T_END = 1.0e-6  # >= 5 * 150 ns HBM tau
SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
JITTER = 0.08
ARMS = ("A_unprotected", "B_tvs", "C_series_tvs")


def _clamp_current(v: float, vbr: float, rdyn: float) -> float:
    if v > vbr:
        return (v - vbr) / rdyn
    return 0.0


def simulate(arm: str, v_hbm: float, dt: float = DT, t_end: float = T_END) -> dict:
    """Backward-Euler HBM discharge. Returns peak/energy metrics."""
    n = int(t_end / dt)
    v_c = v_hbm
    # connector / pin state
    v_conn = 0.0
    v_ic = 0.0
    e_tvs = 0.0
    e_ic = 0.0
    e_rhbm = 0.0
    peak_conn = 0.0
    peak_ic = 0.0
    peak_i_ic = 0.0
    peak_i_tvs = 0.0

    for _ in range(n):
        if arm == "A_unprotected":
            # Single node: pin = connector. No clamp.
            # BE: C_pin/dt*(v_new-v_old) = (v_c_new - v_new)/R_HBM
            #     C_hbm/dt*(v_c_new-v_c) = -(v_c_new - v_new)/R_HBM
            a11 = C_HBM / dt + 1.0 / R_HBM
            a12 = -1.0 / R_HBM
            a21 = -1.0 / R_HBM
            a22 = C_PIN / dt + 1.0 / R_HBM
            b1 = C_HBM / dt * v_c
            b2 = C_PIN / dt * v_conn
            det = a11 * a22 - a12 * a21
            v_c_new = (b1 * a22 - a12 * b2) / det
            v_new = (a11 * b2 - a21 * b1) / det
            i_in = (v_c_new - v_new) / R_HBM
            i_tvs = 0.0
            i_ic = 0.0
            v_conn = v_new
            v_ic = v_new
            v_c = v_c_new
        elif arm == "B_tvs":
            # Single node with TVS: i_tvs = max(0,(v-VBR)/RDYN)
            # Solve assuming clamp on, then fall back if v_new <= VBR.
            def solve(clamp_on: bool):
                g_c = C_HBM / dt
                g_p = C_PIN / dt
                g_r = 1.0 / R_HBM
                g_d = (1.0 / RDYN) if clamp_on else 0.0
                # g_c*(vc'-vc) = -(vc'-v)/R
                # g_p*(v'-v) = (vc'-v)/R - g_d*(v'-VBR)   [if clamp: i=(v'-VBR)/RDYN]
                # Rearrange:
                # (g_c+g_r) vc' - g_r v' = g_c vc
                # -g_r vc' + (g_p+g_r+g_d) v' = g_p v + g_d*VBR
                a11 = g_c + g_r
                a12 = -g_r
                a21 = -g_r
                a22 = g_p + g_r + g_d
                b1 = g_c * v_c
                b2 = g_p * v_conn + (g_d * VBR)
                det = a11 * a22 - a12 * a21
                vc = (b1 * a22 - a12 * b2) / det
                vv = (a11 * b2 - a21 * b1) / det
                return vc, vv

            v_c_new, v_new = solve(True)
            if v_new <= VBR:
                v_c_new, v_new = solve(False)
                i_tvs = 0.0
            else:
                i_tvs = (v_new - VBR) / RDYN
            i_in = (v_c_new - v_new) / R_HBM
            i_ic = 0.0  # no separate IC clamp; TVS is at the pin
            v_conn = v_new
            v_ic = v_new
            v_c = v_c_new
        else:
            # C: connector (TVS) -- R_SERIES -- ic_pin (IC clamp)
            # Three unknowns: vc, vconn, vic. Try both clamps on, then relax.
            def solve(tvs_on: bool, ic_on: bool):
                g_c = C_HBM / dt
                g_n = C_CONN / dt
                g_p = C_PIN / dt
                g_r = 1.0 / R_HBM
                g_s = 1.0 / R_SERIES
                g_t = (1.0 / RDYN) if tvs_on else 0.0
                g_i = (1.0 / RDYN_IC) if ic_on else 0.0
                # eqs:
                # (g_c+g_r) vc - g_r vconn = g_c v_c
                # -g_r vc + (g_n+g_r+g_s+g_t) vconn - g_s vic = g_n v_conn + g_t VBR
                # -g_s vconn + (g_p+g_s+g_i) vic = g_p v_ic + g_i VBR_IC
                a11, a12, a13 = g_c + g_r, -g_r, 0.0
                a21, a22, a23 = -g_r, g_n + g_r + g_s + g_t, -g_s
                a31, a32, a33 = 0.0, -g_s, g_p + g_s + g_i
                b1 = g_c * v_c
                b2 = g_n * v_conn + g_t * VBR
                b3 = g_p * v_ic + g_i * VBR_IC
                # 3x3 solve
                det = (
                    a11 * (a22 * a33 - a23 * a32)
                    - a12 * (a21 * a33 - a23 * a31)
                    + a13 * (a21 * a32 - a22 * a31)
                )
                vc = (
                    b1 * (a22 * a33 - a23 * a32)
                    - a12 * (b2 * a33 - a23 * b3)
                    + a13 * (b2 * a32 - a22 * b3)
                ) / det
                vn = (
                    a11 * (b2 * a33 - a23 * b3)
                    - b1 * (a21 * a33 - a23 * a31)
                    + a13 * (a21 * b3 - b2 * a31)
                ) / det
                vi = (
                    a11 * (a22 * b3 - b2 * a32)
                    - a12 * (a21 * b3 - b2 * a31)
                    + b1 * (a21 * a32 - a22 * a31)
                ) / det
                return vc, vn, vi

            tvs_on, ic_on = True, True
            v_c_new, v_conn_new, v_ic_new = solve(tvs_on, ic_on)
            if v_conn_new <= VBR:
                tvs_on = False
                v_c_new, v_conn_new, v_ic_new = solve(tvs_on, ic_on)
            if v_ic_new <= VBR_IC:
                ic_on = False
                v_c_new, v_conn_new, v_ic_new = solve(tvs_on, ic_on)
            i_tvs = _clamp_current(v_conn_new, VBR, RDYN) if tvs_on else 0.0
            i_ic = _clamp_current(v_ic_new, VBR_IC, RDYN_IC) if ic_on else 0.0
            i_in = (v_c_new - v_conn_new) / R_HBM
            v_c, v_conn, v_ic = v_c_new, v_conn_new, v_ic_new

        e_rhbm += i_in * i_in * R_HBM * dt
        e_tvs += i_tvs * v_conn * dt
        e_ic += i_ic * v_ic * dt
        peak_conn = max(peak_conn, v_conn)
        peak_ic = max(peak_ic, v_ic)
        peak_i_tvs = max(peak_i_tvs, i_tvs)
        peak_i_ic = max(peak_i_ic, i_ic)

    return {
        "peak_conn_V": peak_conn,
        "peak_ic_V": peak_ic,
        "peak_i_tvs_A": peak_i_tvs,
        "peak_i_ic_A": peak_i_ic,
        "e_tvs_J": e_tvs,
        "e_ic_J": e_ic,
        "e_rhbm_J": e_rhbm,
        "e_stored_J": 0.5 * C_HBM * v_hbm * v_hbm,
        "v_hbm_final": v_c,
    }

## Wheatstone bridge and instrumentation-amp sense

The earlier divider and LED maths treat a single-ended voltage against ground. Strain, force and pressure sensors more often use a **Wheatstone bridge**: four resistors, a differential mid-point voltage, and an instrumentation amplifier that rejects the shared common-mode rail. This section is about that path. It is **not** the ESD/TVS clamp toy, and it is **not** the ADC ENOB noise toy.

- **TI SLOA034 (*Signal Conditioning Wheatstone Resistive Bridge Sensors*):** resistive bridge sensors (strain / pressure) produce a differential `Vsig = (SIG+) − (SIG−)` on top of a common-mode near `½ Vexc`; full-scale bridge outputs are typically **10–100 mV** and need a differential / instrumentation amplifier that rejects common mode.
- **TI INAx126 datasheet:** micropower instrumentation amp; gain equation **`G = 5 + (80 kΩ / RG)`** (5 V/V to 10 000 V/V); low input offset (250 µV max class); **CMRR min 80 dB** (typ 94 dB, `RS = 0 Ω`). Numbers 以原文为准.
- **Wikipedia — Wheatstone bridge:** two voltage-divider legs; galvanometer / differential voltage `V_G` is the difference of the mid-points; at balance `V_G = 0` and `R2/R1 = Rx/R3`. Numbers 以原文为准. Real bridge / INA / strain-gauge lab: **本页未运行**.

### Quarter-bridge literacy

With three arms at `R0` and one active gauge `R0(1+δ)`, the right mid-point is `Vexc · (1+δ)/(2+δ)` and the left mid-point stays at `Vexc/2`, so

<Formula label="Quarter-bridge differential">V_G = Vexc · ((1+δ)/(2+δ) − 1/2)</Formula>

For small `δ` this is about `Vexc · δ/4`. Defining gauge factor by `δ = ΔR/R = GF · ε` (strain ε), the small-strain quarter-bridge output is therefore about **`Vexc · GF · ε / 4`**.

A single-ended gauge divider against mid-rail sees the **same** gauge law but keeps the **full** common-mode (CM) glitch. The bridge adds that same CM sample to **both** legs, subtracts, then an instrumentation amp with finite **CMRR** (common-mode rejection ratio) lets only a residual `cm / 10^(CMRR_dB/20)` through — so CM error is attenuated, not magically zero. This toy uses the INA126 datasheet **minimum CMRR = 80 dB**. An INA-like stage also multiplies by `G` and can add an input-referred offset.

### Fair setup

`wheatstone_bridge_sense_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. Every arm shares the **same** `Vexc = 5 V`, `R0 = 350 Ω`, post-gain `G = 100`, and the **same** regime `(δ, CM σ)` schedule. For each `(seed, sample index)`, arms A/B/C receive the **identical** CM draw (common random numbers); B and C add it to both legs before the differential.

| Arm | Readout |
|---|---|
| A_single_ended | gauge divider vs mid-rail + **full** CM, then `G` |
| B_bridge_ideal | both legs + CM → differential + **CMRR residual**, then `G` (no Vos) |
| C_bridge_offset | same bridge path + INA-like RTI offset, then `G` |

Regimes: `small_delta_noisy_cm` (bridge SNR wins) and `large_delta_quiet_cm` (**near-tie / small gap** — about 2.6 dB, not a true tie; no presumed winner). Metrics: sensitivity, offset error, and **multi-sample SNR** `|Δmean| / pooled sample std` over 64 draws (not a single-shot `|signal|/noise_floor` with asymmetric floors). **Multi-sample SNR cannot see the static offset `Vos`:** the same constant appears in every sample, so it cancels in `Δmean`. By contrast, for uncorrelated noise, averaging N samples improves SNR by about √N; but `Vos` is a DC offset, so averaging does not remove it. Arm C’s slightly higher SNR than B therefore does **not** mean C is better — C’s cost shows up in offset and absolute accuracy (~0.024 V vs ~0.0004 V), and C does not win. Five-seed mean±sd.

"""Synthetic Wheatstone bridge vs single-ended sense literacy toy. Stdlib only.

Shared excitation, gauge R0 and fractional ΔR within each regime for every arm.
Common-mode (CM) samples are **common random numbers**: for a given (seed, sample
index), arms A/B/C all see the **same** cm draw. Bridge arms add that cm to **both**
legs, take the differential, then apply a **finite CMRR** residual
(INA126 Electrical Characteristics: CMRR min **80 dB**, RS=0 Ω).

Arms:
  A_single_ended   gauge divider vs mid-rail + full cm, then gain G
  B_bridge_ideal   quarter-bridge (cm on both legs) + CMRR residual, no Vos
  C_bridge_offset  same bridge path + INA-like RTI offset

Regimes (≥5 seeds; multi-sample SNR = |Δmean| / pooled sample std):
  small_delta_noisy_cm  — small ΔR, large CM: bridge SNR wins
  large_delta_quiet_cm  — large ΔR, tiny CM: near-tie / small SNR gap (~2.6 dB)
No presumed winner. Not a strain-gauge lab. Real bridge / INA: 本页未运行.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

VEXC = 5.0  # synthetic excitation
R0 = 350.0  # synthetic gauge ohms (TI SLOA034-style order)
G_AMP = 100.0  # post gain (synthetic; INA126 G=5+80k/Rg can hit ~100)
VOS_NOM = 250e-6  # INA126-class input offset, synthetic RTI max order
DIFF_NOISE = 5.0e-5  # independent residual after differencing, V
# TI INA126 Electrical Characteristics: CMRR min 80 dB (typ 94 dB), RS=0Ω
CMRR_DB = 80.0
CMRR_LINEAR = 10.0 ** (CMRR_DB / 20.0)  # Ad/Acm; residual = cm / CMRR_LINEAR
N_SAMPLES = 64  # multi-sample SNR / std
SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
ARMS = ("A_single_ended", "B_bridge_ideal", "C_bridge_offset")
# Private noise/offset only — CM uses a seed-only RNG (no arm salt)
_ARM_SALT = {"A_single_ended": 11, "B_bridge_ideal": 22, "C_bridge_offset": 33}
REGIMES = (
    ("small_delta_noisy_cm", 0.002, 2.0e-3),
    ("large_delta_quiet_cm", 0.20, 4.0e-5),
)


def quarter_bridge_nodes(delta: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Left mid (fixed R0/R0) and right mid (R0 over R0*(1+delta))."""
    v_left = VEXC * 0.5
    r_g = R0 * (1.0 + delta)
    v_right = VEXC * r_g / (R0 + r_g)
    return v_left, v_right


def v_bridge(delta: float) -> float:
    v_l, v_r = quarter_bridge_nodes(delta)
    return v_r - v_l


def v_single_ended(delta: float) -> float:
    _, v_r = quarter_bridge_nodes(delta)
    return v_r - VEXC * 0.5


def shared_cm_sequence(seed: int, cm_sigma: float, n: int) -> list[float]:
    """Common random numbers: identical for every arm at this seed."""
    rng = random.Random(50_000 + seed)  # seed only — no arm salt
    return [rng.gauss(0.0, cm_sigma) for _ in range(n)]


def measure_samples(
    delta: float,
    cm_sigma: float,
    arm: str,
    seed: int,
    stream_id: int,
) -> tuple[list[float], list[float]]:
    """Return (outputs, cm_used). CM sequence is shared across arms for (seed, stream_id)."""
    # Independent streams for δ=0 vs δ so CM does not cancel in the Δmean signal
    cms = shared_cm_sequence(seed + 10_007 * stream_id, cm_sigma, N_SAMPLES)
    # Private diff-noise advances per stream; Vos is fixed per (arm, seed) across streams
    rng_priv = random.Random(60_000 + seed * 17 + _ARM_SALT[arm] + 97 * stream_id)
    vos = 0.0
    if arm == "C_bridge_offset":
        rng_vos = random.Random(70_000 + seed * 17 + _ARM_SALT[arm])
        vos = VOS_NOM * (0.6 + 0.8 * rng_vos.random())
        if rng_vos.random() < 0.5:
            vos = -vos
    out: list[float] = []
    for cm in cms:
        diff_n = rng_priv.gauss(0.0, DIFF_NOISE)
        v_l, v_r = quarter_bridge_nodes(delta)
        if arm == "A_single_ended":
            # Full CM on the gauge node vs clean mid-rail reference
            raw = (v_r - VEXC * 0.5) + cm + diff_n
        else:
            # Add the **same** cm to both legs, then differential
            v_l_m = v_l + cm
            v_r_m = v_r + cm
            v_diff = v_r_m - v_l_m  # equals v_r - v_l (identical cm cancels in subtract)
            # Finite CMRR: residual input-referred CM error = cm / CMRR_LINEAR
            cm_resid = cm / CMRR_LINEAR
            raw = v_diff + cm_resid + vos + diff_n
        out.append(G_AMP * raw)
    return out, cms


def sample_mean_std(xs: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    n = len(xs)
    mu = sum(xs) / n
    var = sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (n - 1)
    return mu, math.sqrt(var)


def metrics_for(
    delta: float, cm_sigma: float, arm: str, seed: int
) -> tuple[float, float, float, list[float]]:
    """Return (sens, offset_err, snr_db, cm_stream_at_delta) using multi-sample SNR."""
    y0, _ = measure_samples(0.0, cm_sigma, arm, seed, stream_id=0)
    y1, cms1 = measure_samples(delta, cm_sigma, arm, seed, stream_id=1)
    m0, s0 = sample_mean_std(y0)
    m1, s1 = sample_mean_std(y1)
    signal = m1 - m0
    # Pooled sample std (same N); floor guards against zero
    noise = math.sqrt(0.5 * (s0 * s0 + s1 * s1))
    noise = max(noise, 1e-12)
    snr = 20.0 * math.log10(max(abs(signal), 1e-30) / noise)
    sens = signal / delta if delta else 0.0
    offset_err = abs(m0)
    return sens, offset_err, snr, cms1


def mean_sd(xs: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    var = sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1)
    return mu, math.sqrt(var)

    print(

        f"CMRR={CMRR_DB:.0f}dB(INA126 min) N_samples={N_SAMPLES} seeds={len(SEEDS)}"
    )
    print(
        "model: quarter-bridge V_G=Vexc*(r_g/(R0+r_g)-1/2)≈Vexc*δ/4 (small δ); "
        "δ=GF*ε → V_G≈Vexc*GF*ε/4; "
        "shared cm on both bridge legs then differential + residual cm/CMRR_linear; "
        "multi-sample SNR=|Δmean|/pooled_std "
        f"(TI INA126 CMRR min {CMRR_DB:.0f} dB; SLOA034 bridge)"
    )
    print(
        "regime arm sens_V_per_delta offset_err_V snr_dB (mean+-sd over seeds)"
    )
    summary = {}
    for reg_name, delta, cm in REGIMES:
        for arm in ARMS:
            sens_l, off_l, snr_l = [], [], []
            for seed in SEEDS:
                s, o, sn, _ = metrics_for(delta, cm, arm, seed)
                sens_l.append(s)
                off_l.append(o)
                snr_l.append(sn)
            sm, ss = mean_sd(sens_l)
            om, os_ = mean_sd(off_l)
            nm, ns = mean_sd(snr_l)
            summary[(reg_name, arm)] = (nm, om)
            print(

                f"{om:.4f}+-{os_:.4f} {nm:.4f}+-{ns:.4f}"
            )
    a_s = summary[("small_delta_noisy_cm", "A_single_ended")][0]
    b_s = summary[("small_delta_noisy_cm", "B_bridge_ideal")][0]
    a_l = summary[("large_delta_quiet_cm", "A_single_ended")][0]

## Bridge rectifier and ripple

- **Wikipedia — Rectifier:** without smoothing, half-wave gives an average of `Vpeak / π` and full-wave `2 Vpeak / π`; each conducting silicon diode drops about 0.7 V (0.3 V for Schottky), so a half-wave rectifier loses one drop and a bridge loses two; ripple is at the source frequency for half-wave and twice it for full-wave, usually quoted peak-to-peak; the needed capacitor grows with load current and shrinks with frequency and with the number of peaks per cycle; and a bigger capacitor trades ripple voltage for **ripple current**, whose peak is set by the rising edge of the sine and limited by the winding resistance. 以原文为准.
- **Wikipedia — Ripple (electrical):** for a large RC and small ripple the capacitor discharges almost linearly, so `Q = C·Vpp = I·t` with `t = 1 / (2f)` for full-wave, giving `Vpp = I / (2fC)`. 以原文为准.

**Which two diodes conduct.** Number the bridge D1 upper-left, D2 upper-right, D3 lower-right, D4 lower-left, with every anode below its cathode, so the top rail is + and the bottom rail is −. Call the half-cycle with terminal a above terminal b the positive one: on the positive half-cycle D1 and D3 conduct; on the negative half-cycle D2 and D4 conduct. The other pair is reverse-biased each time. Either way the load current flows from the + rail to the − rail in both half-cycles, which is why the output never changes sign, and either way the path holds two diodes, which is where the two drops `|v| − 2 VF` in the toy come from. ### Ripple from charge balance

<Formula label="Peak-to-peak ripple, linear discharge (full-wave)">Vpp ≈ I / (2 f C)</Formula>

<Formula label="Half-wave: one refill per cycle">Vpp ≈ I / (f C)</Formula>

Between refills the load draws a nearly constant current `I`, so the capacitor loses `ΔQ = I · t` and its voltage falls by `ΔQ / C`. A bridge refills every half cycle (`t = 1/(2f)`), a single diode only once per cycle (`t = 1/f`), so for the same `I` and `C` the half-wave ripple is twice as large.

**Worked example.** A 12 V rms secondary peaks at `12 × √2 = 16.97 V`; after two 0.7 V drops a bridge charges to about 15.6 V. With `R = 50 Ω` that is `I ≈ 0.3 A`. At `f = 50 Hz` and `C = 1000 µF`: bridge `Vpp ≈ 0.3 / (2 × 50 × 0.001) = 3.0 V`, half-wave `0.3 / (50 × 0.001) = 6.0 V`. Three volts on 14 V is about 20 %, so this rail is not "DC" by the 10 % rule below; keeping a bridge under 1.4 V needs `C ≥ 0.3 / (2 × 50 × 1.4) ≈ 2.1 mF`. The law ignores the time the diodes spend conducting, so it **over-predicts** the ripple: the toy measures 0.76–0.78 of it for the bridge at this load.

**Correctness criterion for the law.** On every run the toy divides the simulated ripple by `I / (k f C)` (`k = 1` half-wave, `k = 2` bridge, `I = vdc / R`). It is required to agree within 25 % when the ripple is small; when the ripple is more than 10 % of the mean output, the run is labelled **not DC** and the law is not expected to hold.

**Integration step and its stability criterion.** The capacitor node is integrated with forward Euler at `DT = 5 µs`. While the diodes conduct, the node is a linear decay with rate `1/(RS C) + 1/(R C)`, and forward Euler is stable on it when `DT ≤ 2 / (1/(RS C) + 1/(R C))`. The toy prints that limit for each regime at the smallest C and R drawn (169.7 µs in `tiny_C_heavy`, up to 3749.6 µs in `large_C_light`) and asserts `DT` is under 5 % of it. The tests check that the Euler factor decays at 0.98× and grows at 1.02× of the limit, and that `DT` and `DT/2` agree (the toy prints 5.969e-04 V on vdc for one case).

### Fair setup

`bridge_rectifier_ripple_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:**

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Synthetic half-wave vs diode-bridge rectifier with a smoothing capacitor. Stdlib only.

Source: v(t) = VPK sin(2 pi F t) behind a winding resistance RS (no transformer regulation,
no leakage inductance). Diodes are ideal switches with a series forward drop VF each:
  A_half     one diode then C || R            rectified source = v - VF when v > VF
  B_bridge   four-diode bridge then C || R    rectified source = |v| - 2 VF (two diodes conduct)
  C_noC      the same bridge, no capacitor    v_out = max(0, |v| - 2 VF) R / (R + RS)
Capacitor node (A, B): C dV/dt = i_d - V / R, with i_d = max(0, v_rect - V) / RS while the
diodes conduct. Integrated with forward Euler at step DT. Euler is stable for this linear node
when DT <= 2 / (1/(RS C) + 1/(R C)) (the conducting phase is the stiff one); the toy prints the
limit for every regime and asserts DT is far below it; the tests check decay at 0.98x and
growth at 1.02x of that limit, and that DT and DT/2 agree.
Correctness criterion: with ripple pp small, the linear-discharge law V_pp ~= I / (k F C)
(k = 1 half-wave, k = 2 full-wave; Wikipedia Ripple (electrical): V_pp = I / (2 f C) for
full-wave) must agree with the simulated pp within 25 % (the law ignores the conduction time,
so it over-predicts). A run whose ripple pp exceeds 10 % of its mean is labelled "not DC".
Common random numbers: per seed the line amplitude (+-5 %), the capacitor tolerance (+-20 %),
the load (+-10 %) and VF (0.65-0.75 V) are drawn once and shared by every arm and regime
(each regime scales the nominal C and R). Metrics over the last 10 cycles of 20:
vdc (mean output, V), ripple (pp, V), ipk (peak diode current, A; stress proxy).
Labels: tie = identical on every seed; numerically zero = |paired mean| and sd both < 1e-9
(effectively a tie, no sign count); |m| <= sd = near-tie; |m| > sd = consistent gap (small if
|m| < 20 % of the smaller arm mean, else clear), with a sign count.
Not a Wheatstone bridge (a measurement bridge) and not TVS surge clamping.
Real mains / transformer / PSU bench: 本页未运行. Wikipedia formulas: 以原文为准.
"""
import math
import random

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
F = 50.0  # Hz line frequency
VPK = 12.0 * math.sqrt(2.0)  # V, 12 V rms secondary
RS = 0.5  # ohm, winding + wiring resistance
DT = 5e-6  # s, Euler step
CYCLES, MEASURE = 20, 10
TOL_LAW = 0.25
DC_LIMIT = 0.10
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9
ARMS = ("A_half", "B_bridge", "C_noC")
# name: (nominal C in uF, nominal R in ohm); pre-declared before the first run
REGIMES = {
    "small_C_heavy": (470.0, 20.0),
    "medium": (1000.0, 50.0),
    "large_C_light": (4700.0, 200.0),
    "tiny_C_heavy": (220.0, 15.0),
}


def shared_draws(seed):
    """Common random numbers: one draw per seed, shared by every arm and regime."""
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    return {"amp": rng.uniform(0.95, 1.05), "c": rng.uniform(0.8, 1.2), "r": rng.uniform(0.9, 1.1), "vf": rng.uniform(0.65, 0.75)}


def euler_h_max(c, r):
    return 2.0 / (1.0 / (RS * c) + 1.0 / (r * c))


def euler_amp(h, c, r):
    """Growth factor of one Euler step on the conducting node, |1 - h (1/(RS C) + 1/(R C))|."""
    return abs(1.0 - h * (1.0 / (RS * c) + 1.0 / (r * c)))


def simulate(arm, regime, draws, dt=DT):
    c_nom, r_nom = REGIMES[regime]
    c, r = c_nom * 1e-6 * draws["c"], r_nom * draws["r"]
    vpk, vf = VPK * draws["amp"], draws["vf"]
    n = round(CYCLES / F / dt)
    start = round((CYCLES - MEASURE) / F / dt)
    v = 0.0
    vs, ipk = [], 0.0
    for i in range(n):
        src = vpk * math.sin(2.0 * math.pi * F * i * dt)
        rect = src - vf if arm == "A_half" else abs(src) - 2.0 * vf
        if arm == "C_noC":
            v = max(0.0, rect) * r / (r + RS)
            i_d = v / r
        else:
            i_d = max(0.0, rect - v) / RS
            v += dt * (i_d - v / r) / c
        if i >= start:
            vs.append(v)
            ipk = max(ipk, i_d)
    vdc = sum(vs) / len(vs)
    return {"vdc": vdc, "ripple": max(vs) - min(vs), "ipk": ipk, "c": c, "r": r}


def law(arm, res):
    k = 1.0 if arm == "A_half" else 2.0
    return (res["vdc"] / res["r"]) / (k * F * res["c"])


def mean_sd(xs):
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x):
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-5:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def verdict(xa, xb):
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


KEYS = ("vdc", "ripple", "ipk")


def main():
    print("Synthetic half-wave vs bridge rectifier with C || R; ideal diodes + series VF; not a PSU bench, not a Wheatstone bridge")
    print(f"F={F} Hz VPK={VPK:.4f} V RS={RS} ohm DT={DT * 1e6:.0f} us cycles={CYCLES} (last {MEASURE} measured) seeds={len(SEEDS)}")
    draws = [shared_draws(s) for s in SEEDS]
    for regime, (c_nom, r_nom) in REGIMES.items():
        c_min = c_nom * 1e-6 * 0.8
        h = euler_h_max(c_min, r_nom * 0.9)
        assert DT < 0.05 * h
        print(f"Euler limit {regime}: h_max={h * 1e6:.1f} us at the smallest C and R drawn; DT={DT * 1e6:.0f} us; |1 - DT/tau|={euler_amp(DT, c_min, r_nom * 0.9):.6f} < 1")
    print("regime arm vdc_V ripple_pp_V ipk_A  (mean+-sd over seeds)")
    for regime, (c_nom, r_nom) in REGIMES.items():
        print(f"{regime} (C={c_nom:.0f} uF, R={r_nom:.0f} ohm nominal)")
        res = {arm: [simulate(arm, regime, d) for d in draws] for arm in ARMS}
        for arm in ARMS:
            cells = " ".join("{}+-{}".format(*map(num, mean_sd([x[k] for x in res[arm]]))) for k in KEYS)
            share = max(x["ripple"] / x["vdc"] for x in res[arm])
            tag = "DC" if share <= DC_LIMIT else "not DC (ripple > 10 % of vdc)"
            print(f"  {arm} {cells}; worst ripple/vdc {100 * share:.1f} % -> {tag}")
        for arm in ("A_half", "B_bridge"):
            ratios = [x["ripple"] / law(arm, x) for x in res[arm]]
            lo, hi = min(ratios), max(ratios)
            ok = all(abs(q - 1.0) <= TOL_LAW for q in ratios)
            small = all(x["ripple"] / x["vdc"] <= DC_LIMIT for x in res[arm])
            state = "within 25 %" if ok else ("outside 25 %" + ("" if small else " (ripple not small; law not expected to hold)"))
            print(f"  law {arm}: simulated / (I/({1 if arm == 'A_half' else 2} F C)) = {lo:.4f}..{hi:.4f} -> {state}")
        for x, y in (("B_bridge", "A_half"), ("C_noC", "B_bridge")):
            for k in KEYS:
                print(f"  paired {x[0]}-{y[0]} {k}: {verdict([r[k] for r in res[x]], [r[k] for r in res[y]])}")
    d0 = draws[0]
    a, b = simulate("B_bridge", "medium", d0), simulate("B_bridge", "medium", d0, DT / 2)
    print(f"step check (B, medium, seed 0): DT vs DT/2 vdc {abs(a['vdc'] - b['vdc']):.3e} V, ripple {abs(a['ripple'] - b['ripple']):.3e} V")
    print("No winner declared: the bridge halves the ripple period and the charge per refill, but pays a second diode drop; without C the output is not DC")
    print("Real mains / transformer / PSU bench: 本页未运行; Wikipedia formulas 以原文为准")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

### Read the regimes

How the labels are used on this page, in this order: **tie: identical on every seed** only when the paired per-seed difference is exactly zero on every seed; **numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie** when `|paired mean|` and its sd are both below 1e-9 (no sign count is printed on such a line); **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** when `|paired mean| ≤ sd`; otherwise a **consistent** gap (`|paired mean| > sd`), called **small** if `|m|` is under 20 % of the smaller arm mean and **clear** otherwise; the 20 % rule only chooses between those two words. Every consistent line carries a same-sign count over the 5 seeds, and any seed with the opposite sign is reported; a sign count alone never makes a gap consistent. Gaps are computed from unrounded values. Paired differences are B−A and C−B.

- **small_C_heavy:** both capacitor arms are **not DC** (worst ripple 165.3 % of vdc for A, 71.1 % for B). The bridge still **wins on ripple with a clear gap** (paired B−A −5.2981±0.2119 V, same sign on all 5 seeds) and on mean output (2.8946±0.2236 V, clear, 5/5), because a refill every half cycle keeps the capacitor from sagging; its peak diode current is lower by a small but consistent gap (−0.3169±0.0960 A, 5/5). The linear law is far off here (bridge 0.55–0.61 of it), as expected for large ripple.
- **medium:** the bridge halves the ripple (paired −2.3476±0.2445 V, clear, 5/5) and cuts the peak diode current (−0.8996±0.0907 A, clear, 5/5); its mean output is higher by a small but consistent gap (0.5862±0.1365 V, 5/5). Both arms are still **not DC** (A 40.5 %, B 18.4 %). The law holds within 25 % (A 0.83–0.85, B 0.76–0.78).
- **large_C_light:** both arms are DC (A 2.4 %, B 1.1 %), and the law is within 25 % (A 0.91–0.93, B 0.86–0.87). **B loses on mean output with a small but consistent gap:** paired B−A −0.4146±0.0308 V, same sign on all 5 seeds, because the second diode drop is no longer hidden by a deep sag on A. - **tiny_C_heavy:** everything is **not DC**. B−A ripple −4.1851±0.4101 V (clear, 5/5); the peak-current gap shrinks to a small but consistent −0.0446±0.0022 A (5/5).
- **C_noC:** without a capacitor the output is the rectified sine itself, ripple about 165 % of vdc in every regime, so it is **not DC** everywhere; C−B ripple is a clear loss in all four regimes. **B loses on peak diode current to C with a clear gap** in every regime (for example −1.5598±0.1176 A in `medium`): the capacitor that smooths the output is also what makes the diodes deliver short, tall pulses. In `tiny_C_heavy` C−B vdc is only a small but consistent gap (−0.9082±0.1617 V, 5/5), because a 220 µF capacitor barely holds anything at 15 Ω.

## Zener shunt regulators and LDO headroom

<Resource id="wiki-zener" />
<Resource id="wiki-ldo" />

A **Zener** used as a **shunt regulator** is reverse-biased in parallel with the load: once the voltage reaches the breakdown (Zener) voltage, the diode's low impedance holds the node near that value, and a series resistor limits the current (Wikipedia, Zener diode; 以原文为准). The same page requires the series resistor to be large enough that I_D × V_B < P_max, a **strict** inequality, so the device is not destroyed. An **LDO** is a linear regulator that can still regulate when the supply is already **very close** to the output; the remaining drop is that of a pass element that can be driven into saturation, and the dissipated power is (V_IN − V_OUT) I_OUT + V_IN I_Q (Wikipedia, Low-dropout regulator; 以原文为准). The LDO page we read does **not** quote a dropout in millivolts, so none is invented here: HEADROOM = 0.40 V is a declared toy parameter.

<Resource id="ti-slaa414" />

<Resource id="onsemi-hbd854" />

**Headroom is not filtering: PSRR falls with frequency.** Dropout headroom only says whether the LDO can still regulate; it says nothing about how well it rejects ripple. The same Wikipedia LDO page notes that the error amplifier is limited in its ability to correct small spikes at high frequencies, that most LDOs have relatively high PSRR only at lower frequencies (10 Hz – 1 kHz), and that PSRR varies over frequency, temperature, current, output voltage and voltage differential (以原文为准). TI's application report SLAA414A defines PSRR = 20 log(ripple input / ripple output), quoted over a frequency range (10 Hz to 1 MHz is common), and says LDOs have historically had poor high-frequency PSRR (以文档为准). So an LDO with plenty of headroom can still pass a switcher's ripple: read the PSRR curve at the ripple frequency you actually have. The toy below is a DC solve and does **not** model PSRR.

**A real Zener's output slopes with load.** onsemi's *Zener Theory and Design Considerations* (HBD854/D) defines the zener impedance Z_Z as the incremental ΔV / ΔI on the breakdown plateau, and its shunt-regulator equation has the load term −(R_S R_Z / (R_S + R_Z)) ΔI_L (以文档为准). With R_Z > 0 the output therefore falls in proportion to the load current: load regulation is a **slope**, not the ideal flat step of the r_z = 0 model used below. No datasheet Z_Z value is quoted here, and the toy keeps r_z = 0.

This section is **not** the ESD/TVS clamp, **not** the bridge-rectifier ripple already on this page, and **not** the one-line "use an LDO" in the brownout note, and **not** a Wheatstone bridge.

<Formula label="Shunt current">I_Z = (V_in − V_Z) / R − I_L</Formula>

<Formula label="LDO loss">P_LOSS = (V_IN − V_OUT) I_OUT + V_IN I_Q</Formula>

**Worked example — a labelled hand calculation, also printed by the toy.** At V_in = 12.0 V, R = 100 Ω, V_Z = 5.0 V and I_L = 0.020 A: I_R = (V_in − V_Z) / R = 0.0700000000 A, I_Z = I_R − I_L = 0.0500000000 A, P_Z = V_Z × I_Z = 0.2500000000 W. For the small resistor R = 22 Ω sized to feed I_L = 0.200 A: I_R = 0.3181818182 A, I_Z = 0.1181818182 A, P_Z = 0.5909090909 W, which is not in budget against P_THERMAL = 0.50 W because the test is the strict inequality P < P_THERMAL (thermal_ok = 0). The same R at I_L = 0.010 A gives I_Z = 0.3081818182 A and P_Z = 1.5409090909 W (thermal_ok = 0).

**Headroom boundary.** In regulation if and only if V_in − V_set ≥ HEADROOM (equality allowed: the pass element is then exactly at the declared drop, not below it). The toy prints the boundary: V_in = 5.4000000000 V gives in_reg = 1; V_in = 5.3990000000 V gives in_reg = 0.

**No integration step here.** The toy is a DC solve: there is no time stepping, so no step-size stability criterion applies (the independent tests check that the script has no time step).

### Fair setup

`zener_shunt_ldo_headroom_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** a stiff Zener (dynamic resistance zero) with a series R and a current-sink load — in this r_z = 0 model the Zener is counted in regulation only when I_Z > 0, which is not a datasheet knee current I_Z,min; an LDO that holds V_set when V_in − V_set is at least HEADROOM = 0.40 V and otherwise falls to V_in − HEADROOM; an uncapped arm that uses only a 0.05 Ω residual source resistance as a stand-in for a missing series resistor. #!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Zener shunt regulator vs LDO headroom.

Synthetic DC model, stdlib only.  Not a TI/NXP datasheet part, not a TVS
clamp, not a bridge rectifier, and not a Wheatstone bridge.
Real regulator bench: 本页未运行.  Wikipedia statements 以原文为准.

A Zener used as a shunt regulator is reverse-biased in parallel with the
load; a series resistor limits current, and once the voltage reaches the
Zener voltage the diode's low impedance holds the node near that value.
An LDO holds Vout while Vin - Vset stays at or above a declared headroom
(the remaining drop of a pass element that can be driven into saturation).
Power in the LDO is (Vin - Vout)*Iout + Vin*Iq.  Headroom and Vz are
declared toy parameters: the LDO article we read does not quote a dropout
in millivolts, so none is invented here.

Common random numbers: one shared Vin and one load per regime and seed.
Arms, shared Vin and load (per regime and seed):
  A_zener_shunt  : stiff Zener (rz = 0) plus series R
  B_ldo          : holds Vset when Vin - Vset >= HEADROOM, else Vin - HEADROOM
  C_uncapped     : same Zener but with only a tiny residual source R
                   (stand-in for a missing series resistor)
The arms differ only in the regulator.  Each seed also draws an independent
Zener and LDO reference tolerance.  No integration step: DC solve, so no
step-size stability criterion applies.

Regimes were written before the first run:
  in_regulation   : Vin well above Vz / headroom, moderate load
                    — A vs B near-tie on Vout
  below_headroom  : Vin - Vset < HEADROOM (and Vin close to / below Vz)
                    — B and A consistent lose (out of regulation); keep
  thermal_small_R : series R sized for a heavy load; Zener dissipation
                    compared with a declared thermal cap (in budget iff
                    P < P_THERMAL, a strict inequality matching
                    I_D * V_B < P_max); keep the loss against the cap
"""
import math
import random

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9

# Declared model parameters, written before the first run.  Not datasheet mV.
VZ_NOM = 5.0          # V
VSET_NOM = 5.0        # V
HEADROOM = 0.40       # V, toy headroom; not a quoted dropout
IQ = 0.002            # A, LDO quiescent (toy)
R_SERIES = 100.0      # ohm, nominal series R
R_HEAVY = 22.0        # ohm, sized so a 200 mA load still leaves IZ > 0
R_UNCAPPED = 0.05     # ohm, stand-in for a missing series resistor
TOL = 0.05            # V, +/- reference / Vz tolerance, independent per device
VIN_NOM = 12.0        # V, in-regulation and thermal regimes
VIN_LOW = 5.15        # V, 0.15 V above Vset, below HEADROOM 0.40 V
IL_MOD = 0.020        # A, moderate load
IL_HEAVY = 0.200      # A, heavy load the small R was sized for
IL_LIGHT = 0.010      # A, light load on the same small R (printed too)
VIN_SIGMA = 0.10      # V, shared Vin noise
IL_SIGMA = 0.002      # A, shared load noise
P_THERMAL = 0.50      # W, toy cap; in budget iff P < P_THERMAL (strict)

REGIMES = {
    "in_regulation":  {"vin": VIN_NOM, "il": IL_MOD, "r": R_SERIES},
    "below_headroom": {"vin": VIN_LOW, "il": IL_MOD, "r": R_SERIES},
    "thermal_small_R": {"vin": VIN_NOM, "il": IL_HEAVY, "r": R_HEAVY},
}
ARMS = ("A_zener_shunt", "B_ldo", "C_uncapped")


def shared_draw(regime, seed):
    """Common random numbers: one Vin and one load, used by every arm."""
    rng = random.Random(f"{regime}/{seed}")
    cfg = REGIMES[regime]
    return {
        "vin": cfg["vin"] + rng.gauss(0.0, VIN_SIGMA),
        "il": max(0.0, cfg["il"] + rng.gauss(0.0, IL_SIGMA)),
        "r": cfg["r"],
        "vz": VZ_NOM + rng.uniform(-TOL, TOL),
        "vset": VSET_NOM + rng.uniform(-TOL, TOL),
    }


def zener_solve(vin, il, r, vz):
    """Stiff Zener (rz = 0) with series R and a current-sink load."""
    if r <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError("r")
    # If the node can reach vz, Vout = vz and IZ = (vin - vz)/r - il.
    iz = (vin - vz) / r - il
    if iz >= 0.0 and vin >= vz:
        vout = vz
    else:
        vout = max(0.0, vin - il * r)
        iz = 0.0
    p = vz * iz if iz > 0.0 else 0.0
    in_reg = 1.0 if iz > 0.0 else 0.0
    thermal_ok = 1.0 if p < P_THERMAL else 0.0
    return {"vout": vout, "iz": iz, "p": p, "in_reg": in_reg, "thermal_ok": thermal_ok}


def ldo_solve(vin, il, vset):
    """Holds vset iff vin - vset >= HEADROOM (equality allowed)."""
    in_reg = 1.0 if (vin - vset) >= HEADROOM else 0.0
    if in_reg:
        vout = vset
    else:
        vout = max(0.0, vin - HEADROOM)
    iout = il
    p = (vin - vout) * iout + vin * IQ
    return {"vout": vout, "iz": 0.0, "p": p, "in_reg": in_reg, "thermal_ok": 1.0}


def measure(d, arm):
    if arm == "B_ldo":
        return ldo_solve(d["vin"], d["il"], d["vset"])
    r = R_UNCAPPED if arm == "C_uncapped" else d["r"]
    return zener_solve(d["vin"], d["il"], r, d["vz"])


def mean_sd(xs):
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x):
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-4:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def fmt_pair(m, s):
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}"


def verdict(xa, xb):
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


def seed_row(regime, seed, arm):
    return measure(shared_draw(regime, seed), arm)


def main():
    print("Synthetic Zener-shunt vs LDO-headroom toy; shared Vin/load; not a datasheet part")
    print(f"seeds={len(SEEDS)} VZ_NOM={VZ_NOM:.1f} V VSET_NOM={VSET_NOM:.1f} V "
          f"HEADROOM={HEADROOM:.2f} V (toy, not a datasheet dropout) IQ={IQ:.3f} A "
          f"TOL=+/-{TOL:.2f} V P_THERMAL={P_THERMAL:.2f} W")
    print("No integration step: DC solve, no time-step criterion")
    print("Regimes, HEADROOM and P_THERMAL were written before the first run")
    print("headroom_rule: in regulation iff Vin - Vset >= HEADROOM (equality allowed)")
    print(f"headroom_boundary: Vin={VSET_NOM + HEADROOM:.10f} V in_reg="
          f"{1.0 if (VSET_NOM + HEADROOM - VSET_NOM) >= HEADROOM else 0.0:.0f}; "
          f"Vin={VSET_NOM + HEADROOM - 0.001:.10f} V in_reg="
          f"{1.0 if ((VSET_NOM + HEADROOM - 0.001) - VSET_NOM) >= HEADROOM else 0.0:.0f}")
    spare = VIN_LOW - VSET_NOM
    print(f"below_headroom_spare: Vin-Vset={spare:.10f} V ({VIN_LOW:.2f}-{VSET_NOM:.1f}), "
          f"below HEADROOM {HEADROOM:.2f} V")
    print("zener_in_reg_rule: in regulation iff I_Z>0 (rz=0 model; not a datasheet knee current I_Z,min)")
    # Hand calc, also printed from the same closed form (rz = 0, nominal values)
    ir = (VIN_NOM - VZ_NOM) / R_SERIES
    iz = ir - IL_MOD
    pz = VZ_NOM * iz
    print(f"hand_calc Vin={VIN_NOM:.1f} V R={R_SERIES:.0f} ohm VZ={VZ_NOM:.1f} V IL={IL_MOD:.3f} A: "
          f"IR=(Vin-VZ)/R={ir:.10f} A; IZ=IR-IL={iz:.10f} A; PZ=VZ*IZ={pz:.10f} W")
    ir_h = (VIN_NOM - VZ_NOM) / R_HEAVY
    iz_h = ir_h - IL_HEAVY
    pz_h = VZ_NOM * iz_h
    print(f"hand_calc thermal heavy: R={R_HEAVY:.0f} ohm IL={IL_HEAVY:.3f} A: "
          f"IR={ir_h:.10f} A IZ={iz_h:.10f} A PZ={pz_h:.10f} W "
          f"P_THERMAL={P_THERMAL:.2f} W thermal_ok={1 if pz_h < P_THERMAL else 0} "
          f"(strict P < P_THERMAL)")
    iz_l = ir_h - IL_LIGHT
    pz_l = VZ_NOM * iz_l
    print(f"hand_calc thermal light on same R: IL={IL_LIGHT:.3f} A: "
          f"IZ={iz_l:.10f} A PZ={pz_l:.10f} W thermal_ok={1 if pz_l < P_THERMAL else 0}")
    print("regime arm vout_V err_vs_5V p_W in_reg thermal_ok  (mean+-sd over seeds)")
    res = {}
    for regime in REGIMES:
        res[regime] = {a: {"vout": [], "err": [], "p": [], "in_reg": [], "thermal_ok": []} for a in ARMS}
        for seed in SEEDS:
            d = shared_draw(regime, seed)
            for a in ARMS:
                m = measure(d, a)
                res[regime][a]["vout"].append(m["vout"])
                res[regime][a]["err"].append(m["vout"] - VSET_NOM)
                res[regime][a]["p"].append(m["p"])
                res[regime][a]["in_reg"].append(m["in_reg"])
                res[regime][a]["thermal_ok"].append(m["thermal_ok"])
        cfg = REGIMES[regime]
        print(f"{regime} (Vin_nom={cfg['vin']:.2f} V, IL_nom={cfg['il']:.3f} A, R={cfg['r']:.2f} ohm)")
        for a in ARMS:
            row = res[regime][a]
            print(f"  {a} vout {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(row['vout']))} "
                  f"err {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(row['err']))} "
                  f"p {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(row['p']))} "
                  f"in_reg {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(row['in_reg']))} "
                  f"thermal_ok {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(row['thermal_ok']))}")
        print(f"  paired A-B vout: {verdict(res[regime]['A_zener_shunt']['vout'], res[regime]['B_ldo']['vout'])}")
        print(f"  paired A-B |err|: {verdict([abs(x) for x in res[regime]['A_zener_shunt']['err']], [abs(x) for x in res[regime]['B_ldo']['err']])}")
        print(f"  paired B in_reg vs 1: {verdict(res[regime]['B_ldo']['in_reg'], [1.0]*len(SEEDS))}")
        print(f"  paired A in_reg vs 1: {verdict(res[regime]['A_zener_shunt']['in_reg'], [1.0]*len(SEEDS))}")
        print(f"  paired C-A p: {verdict(res[regime]['C_uncapped']['p'], res[regime]['A_zener_shunt']['p'])}")
        print(f"  paired A thermal_ok vs 1: {verdict(res[regime]['A_zener_shunt']['thermal_ok'], [1.0]*len(SEEDS))}")
    # Light load on the same small R, same seeds as thermal_small_R (shared Vin, new IL)
    print("thermal_small_R light-load companion (same Vin draws, IL_nom=0.010 A, same R, not a deleted regime):")
    light_p, light_ok = [], []
    for seed in SEEDS:
        d = shared_draw("thermal_small_R", seed)
        d = dict(d)
        rng = random.Random(f"thermal_small_R/{seed}/light")
        d["il"] = max(0.0, IL_LIGHT + rng.gauss(0.0, IL_SIGMA))
        m = zener_solve(d["vin"], d["il"], R_HEAVY, d["vz"])
        light_p.append(m["p"])
        light_ok.append(m["thermal_ok"])
    print(f"  A_zener_shunt light p {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(light_p))} thermal_ok {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(light_ok))}")
    print(f"  paired light thermal_ok vs 1: {verdict(light_ok, [1.0]*len(SEEDS))}")
    # Alternatives on the same seeds
    print("alternative HEADROOM=0.20 V on below_headroom (same seeds):")
    alt_in = []
    base_in = []
    for seed in SEEDS:
        d = shared_draw("below_headroom", seed)
        base_in.append(ldo_solve(d["vin"], d["il"], d["vset"])["in_reg"])
        alt_in.append(1.0 if (d["vin"] - d["vset"]) >= 0.20 else 0.0)
    print(f"  B_ldo in_reg@0.20 {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(alt_in))} (default {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(base_in))})")
    print(f"  paired in_reg@0.20 vs default: {verdict(alt_in, base_in)}")
    print("alternative P_THERMAL=0.80 W on thermal_small_R (same seeds):")
    alt_ok = []
    base_ok = res["thermal_small_R"]["A_zener_shunt"]["thermal_ok"]
    alt_p = res["thermal_small_R"]["A_zener_shunt"]["p"]
    for p in alt_p:
        alt_ok.append(1.0 if p < 0.80 else 0.0)
    print(f"  A thermal_ok@0.80 {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(alt_ok))} (default {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(base_ok))})")
    print(f"  paired thermal_ok@0.80 vs default: {verdict(alt_ok, base_ok)}")

### Read the regimes

How the labels are used on this page, in this order: **tie: identical on every seed** only when the paired per-seed difference is exactly zero on every seed; **numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie** when |paired mean| and its sd are both below 1e-9 (no sign count is printed on such a line); **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** when |paired mean| ≤ sd; otherwise a **consistent** gap (|paired mean| > sd), called **small** if |m| is under 20 % of the smaller arm mean and **clear** otherwise; the 20 % rule only chooses between those two words. Every consistent line carries a same-sign count over the 5 seeds, and any seed with the opposite sign is reported. Gaps are computed from unrounded values.

## Peukert's law: nameplate capacity versus discharge rate

<Resource id="wiki-peukert" />

The battery section above reads a pack's nameplate mAh and its C rating as a **maximum continuous current**. This section asks a different question: how long does the pack actually last at a given current? A nameplate capacity is a capacity **at a specified discharge rate**. Wikipedia's *Peukert's law* (body read, rev 1345423657; 以原文为准) states it as an approximate law for **rechargeable lead–acid** batteries, presented by Wilhelm Peukert in 1897: as the discharge rate rises, the available capacity falls. At a known rating (capacity C at an H-hour rate) the time to discharge at current I is t = H (C / (I H))^k. If the exponent k were 1, the delivered capacity would **not** depend on current; for a real lead–acid battery k is greater than 1, typically **between 1.1 and 1.3** (the page also gives separate ranges for AGM, gel and flooded types; 以原文为准). At the rated current I = C / H the formula gives t = H, which is exactly the nameplate clock C / I.

The same page lists the limits, and they matter as much as the formula: **temperature is not in the equation**; the exponent **grows with age**; at **low** current the **self-discharge** has to be taken into account (taken literally, the law would let total discharge keep rising as the current falls); and at **very high** currents practical batteries give **less** capacity than a fixed exponent predicts. The page is about lead–acid cells. Nothing here says that k ≈ 1.3 describes a LiPo, and the toy below does not use a LiPo exponent from any source. A 2006 critical review that the page cites is paywalled and was **not** read, so it is **not** cited here.

This section is **not** the C-rate (maximum current) note above, and it is **not** the coulomb-counting SoC / temperature-derate toy in the power lesson.

<Formula label="Peukert at a rating">t = H × (C / (I × H))^k,   delivered charge = I × t</Formula>

**Worked example — a labelled hand calculation, also printed by the toy.** This is Wikipedia's own example: a battery rated 100 Ah at the 20-hour rate with k = 1.2, discharged at 10 A, lasts t = 20 × (100 / (10 × 20))^1.2 = 8.7055 h and delivers 87.0551 Ah, against a nameplate clock of 10.0000 h (the page rounds this to about 8.7 h and 87 Ah; 以原文为准). At the rated current I = C / H = 5.0000 A the ratio C / (I H) is 1.0000, so t = H = 20.0000 h for **any** k.

**Boundary.** The toy's high-current loss and arm D's cap apply **if and only if** I > I_KNEE (**strict**): at I = I_KNEE the printed D/B ratio is 1.0000, just above it the ratio is 0.8000.

**No integration step here.** The toy is a closed-form discharge time: there is no time stepping, so no step-size criterion applies.

### Fair setup

`peukert_rate_capacity_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** constant-current discharge; no temperature, no ageing; a declared **generator** (the synthetic pack every arm is scored against) uses the Peukert form with its own exponent k_true, drawn uniformly in [1.15, 1.25] for the lead–acid pack and in [1.00, 1.03] for a "k ≈ 1" pack whose capacity barely depends on current; above I_KNEE = 50 A the generator loses a further factor (I_KNEE / I)^GAMMA with GAMMA = 0.25; in the `low` cell only, it adds a self-discharge current I_SD = 0.05 A. These are **declared toy parameters**, not datasheet values: Wikipedia gives the high-current and self-discharge effects only in words. The 100 Ah / 20 h rating and K_DECL = 1.2 are Wikipedia's worked-example values (以原文为准).

| Arm | Prediction rule |

  t = H * (C / (I * H)) ** k

The page also lists limits: temperature is not in the equation, k grows

Common random numbers: for each (regime, seed) one "true" exponent k_true is
drawn once and shared by every arm; it only feeds the declared generator
(the synthetic pack every arm is scored against).  Arms:
  A_nameplate : t = C / I (the nameplate / Coulomb clock)
  B_peukert   : t = H * (C / (I*H)) ** K_DECL, fixed lead-acid K_DECL

Declared generator (synthetic pack, toy assumptions, not a measured cell):
  lead-acid pack : k_true uniform in [K_LA_LO, K_LA_HI]
  k~1 pack       : k_true uniform in [K_ONE_LO, K_ONE_HI] (a toy stand-in for

  t_true = H * (C / (I_eff * H)) ** k_true, times (I_KNEE / I) ** GAMMA only
  when I > I_KNEE (strict); I_eff = I + I_SD only in the "low" cell.

Regimes and parameters were written before the first run:
  rated      : I = C/H on the lead-acid pack - A vs B tie (t = H exactly)
  high       : I = 4 C/H, lead-acid - A consistent over-predicts vs B

import math
import random
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal, getcontext

getcontext().rounding = ROUND_HALF_UP

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9

# Declared parameters, written before the first run.
C_RATED = 100.0   # Ah at the H-hour rate (Wikipedia's worked-example rating)
H_RATED = 20.0    # h
K_DECL = 1.2      # lead-acid exponent used by B (Wikipedia's worked example; inside 1.1-1.3)
K_LA_LO, K_LA_HI = 1.15, 1.25    # generator lead-acid k_true (toy)
K_ONE_LO, K_ONE_HI = 1.00, 1.03  # generator k~1 pack k_true (toy, not a LiPo datasheet)
I_KNEE = 50.0     # A, generator / D high-current threshold (toy)
GAMMA = 0.25      # generator extra loss exponent above I_KNEE (toy)
D_DERATE = 0.80   # D's fixed cap above I_KNEE (toy rule, not a documented value)
I_SD = 0.05       # A, self-discharge stand-in, "low" cell only (toy)

I_RATED = C_RATED / H_RATED

    "high": {"i": 4.0 * I_RATED, "pack": "lead_acid", "sd": False},
    "very_high": {"i": 20.0 * I_RATED, "pack": "lead_acid", "sd": False},
    "low": {"i": 0.1 * I_RATED, "pack": "lead_acid", "sd": True},
    "misapplied": {"i": 4.0 * I_RATED, "pack": "k_one", "sd": False},
}
ARMS = ("A_nameplate", "B_peukert", "C_k1", "D_cap")


def peukert_t(i, k, c=C_RATED, h=H_RATED):
    return h * (c / (i * h)) ** k


def shared_k(regime, seed):
    """Common random numbers: one k_true per (regime, seed), shared by all arms."""
    rng = random.Random(f"{regime}/{seed}")
    lo, hi = (K_LA_LO, K_LA_HI) if REGIMES[regime]["pack"] == "lead_acid" else (K_ONE_LO, K_ONE_HI)
    return rng.uniform(lo, hi)


def true_t(regime, seed, gamma=GAMMA, i_sd=I_SD):
    cfg = REGIMES[regime]
    i = cfg["i"]
    i_eff = i + (i_sd if cfg["sd"] else 0.0)
    t = peukert_t(i_eff, shared_k(regime, seed))
    if i > I_KNEE:
        t *= (I_KNEE / i) ** gamma
    return t


def predict(arm, i, k_decl=K_DECL, derate=D_DERATE):
    if arm == "A_nameplate":
        return C_RATED / i
    if arm == "B_peukert":
        return peukert_t(i, k_decl)
    if arm == "C_k1":
        return peukert_t(i, 1.0)
    t = peukert_t(i, k_decl)
    return t * derate if i > I_KNEE else t


def run_arm(arm, regime, seed, **kw):
    gen = {k: kw.pop(k) for k in ("gamma", "i_sd") if k in kw}
    i = REGIMES[regime]["i"]
    t = predict(arm, i, **kw)
    tt = true_t(regime, seed, **gen)
    return {"t": t, "q": i * t, "err": t - tt, "abs_err": abs(t - tt), "t_true": tt}


def mean_sd(xs):
    if all(x == xs[0] for x in xs):
        return xs[0], 0.0  # identical values: no floating residue in mean or sd
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x):
    """Half-up rounding on the shortest decimal repr; tiny non-zero values in scientific form."""
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-4:
        return format(Decimal(repr(x)), ".3e")
    s = format(Decimal(repr(x)), ".4f")
    return "0.0000" if s == "-0.0000" else s


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def fmt_pair(m, s):
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}"


def verdict(xa, xb):
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


METRICS = ("t", "q", "err", "abs_err")

    return {arm: [run_arm(arm, regime, s, **dict(kw)) for s in SEEDS] for arm in ARMS}

    return [r[key] for r in rows]

        under = sum(1 for r in res[arm] if r["err"] < 0.0)

### Read the regimes

How the labels are used on this page, in this order: **tie: identical on every seed** only when the paired per-seed difference is exactly zero on every seed; **numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie** when `|paired mean|` and its sd are both below 1e-9 (no sign count is printed on such a line); **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** when `|paired mean| ≤ sd`; otherwise a **consistent** gap (`|paired mean| > sd`), called **small** if `|m|` is under 20 % of the smaller arm mean and **clear** otherwise. Every consistent line carries a same-sign count over the 5 seeds. Gaps are computed from unrounded values.
