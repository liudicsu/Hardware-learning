## Defining voltage, current and resistance

Electrical quantities are not directly visible, but they can be defined by measurable quantities. The three definitions below support every calculation in this lesson.

- **Voltage** is the electric potential difference between two points, measured in volts (V). A 9 V battery maintains about 9 V between its terminals. Voltage is always between two points.
- **Current** is the rate of charge flow: charge passing a point each second. Its unit is the ampere (A), and 1 A means 1 coulomb per second. Small circuits use milliamps (mA).
- **Resistance** is the ratio of the voltage across a component to the current through it, measured in ohms (Ω). A resistance of 1 Ω carries 1 A when 1 V is across it.

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit | Typical in a small robot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Voltage | V | volt (V) | 3.3 V, 5 V, 12 V |
| Current | I | ampere (A) | 5 mA to 2 A |
| Resistance | R | ohm (Ω) | 100 Ω to 1 MΩ |

| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| m (milli) | 10^-3 | 20 mA = 0.020 A |
| k (kilo) | 10^3 | 4.7 kΩ = 4700 Ω |
| M (mega) | 10^6 | 1 MΩ = 1,000,000 Ω |

Ground (GND) is the node the circuit defines as 0 V. Every voltage in this lesson, Vout included, is measured from GND: the meter's black (COM) lead goes on GND, the red lead on the node of interest.

<Term id="current">Current</Term> only flows around a **closed loop**. Break the path and it stops, though <Term id="voltage">voltage</Term> remains across the break; a battery on a table has voltage but no current. We draw **conventional current** leaving the positive terminal and returning to the negative one; electrons drift the other way. Schematics use it too.

These definitions do not depend on a mechanical model: electrons already fill the wire and the field establishes almost instantly, a <Term id="resistance">resistance</Term> converts electrical energy to heat, and voltage can exist with no current. Use the definitions and the equations directly.

## Ohm's law

One simple law ties them together:

<Formula label="Ohm's law">V = I × R</Formula>

With volts and ohms, current comes out in amps. Rearranged, `I = V / R` and `R = V / I`: know any two quantities and you can find the third. For fixed <Term id="resistance">resistance</Term>, doubling the voltage doubles the current; for fixed voltage, doubling the resistance halves it. Large resistance is nearly an open circuit, small resistance nearly a short.

**Worked example.** A 9 V battery is connected across a 1 kΩ resistor. How much current flows?

1. Convert first: 1 kΩ = 1000 Ω.
2. Apply the law: I = 9 V / 1000 Ω.
3. Divide: I = 0.009 A.
4. Convert: 0.009 A × 1000 = 9 mA.

Reverse check: 0.009 × 1000 = 9 V. Make that a bench habit.

Predict before reading the numbers. Both resistors see 5 V, so the 100 Ω one carries 5 / 100 = 0.05 A = 50 mA, and the 1 kΩ one carries 5 / 1000 = 0.005 A = 5 mA: ten times the resistance, one tenth the current. Hover to check, then double-click, change the value, and predict again.

## Power: a resistor converts electrical energy to heat

Current through resistance does work, which in a resistor becomes heat. <Term id="power">Power</Term> is energy per second, in watts (W):

<Formula label="Power in a resistor">P = V × I = I² × R = V² / R</Formula>

Substitute V = I × R into P = V × I to get I² × R, or substitute I = V / R to get V² / R. Use the form you know.

Every resistor has a **power rating**, the maximum it can dissipate safely. Kits commonly contain 1/4 W through-hole parts; 1/8, 1/2, 1 and 2 W also exist. The rating is a limit, not a target.

**Worked example.** A 100 Ω resistor has 5 V across it. The current is I = 5 / 100 = 0.05 A = 50 mA, so P = 5 × 0.05 = 0.25 W. The V² / R form agrees: 25 / 100 = 0.25 W.

That is exactly the 1/4 W rating. At 100 % it runs very hot and ages quickly. Use at most about 50 %, so fit a 1/2 W part here: 0.25 / 0.5 = 50 %. In a closed box, go one size larger.

## Series and parallel

**Series** means one path. The same current flows through every part, the voltages add, and the resistances add:

R(total) = R1 + R2 + R3 + ...

For example, 220 Ω + 330 Ω = 550 Ω; on 5 V the current is 5 / 550 = 0.00909 A ≈ 9.1 mA.

**Parallel** means every component shares two nodes. All see the same voltage and the current splits. Resistances combine as reciprocals:

1 / R(total) = 1 / R1 + 1 / R2 + 1 / R3 + ...

For two resistors, R = R1 × R2 / (R1 + R2). Two equal resistors give half, so 10 kΩ in parallel with 10 kΩ is 5 kΩ. Check: 1 / R = 1 / 10000 + 1 / 10000 = 2 / 10000, so R = 5000 Ω.

Sanity checks: a parallel result is always smaller than the smallest resistor, and a series result always larger than the largest. If not, you inverted the wrong thing.

## The voltage divider

A <Term id="voltage-divider">voltage divider</Term> uses two resistors to scale a voltage down. R1 goes from the input to the output node, R2 from the output to <Term id="gnd">ground</Term>, and the output is across R2:

<Formula label="Voltage divider">Vout = Vin × R2 / (R1 + R2)</Formula>

It follows from series rules in three lines, since both resistors carry the same current:

1. I = Vin / (R1 + R2)
2. Vout = I × R2, because the output is the voltage across R2
3. Substitute I: Vout = Vin × R2 / (R1 + R2)

Check the extremes: R2 = 0 Ω gives Vout = 0 V, a very large R2 passes almost all of Vin, and equal resistors give half. The formula agrees.

**The practice task.** Take Vin = 5 V, R1 = 10 kΩ on top, R2 = 4.7 kΩ to ground. Compute by hand first.

1. Add them: R1 + R2 = 10 kΩ + 4.7 kΩ = 14.7 kΩ.
2. Find the current: I = 5 V / 14,700 Ω = 0.0003401 A.
3. Multiply by R2: Vout = 0.0003401 A × 4700 Ω = 1.5986 V.
4. Round: Vout ≈ 1.6 V.

Dividers are everywhere in robotics. A 12 V battery often feeds an <Term id="adc">ADC</Term> that accepts only 5 V or 3.3 V, so a divider scales it down, and a 5 V sensor signal may need shifting to 3.3 V. A potentiometer is a divider with a moving wiper: its ends sit across the supply, and the wiper voltage reports shaft angle — the basis of many joysticks and position sensors.

**Worked example: battery voltage into an ADC.**

```text
Uno A0, ADC reference 5 V. A 2S LiPo is 8.4 V when full. R1 = R2 = 10 kΩ:
Vout = 8.4 × 10 / (10 + 10) = 4.20 V, below 5 V.
The divider draws 8.4 V / 20 kΩ = 0.42 mA all the time.
analogRead returns about 4.20 / 5 × 1023 = 859.

ESP32-S3: a 3S LiPo is 12.6 V when full. R1 = 100 kΩ, R2 = 27 kΩ:
Vout = 12.6 × 27 / 127 = 2.68 V; the divider draws 12.6 V / 127 kΩ = 99 µA.
```

The Uno ADC formula V = raw × 5 / 1023 is used in Month 2, Arduino I/O. The usable input range of the ESP32-S3 ADC depends on the attenuation setting, so check it in [Espressif's ADC documentation](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/adc_oneshot.html) before choosing R2.

Larger resistors waste less battery current; the next section shows why the ADC input must still not be loaded heavily.

## Loading: a divider is not a power supply

The formula assumes nothing is connected to the output. A real load draws current and sits in parallel with R2, making the effective bottom resistance smaller than R2. A smaller bottom resistance takes a smaller share of the input, so Vout drops.

Take the previous divider and add a 10 kΩ load across R2. The bottom becomes 4.7 kΩ in parallel with 10 kΩ: (4.7 × 10) / (4.7 + 10) = 47 / 14.7 = 3.197 kΩ ≈ 3.2 kΩ. Recalculate: Vout = 5 × 3.2 / 13.2 = 1.212 V. Instead of 1.6 V you get about 1.2 V, a 25 % drop. A motor would pull far more and collapse the output completely.

<Falstad title="Loaded divider: a switch adds a 10 kΩ load" circuit="loadedDivider" height={420} />

The same 5 V, 10 kΩ over 4.7 kΩ divider as above. With switch S open the probe reads 1.60 V; click S to connect the 10 kΩ load RL and the probe drops to about 1.21 V, matching the hand calculation of 1.212 V. The simulator also shows the current through R1 now splitting: about 0.26 mA through R2 and 0.12 mA through RL — KCL, which the next section explains. Try it: double-click RL and change it to 100 kΩ, but predict first. It should drop much less, the 10× rule.

As a rule of thumb, keep the load at least 10× R2; even then the output sags slightly. For a stiff voltage, use a lower-resistance divider, or better, a regulator.

<Callout type="warn" title="Never power a load from a divider">
A divider is a **signal**, not a supply. It cannot deliver current without its output collapsing. Do not power a motor, relay, LED strip or microcontroller from one — use a linear regulator or a buck converter. If a divider feeds an ADC, check the loaded voltage, not just the open-circuit value.
</Callout>

## Kirchhoff in brief

**Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL)** says the signed voltages around any closed loop add to zero: every rise is balanced by drops. Around the divider loop, Vin − I × R1 − I × R2 = 0, which rearranges to I = Vin / (R1 + R2). That is where the divider formula comes from.

**Kirchhoff's current law (KCL)** says current into a node equals current out; charge does not pile up. At the divider output, the current arriving through R1 splits between R2 and any load: I(R1) = I(R2) + I(load). Add a load and it takes a share, which is the loading effect in one sentence.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing mA and A, or kΩ and Ω.** 5 V across 220 Ω is 0.0227 A = 22.7 mA, not 22.7 A. Write units every step and convert prefixes before dividing.
- **Swapping R1 and R2.** R1 is the top resistor from Vin to the output; R2 is the bottom one to ground. A larger R2 gives a higher Vout; check R2 = 0 and R2 huge to see whether the formula is the right way up.
- **Forgetting the power rating.** A resistance value does not tell you whether a resistor survives; calculate P and choose at least twice the dissipation.
- **Using a divider as a supply.** Fine for an ADC input or a reference, not for anything drawing real current.
- **Blaming the simulator.** Check for a missing ground, an open switch, a wrong value or a unit error before doubting the tool; most big discrepancies come from the wiring.

## Thévenin and Norton equivalents

The loading section above recomputed `R2 ∥ RL` by hand for one load. When the load changes (a sensor, an ADC input, a test resistor), it is faster to collapse everything behind the output into **two numbers** once. This section is that step. It is **not** the Wheatstone bridge from the next lesson, and it is **not** just the "read the open-circuit voltage" tip from the multimeter lesson.

<Resource id="wiki-thevenin" />

**Sources actually opened (HTML body read).**

- **Wikipedia — Thévenin's theorem:** any linear network of voltage sources, current sources and resistances can be replaced at two terminals by one voltage source `Vth` in series with one resistance `Rth`. `Vth` is the open-circuit voltage at the terminals; `Rth` is the resistance seen with ideal voltage sources replaced by shorts and current sources by opens, or equivalently `Vth` divided by the short-circuit current. Numbers 以原文为准.
- **Wikipedia — Norton's theorem:** the dual form, a current source `In` (the short-circuit current) in parallel with `Rn`; `Rn = Rth`, `Vth = In × Rn`. Its worked example (a 15 V network that becomes 3.75 mA in parallel with 2 kΩ) 以原文为准.
- **Wikipedia — Maximum power transfer theorem:** load power is maximum when the load resistance equals the source resistance, but efficiency `η = RL / (RL + RS)` is then only 50 %; maximum efficiency needs the source resistance as close to zero as possible. 以原文为准.

### Two numbers for this lesson's divider

Take the practice divider again: 5 V, R1 = 10 kΩ on top, R2 = 4.7 kΩ to ground.

1. Open circuit (no load): `Vth = 5 × 4.7 / 14.7 = 1.5986 V`, the familiar 1.6 V.
2. Zero the source (replace the 5 V source by a wire): seen from the output, R1 and R2 both go to ground, so `Rth = 10 kΩ ∥ 4.7 kΩ = 3197 Ω ≈ 3.2 kΩ`.

<Formula label="Thévenin loaded voltage">V_L = Vth × RL / (Rth + RL)</Formula>

With RL = 10 kΩ: `1.5986 × 10 / 13.197 = 1.2113 V`, the same ≈ 1.21 V the loading section found with `R2 ∥ RL`. The Norton twin is `In = Vth / Rth = 0.5000 mA` in parallel with `Rn = 3197 Ω`, and `In × (Rn ∥ RL)` gives the same 1.2113 V. The formula also explains the 10× rule: reading only `Vth` is wrong by the fraction `Rth / (Rth + RL)`. With RL = 47 kΩ (10 × R2) that is `3.197 / 50.2 ≈ 6.4 %`; because `Rth` is always smaller than R2, "10 × R2" is a safe stand-in, but the ratio that actually sets the error is RL against **Rth**.

**Maximum power transfer:** for a fixed `Rth`, the load power `V_L² / RL` is largest at `RL = Rth` (here `Vth² / (4·Rth) ≈ 0.20 mW` at 3.2 kΩ), but the efficiency `RL / (RL + Rth)` is then only 50 % — half the power is lost in the source resistance — so power supplies and battery-powered systems do not aim for impedance matching; they keep the source resistance far below the load (Wikipedia — Maximum power transfer theorem, 以原文为准).

### Measuring Rth on the bench

- **Two readings with a known test resistor.** Read `Voc`, then connect a known `Rtest` and read `Vtest`. Since `Vtest = Voc × Rtest / (Rth + Rtest)`, `Rth = Rtest × (Voc / Vtest − 1)`.
- **Short-circuit current.** `Rth = Voc / Isc`. An ammeter is not a perfect short: its burden resistance sits in series, so it reads `Voc / (Rth + R_burden)`. Never short a battery or a supply that can deliver large current just to measure this.

### Fair setup

`thevenin_loaded_divider_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** linear resistors; an ideal 5 V source with no source resistance and no drift; every resistor (R1, R2, RL, Rtest) is a 1 % part drawn uniformly; meter readings have Gaussian noise of 1 mV (voltage) or 1 µA (current) and are otherwise ideal (no input loading); the ammeter adds a **synthetic 100 Ω burden** (an exaggerated stand-in, real burden depends on the meter and range); `Rtest` is used at its nominal value. Truth is an independent KCL solve at the output node with the actual parts.

| Arm | Prediction of the loaded voltage |
|---|---|
| A_voc_only | `V_L = Voc` (ignores Rth) |
| B_thevenin | `Vth = Voc`, `Rth` from the two-reading method, then the formula above |
| C_norton | `In` = short-circuit reading through the 100 Ω burden, `Rn = Voc / In`, `V_L = In × (Rn ∥ RL)` |

**Common random numbers:** for each (seed, board) the actual R1, R2, RL and Rtest and the meter-noise draws are made once and shared by all three arms; all arms use the same `Voc` reading. Five seeds × 32 boards; metric = mean `|V_pred − V_true|` per seed, reported as mean±sd over seeds. The metric scores only the loaded voltage. It is **blind** to the risk of the short-circuit reading (a real supply can be damaged), to the time each method takes, and to the power in the test resistor.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Synthetic Thevenin / Norton equivalent of a loaded divider. Stdlib only.

Network (linear resistors, ideal 5 V source, no source resistance):
  Vs --R1-- out --R2-- GND, load RL from out to GND. Nominal R1 = 10 kOhm,
  R2 = 4.7 kOhm (the lesson's divider), so Vth = 1.5986 V, Rth = R1||R2 = 3197 Ohm.

Truth: KCL at node out with the ACTUAL (toleranced) parts:
  (Vs - V)/R1 = V/R2 + V/RL   ->   V = (Vs/R1) / (1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/RL)

Every arm predicts the loaded voltage from bench measurements of the unloaded
network, then is scored against that truth:
  A_voc_only   V_L = Voc                       (ignores Rth)
  B_thevenin   Vth = Voc; Rth from a second reading with a known 10 kOhm test
               resistor: Rth = Rtest_nominal * (Voc/Vtest - 1);
               V_L = Vth * RL / (Rth + RL)
  C_norton     In = short-circuit current read through an ammeter with a
               100 Ohm burden; Rn = Voc / In; V_L = In * (Rn || RL)

Common random numbers: per (seed, board) the actual R1, R2, RL and Rtest (1 %
parts, uniform) and the meter noise draws z1 (Voc), z2 (Vtest / Isc) are drawn
once and shared by every arm. The meter is otherwise ideal (no input loading).

Regimes (5 seeds x 32 boards): heavy_load RL = 3.3 kOhm (about Rth),
lesson_load RL = 10 kOhm, light_load RL = 10 MOhm (B and C: near-tie / small gap;
A-B: small but consistent gap, not a tie). "Consistent" means only |paired
mean| > paired sd over seeds; the sign count says how many seeds disagree.
No presumed winner. Real DMM / bench Thevenin lab: 本页未运行.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

VS = 5.0  # V, ideal source
R1_NOM = 10_000.0  # Ohm
R2_NOM = 4_700.0  # Ohm
RTEST_NOM = 10_000.0  # Ohm, known test resistor for the two-reading Rth method
TOL = 0.01  # 1 % parts, uniform
V_NOISE = 1e-3  # V, meter reading sd (about one count on a 2 V range)
I_NOISE = 1e-6  # A, meter reading sd (about one count on a 2 mA range)
R_BURDEN = 100.0  # Ohm, ammeter burden in series during the short-circuit reading
SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
BOARDS = 32
ARMS = ("A_voc_only", "B_thevenin", "C_norton")
REGIMES = (("heavy_load", 3_300.0), ("lesson_load", 10_000.0), ("light_load", 10_000_000.0))


def thevenin(vs: float, r1: float, r2: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Open-circuit voltage and resistance seen at `out` with the source zeroed."""
    return vs * r2 / (r1 + r2), r1 * r2 / (r1 + r2)


def truth_kcl(vs: float, r1: float, r2: float, rl: float) -> float:
    """Loaded node voltage from KCL, independent of the equivalent circuits."""
    return (vs / r1) / (1.0 / r1 + 1.0 / r2 + 1.0 / rl)


def parallel(a: float, b: float) -> float:
    return a * b / (a + b)


def shared_board(seed: int, board: int, rl_nom: float) -> dict:
    """Common random numbers: one draw per (seed, board), shared by every arm."""
    rng = random.Random(1_000_003 * seed + 7_919 * board + int(rl_nom) % 100_003)
    tol = lambda nom: nom * (1.0 + rng.uniform(-TOL, TOL))  # noqa: E731
    return {
        "r1": tol(R1_NOM),
        "r2": tol(R2_NOM),
        "rl": tol(rl_nom),
        "rtest": tol(RTEST_NOM),
        "z1": rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0),
        "z2": rng.gauss(0.0, 1.0),
    }


def predict(arm: str, b: dict) -> float:
    vth, rth = thevenin(VS, b["r1"], b["r2"])
    voc = vth + V_NOISE * b["z1"]  # same reading for every arm
    if arm == "A_voc_only":
        return voc
    if arm == "B_thevenin":
        vtest = truth_kcl(VS, b["r1"], b["r2"], b["rtest"]) + V_NOISE * b["z2"]
        rth_hat = RTEST_NOM * (voc / vtest - 1.0)  # uses the NOMINAL test value
        return voc * b["rl"] / (rth_hat + b["rl"])
    if arm == "C_norton":
        isc = vth / (rth + R_BURDEN) + I_NOISE * b["z2"]  # burden lowers the reading
        rn_hat = voc / isc
        return isc * parallel(rn_hat, b["rl"])
    raise ValueError(arm)


def mean_sd(xs: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    var = sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1)
    return mu, math.sqrt(var)


def sign_note(diffs: list[float], m: float) -> str:
    """How many seeds disagree in sign with the paired mean (zeros listed apart)."""
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def run_regime(rl_nom: float) -> dict:
    """Per arm: per-seed mean |V_pred - V_true| over the boards, in mV."""
    out = {arm: [] for arm in ARMS}
    for seed in SEEDS:
        boards = [shared_board(seed, i, rl_nom) for i in range(BOARDS)]
        for arm in ARMS:
            errs = [abs(predict(arm, b) - truth_kcl(VS, b["r1"], b["r2"], b["rl"])) for b in boards]
            out[arm].append(1e3 * sum(errs) / len(errs))
    return out


def main() -> None:
    vth, rth = thevenin(VS, R1_NOM, R2_NOM)
    print("Synthetic Thevenin/Norton toy; shared parts and meter-noise draws; not a bench lab")
    print(
        f"nominal Vs={VS}V R1={R1_NOM:.0f}ohm R2={R2_NOM:.0f}ohm -> Vth={vth:.4f}V Rth={rth:.1f}ohm; "
        f"tol={TOL * 100:.0f}% V_noise={V_NOISE * 1e3:.0f}mV I_noise={I_NOISE * 1e6:.0f}uA "
        f"burden={R_BURDEN:.0f}ohm seeds={len(SEEDS)} boards={BOARDS}"
    )
    print(
        f"nominal check: RL=10k -> V_L = Vth*RL/(Rth+RL) = {vth * 1e4 / (rth + 1e4):.4f} V; "
        f"KCL = {truth_kcl(VS, R1_NOM, R2_NOM, 1e4):.4f} V; Norton In=Vth/Rth={vth / rth * 1e3:.4f} mA"
    )
    print("regime RL_ohm arm |V_pred-V_true|_mV (mean+-sd over seeds)")
    summary = {}
    paired = {}
    for name, rl in REGIMES:
        res = run_regime(rl)
        ab = [x - y for x, y in zip(res["A_voc_only"], res["B_thevenin"])]
        cb = [x - y for x, y in zip(res["C_norton"], res["B_thevenin"])]
        paired[name] = (mean_sd(ab), mean_sd(cb), sign_note(ab, mean_sd(ab)[0]), sign_note(cb, mean_sd(cb)[0]))
        for arm in ARMS:

## Superposition in a linear circuit

Kirchhoff's laws already let you write one joint set of equations for a whole net. This section is a different shortcut that only works when every element is linear: the net response of two or more sources is the sum of the responses each source would cause by itself. In electrical engineering, in a linear circuit, an input (an applied voltage or current) is related to an output (a voltage or current anywhere in the circuit) by a linear transformation, so a superposition of inputs yields the superposition of the responses (Wikipedia — Superposition principle, electrical-engineering paragraph; the title Superposition theorem redirects there; 以原文为准). This is **not** a redo of the Kirchhoff-in-brief notes above, **not** the Thévenin / Norton collapse of the loaded divider, **not** the Wheatstone bridge in the next lesson, and **not** the rectifier-ripple toy on the components page.

### Fair setup

`superposition_linear_circuit_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** lumped DC; two ideal independent voltage sources and three linear resistors, except in the nonlinear regime where the resistor to ground is replaced by a series ideal diode plus `Rf`; no source resistance and no element drift; meter noise, when present, is independent Gaussian on each reading. The shared net is `Vs1 = 5 V` through `R1 = 1000 Ω` into a node `V`, `Vs2 = 3 V` through `R2 = 2000 Ω` into the same node, and `R3 = 4000 Ω` (or the diode branch) to ground.

| Arm | What it computes |
|---|---|
| A_joint | one nodal KCL solve with both sources on |
| B_super | sum of the two single-source responses; the other independent voltage source is set to zero (replaced by a short) |
| residual | on the diode branch, the residual of superposition minus the joint solve |

**Common random numbers:** five seeds. The noiseless and diode regimes are deterministic (the five seeds repeat the same DC value). In `linear_meter`, each seed has 32 boards; the three Gaussian draws for A's one reading and B's two single-source readings are made once per board and shared in a fixed order. The metric is the mean node voltage per seed, reported as mean±sd over seeds. There is **no integration step** (DC nodal equation; the linear net is closed form, the diode net is a one-node bisection). The comparison is **blind** to wiring mistakes, meter loading, and any AC or time-varying story. Real breadboard / DMM: 本页未运行.

**Regimes, written before the first run:** linear resistors with no meter noise — A and B should be a **tie** or **numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie**; linear resistors plus tiny meter noise (`σ = 0.001 V`) — **near-tie**; the diode branch — superposition should **lose consistently** against the joint solve.

"""Synthetic superposition theorem vs a joint nodal solve vs a nonlinear element.

Stdlib only. Shared linear net: Vs1 -- R1 -- node V -- R2 -- Vs2, with R3 (or a
series ideal-diode + Rf stand-in) from V to ground. Arms:

  A_joint     one KCL nodal solve with both independent sources on
  B_super     sum of the two single-source responses (the other independent
              source is set to zero, i.e. a voltage source is replaced by a short)
  C_nonlinear same topology with R3 replaced by a series ideal diode + Rf; the
              sum of single-source responses is compared with the joint solve

Common random numbers: when a regime has meter noise, the 32 board indices and
the Gaussian draws are shared in a fixed order across arms on each seed (A uses
z0, B uses z1 then z2 on the two single-source readings). Seeds 0-4.

No integration step: DC nodal equation, closed form on the linear net, bisection
on the one-node nonlinear residual. Written before the first run.

Regimes (declared before the first run):
  linear_noiseless  resistors only, no meter noise  -> A vs B numerically zero / tie
  linear_meter      same linear net + tiny meter noise -> A vs B near-tie
  nonlinear_diode   series diode + Rf to ground     -> B (superposition) consistent lose

Not a Thévenin collapse, not a Wheatstone bridge, not a rectifier-ripple toy.
Real breadboard / DMM: 本页未运行. Wikipedia Superposition principle 以原文为准.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
BOARDS = 32
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9
VS1 = 5.0
VS2 = 3.0
R1 = 1000.0
R2 = 2000.0
R3 = 4000.0
VF = 0.7
RF = 2000.0
SIGMA = 0.001  # V, tiny meter noise (linear_meter)
SIGMA_ALT = 0.010  # V, alternative printed on the same seeds
ARMS = ("A_joint", "B_super", "C_nonlinear")
# name: kind; written before the first run
REGIMES = ("linear_noiseless", "linear_meter", "nonlinear_diode")
STREAM = {"linear_noiseless": 0, "linear_meter": 1, "nonlinear_diode": 2}


def g_parallel() -> float:
    return 1.0 / R1 + 1.0 / R2 + 1.0 / R3


def linear_node(vs1: float, vs2: float) -> float:
    """1x1 nodal KCL solve: V * (1/R1+1/R2+1/R3) = vs1/R1 + vs2/R2."""
    return (vs1 / R1 + vs2 / R2) / g_parallel()


def diode_current(v: float, vf: float, rf: float) -> float:
    return 0.0 if v <= vf else (v - vf) / rf


def nonlinear_residual(v: float, vs1: float, vs2: float, vf: float, rf: float) -> float:
    return (v - vs1) / R1 + (v - vs2) / R2 + diode_current(v, vf, rf)


def nonlinear_node(vs1: float, vs2: float, vf: float = VF, rf: float = RF) -> float:
    """Bisection on the one-node KCL residual. No time step."""
    lo, hi = -20.0, 20.0
    flo, fhi = nonlinear_residual(lo, vs1, vs2, vf, rf), nonlinear_residual(hi, vs1, vs2, vf, rf)
    if flo == 0.0:
        return lo
    if fhi == 0.0:
        return hi
    if flo * fhi > 0.0:
        raise RuntimeError("nonlinear bracket failed")
    for _ in range(80):
        mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        fmid = nonlinear_residual(mid, vs1, vs2, vf, rf)
        if fmid == 0.0 or 0.5 * (hi - lo) < 1e-15:
            return mid
        if flo * fmid <= 0.0:
            hi, fhi = mid, fmid
        else:
            lo, flo = mid, fmid
    return 0.5 * (lo + hi)


def mean_sd(xs: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    if len(xs) == 1:
        return mu, 0.0
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x: float) -> str:
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-5:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs: list[float], m: float) -> str:
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def verdict(xa: list[float], xb: list[float]) -> str:
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


def shared_noise(regime: str, seed: int, sigma: float) -> list[tuple[float, float, float]]:
    rng = random.Random(1000 * STREAM[regime] + seed)
    return [(rng.gauss(0.0, sigma), rng.gauss(0.0, sigma), rng.gauss(0.0, sigma)) for _ in range(BOARDS)]


def arm_voltage(arm: str, kind: str, vs1: float, vs2: float, noise: tuple[float, float, float], vf: float, rf: float) -> float:
    z0, z1, z2 = noise
    if kind == "linear":
        v_joint = linear_node(vs1, vs2)
        v_s1 = linear_node(vs1, 0.0)
        v_s2 = linear_node(0.0, vs2)
    else:
        v_joint = nonlinear_node(vs1, vs2, vf, rf)
        v_s1 = nonlinear_node(vs1, 0.0, vf, rf)
        v_s2 = nonlinear_node(0.0, vs2, vf, rf)
    if arm == "A_joint":
        return v_joint + z0
    if arm == "B_super":
        return (v_s1 + z1) + (v_s2 + z2)
    return v_joint + z0  # C reports the joint nonlinear voltage when kind is nonlinear


def seed_means(kind: str, sigma: float, regime: str, vf: float = VF, rf: float = RF) -> dict[str, list[float]]:
    out = {arm: [] for arm in ARMS}
    for seed in SEEDS:
        if sigma == 0.0:
            draws = [(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)]
        else:
            draws = shared_noise(regime, seed, sigma)
        acc = {arm: 0.0 for arm in ARMS}
        for noise in draws:
            for arm in ARMS:
                acc[arm] += arm_voltage(arm, kind, VS1, VS2, noise, vf, rf)
        n = len(draws)
        for arm in ARMS:
            out[arm].append(acc[arm] / n)
    return out


def main() -> None:
    v_joint = linear_node(VS1, VS2)
    v_s1 = linear_node(VS1, 0.0)
    v_s2 = linear_node(0.0, VS2)
    v_sum = v_s1 + v_s2
    residual = v_joint - v_sum
    i1 = (VS1 - v_joint) / R1
    i2 = (v_joint - VS2) / R2
    i3 = v_joint / R3
    # hand values 26/7, 20/7, 6/7 V
    print("Synthetic superposition toy; shared two-source linear net; not Thevenin, not Wheatstone, not a rectifier")
    print(f"VS1={VS1} V VS2={VS2} V R1={R1:.0f}ohm R2={R2:.0f}ohm R3={R3:.0f}ohm VF={VF} V RF={RF:.0f}ohm")
    print(f"seeds={len(SEEDS)} boards={BOARDS} meter_sigma={SIGMA} V alt_sigma={SIGMA_ALT} V")
    print("no integration step: DC nodal KCL, closed form on the linear net, bisection on the one-node nonlinear residual")
    print(f"linear_hand V_joint={v_joint:.10f} V (hand 26/7 V) V_s1_only={v_s1:.10f} V (hand 20/7 V) V_s2_only={v_s2:.10f} V (hand 6/7 V)")
    print(f"linear_hand V_sum={v_sum:.10f} V residual_A-B={residual:.3e} V I_R1={i1:.10f} A I_R2={i2:.10f} A I_R3={i3:.10f} A")
    print(f"KCL check I_R1-I_R2-I_R3={i1 - i2 - i3:.3e} A")

    v_nl = nonlinear_node(VS1, VS2)
    v_nl_s1 = nonlinear_node(VS1, 0.0)
    v_nl_s2 = nonlinear_node(0.0, VS2)
    print(f"nonlinear_hand V_joint={v_nl:.10f} V V_s1_only={v_nl_s1:.10f} V V_s2_only={v_nl_s2:.10f} V V_sum={v_nl_s1 + v_nl_s2:.10f} V residual_B-A={(v_nl_s1 + v_nl_s2) - v_nl:.10f} V")

    for regime in REGIMES:
        if regime == "linear_noiseless":
            kind, sigma, tag = "linear", 0.0, "linear resistors, no meter noise"
        elif regime == "linear_meter":
            kind, sigma, tag = "linear", SIGMA, f"linear resistors, meter noise N(0, {SIGMA} V)"
        else:
            kind, sigma, tag = "nonlinear", 0.0, f"series ideal diode + Rf, VF={VF} V RF={RF:.0f}ohm"
        print(f"{regime} ({tag})")
        res = seed_means(kind, sigma, regime)
        for arm in ("A_joint", "B_super"):
            m, s = mean_sd(res[arm])
            print(f"  {arm} {num(m)}+-{num(s)} V")
        residual = [b - a for a, b in zip(res["A_joint"], res["B_super"])]
        zeros = [0.0] * len(residual)
        print(f"  residual B-A {num(mean_sd(residual)[0])}+-{num(mean_sd(residual)[1])} V")
        print(f"  paired A-B nodeV: {verdict(res['A_joint'], res['B_super'])}")
        print(f"  paired residual B-A vs 0: {verdict(residual, zeros)}")

    # Alternative meter sigma on the same seeds (linear_meter stream), disclosed
    res_alt = seed_means("linear", SIGMA_ALT, "linear_meter")
    print(f"alternative linear_meter sigma={SIGMA_ALT} V (same seeds, same board indices, rescaled draws)")
    print(f"  paired A-B nodeV: {verdict(res_alt['A_joint'], res_alt['B_super'])}")
    # Alternative Rf on the same nonlinear net (deterministic)
    v_alt = nonlinear_node(VS1, VS2, VF, 1000.0)
    v_alt_s1 = nonlinear_node(VS1, 0.0, VF, 1000.0)
    v_alt_s2 = nonlinear_node(0.0, VS2, VF, 1000.0)
    print(f"alternative nonlinear Rf=1000ohm V_joint={v_alt:.10f} V V_sum={v_alt_s1 + v_alt_s2:.10f} V residual_B-A={(v_alt_s1 + v_alt_s2) - v_alt:.10f} V")
    print("No winner declared: superposition matches the joint solve on a linear net and fails once a diode is in the branch")
    print("Real breadboard / DMM: 本页未运行; Wikipedia Superposition principle 以原文为准")

A KCL check on the branch currents (`I_R1 = 0.0012857143 A`, `I_R2 = 0.0003571429 A`, `I_R3 = 0.0009285714 A`) leaves a floating-point residue of `-1.084e-19 A`.

- **linear_noiseless.** A and B both print `3.7143±0.0000 V`. The paired A−B node voltage and the residual of B−A against 0 are both `0.0000±0.0000: tie: identical on every seed`. Superposition and the joint solve agree exactly on this linear net. That is a **tie**, not a "clear" gap, and not a numerically-zero floating-point residue (the voltage residual printed as `0.000e+00 V`).
- **linear_meter.** With 1 mV meter noise, A is `3.7143±0.0002 V` and B is `3.7144±0.0003 V`. The paired A−B residual is `-0.0001±0.0003 V`: **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** (`|m| ≤ sd`; 1 of 5 seeds has the opposite sign).
- **nonlinear_diode.** The joint diode-branch voltage is `3.4250000000 V`; the two single-source voltages `2.6750000000 V` and `0.9250000000 V` sum to `3.6000000000 V`. The residual of superposition minus the joint solve is `0.1750000000 V`. Across the five seeds this residual versus 0 is `0.1750±0.0000`: **clear gap, not a tie** (`|m| > sd`; same sign on all 5 seeds). Superposition does not hold once a diode is in the branch. Comparing the raw node voltages `3.4250 V` and `3.6000 V` with each other also gives a **small but consistent** gap (`-0.1750±0.0000`; same sign on all 5 seeds), because `|m|` is under 20 % of the node voltage; the residual-versus-0 label is the one that scores the theorem.

**Post-hoc alternatives, same seeds.** Raising the meter noise to `σ = 0.01 V` still gives a **near-tie** on A−B (`-0.0010±0.0026 V`; 1 of 5 seeds has the opposite sign). Changing `Rf` to `1000 Ω` (disclosed alternative) makes the diode-branch residual `0.2800000000 V` (`V_joint = 2.8800000000 V`, `V_sum = 3.1600000000 V`); superposition still fails.

No arm is declared a universal winner: superposition matches the joint solve on a linear net and fails once a diode is in the branch.

## Y-Δ / Δ-Y reduction of two-terminal networks

Series and parallel combinations already let you reduce many two-terminal resistor nets to a single equivalent resistance. They are not always enough. English Wikipedia — Y-Δ transform (also written wye-delta, and also known as star-delta or T-Π; the circuit transformation theory was published by Arthur Edwin Kennelly in 1899; 以原文为准) is the three-resistor special case of the star-mesh transform, and it gives the formulae that make two three-terminal nets equivalent (the resistance between any pair of terminals is the same). This section teaches those opened Δ↔Y formulae, then contrasts a series-parallel-only reducer with a reducer that may apply one official Y-Δ or Δ-Y step and then series-parallel. The two-terminal five-resistor bridge is a reduction example, as in the article's *Simplification of networks* figures. This is **not** the Wheatstone measurement bridge from the components lesson, and that page is **not** reopened. It is **not** a second Thévenin, superposition, or Kirchhoff lecture, and mesh/nodal analysis is **not** a taught unit here.

The article states that series and parallel transforms "do not suffice" for a bridge; Y-Δ can eliminate a node (or Δ-Y can add a node) so the net can be reduced further (以原文为准). Real positive resistances only. Out of scope, though named on the opened page: complex impedances; admittance forms (not needed for the printed 手算); the three-phase "practical generator" conversion; graph-theory / Petersen-family non-planar examples; and star-mesh for n>3 (not opened as a body).

### The opened formulae

Δ → Y, with the article's labels:

- R1 = (Rb × Rc) / (Ra + Rb + Rc)
- R2 = (Ra × Rc) / (Ra + Rb + Rc)
- R3 = (Ra × Rb) / (Ra + Rb + Rc)

Y → Δ:

- Ra = (R1×R2 + R2×R3 + R3×R1) / R1 = R2 + R3 + (R2×R3)/R1
- Rb = (R1×R2 + R2×R3 + R3×R1) / R2 = R1 + R3 + (R1×R3)/R2
- Rc = (R1×R2 + R2×R3 + R3×R1) / R3 = R1 + R2 + (R1×R2)/R3

The rewritten forms are in the same section of the article; 以原文为准. In this lesson the official step, on both the ladder and the bridge, is **Y → Δ**, which eliminates one star node, then series-parallel. The swapped-direction arm uses the Δ → Y formulae in those same slots.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Synthetic Y-Δ / Δ-Y reduction of two-terminal resistor networks.

Stdlib only. Real positive resistances. Arms:

  A_series_parallel  series-parallel only. On a ladder it finishes and returns
                     Req. On a five-resistor bridge it uses the documented naive
                     shortcut of ignoring the cross edge and treating the two
                     remaining paths as parallel (always a number).
  B_official         official Y-Δ (formulae with the article's labels R1, R2, R3
                     / Ra, Rb, Rc) that eliminates one star node, then
                     series-parallel to a single Req.
  C_swapped          same skeleton as B, but uses the Δ-Y formulae where the
                     step is Y-Δ. Pre-declared lose, kept.

Common random numbers: seeds 0-4. Every regime is deterministic (fixed positive
resistances; the five seeds repeat the same DC value). No integration step:
algebraic series-parallel and the official transform, closed form.

Truth for error metrics is Arm B's official reduction on that net. An independent
nodal test-source check is printed as hand_calc only; it is not a taught
mesh/nodal unit and is not used to retune B.

Regimes (declared before the first run):
  series_parallel_ladder  no cross edge              -> A vs B exact tie
  large_cross_bridge      cross resistance very large -> A vs B near-tie or small
  moderate_bridge         cross comparable to the arms -> B clearly better than A
                                                     (20 % of the smaller-arm mean);
                                                     C loses on the same bridge

This is a two-terminal reduction example, not the Wheatstone measurement bridge
from the components lesson. Real breadboard / DMM: 本页未运行.
Wikipedia Y-Δ transform 以原文为准.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9
CLEAR_FRAC = 0.2000  # small-vs-clear factor; printed before the first official run
VTEST = 1.0  # V, nodal test-source check only (hand_calc)
# Ladder, P to Q: P--RS1--N1--RS2--N2--RS3--Q, RP1 from N1 to Q, RP2 from N2 to Q.
RS1 = 1000.0
RP1 = 2000.0
RS2 = 3000.0
RP2 = 4000.0
RS3 = 5000.0
# Bridge, terminals P and Q, internal nodes L and R:
# RPL = P-L, RPR = P-R, RQL = L-Q, RQR = R-Q, RX = L-R (the cross edge).
RPL = 100.0
RPR = 10000.0
RQL = 10000.0
RQR = 100.0
RX_LARGE = 1000000.0
RX_MODERATE = 100.0
RX_ALT_LARGE = 10000000.0  # disclosed alternative, same bridge arms, not adopted
ARMS = ("A_series_parallel", "B_official", "C_swapped")
REGIMES = ("series_parallel_ladder", "large_cross_bridge", "moderate_bridge")


def par(*rs: float) -> float:
    return 1.0 / sum(1.0 / r for r in rs)


def delta_from_wye(r1: float, r2: float, r3: float) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Official Y → Δ, article labels. Ra opposite R1, Rb opposite R2, Rc opposite R3."""
    p = r1 * r2 + r2 * r3 + r3 * r1
    ra = p / r1  # = r2 + r3 + (r2 * r3) / r1
    rb = p / r2
    rc = p / r3
    return ra, rb, rc


def wye_from_delta(ra: float, rb: float, rc: float) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Official Δ → Y, article labels."""
    s = ra + rb + rc
    r1 = (rb * rc) / s
    r2 = (ra * rc) / s
    r3 = (ra * rb) / s
    return r1, r2, r3


def solve2(a11: float, a12: float, a21: float, a22: float, b1: float, b2: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
    det = a11 * a22 - a12 * a21
    return (b1 * a22 - a12 * b2) / det, (a11 * b2 - b1 * a21) / det


def series_parallel_ladder() -> float:
    return RS1 + par(RP1, RS2 + par(RP2, RS3))


def official_ladder(transform) -> float:
    """Y at N1: R1=RS1 to P, R2=RS2 to N2, R3=RP1 to Q; then series-parallel."""
    ra, rb, rc = transform(RS1, RS2, RP1)
    # Ra between N2-Q, Rb between P-Q, Rc between P-N2; RP2 and RS3 remain N2-Q.
    return par(rb, rc + par(ra, RP2, RS3))


def series_parallel_bridge_shortcut() -> float:
    """Naive shortcut: ignore the cross edge; two series paths in parallel."""
    return par(RPL + RQL, RPR + RQR)


def official_bridge(rx: float, transform) -> float:
    """Y at L: R1=RPL to P, R2=RQL to Q, R3=RX to R; then series-parallel."""
    ra, rb, rc = transform(RPL, RQL, rx)
    # Ra between Q-R, Rb between P-R, Rc between P-Q; RPR remains P-R, RQR remains R-Q.
    return par(rc, par(rb, RPR) + par(ra, RQR))


def nodal_ladder(vtest: float = VTEST) -> float:
    """Two-node test-source check, hand_calc only."""
    a11 = 1.0 / RS1 + 1.0 / RP1 + 1.0 / RS2
    a12 = -1.0 / RS2
    a21 = -1.0 / RS2
    a22 = 1.0 / RS2 + 1.0 / RP2 + 1.0 / RS3
    v1, _v2 = solve2(a11, a12, a21, a22, vtest / RS1, 0.0)
    return vtest / ((vtest - v1) / RS1)


def nodal_bridge(rx: float, vtest: float = VTEST) -> float:
    """Two-node test-source check, hand_calc only."""
    a11 = 1.0 / RPL + 1.0 / RQL + 1.0 / rx
    a12 = -1.0 / rx
    a21 = -1.0 / rx
    a22 = 1.0 / RPR + 1.0 / RQR + 1.0 / rx
    vl, vr = solve2(a11, a12, a21, a22, vtest / RPL, vtest / RPR)
    current = (vtest - vl) / RPL + (vtest - vr) / RPR
    return vtest / current


def mean_sd(xs: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    if len(xs) == 1:
        return mu, 0.0
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x: float) -> str:
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-5:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs: list[float], m: float) -> str:
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def verdict(xa: list[float], xb: list[float]) -> str:
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < CLEAR_FRAC * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


def seed_values(req: dict[str, float]) -> dict[str, list[float]]:
    return {arm: [req[arm]] * len(SEEDS) for arm in ARMS}


def print_regime(name: str, tag: str, req: dict[str, float]) -> None:
    res = seed_values(req)
    print(f"{name} ({tag})")
    for arm in ARMS:
        m, s = mean_sd(res[arm])
        print(f"  {arm} {num(m)}+-{num(s)} ohm")
    err_a = [a - b for a, b in zip(res["A_series_parallel"], res["B_official"])]
    err_c = [c - b for c, b in zip(res["C_swapped"], res["B_official"])]
    zeros = [0.0] * len(SEEDS)
    print(f"  residual A-B {num(mean_sd(err_a)[0])}+-{num(mean_sd(err_a)[1])} ohm")
    print(f"  residual C-B {num(mean_sd(err_c)[0])}+-{num(mean_sd(err_c)[1])} ohm")
    print(f"  paired A-B Req: {verdict(res['A_series_parallel'], res['B_official'])}")
    print(f"  paired C-B Req: {verdict(res['C_swapped'], res['B_official'])}")
    print(f"  paired residual A-B vs 0: {verdict(err_a, zeros)}")
    print(f"  paired residual C-B vs 0: {verdict(err_c, zeros)}")


def main() -> None:
    ra_l, rb_l, rc_l = delta_from_wye(RS1, RS2, RP1)
    ra_l_rw = RS2 + RP1 + (RS2 * RP1) / RS1
    req_a_l = series_parallel_ladder()
    req_b_l = official_ladder(delta_from_wye)
    req_c_l = official_ladder(wye_from_delta)
    nod_l = nodal_ladder()

    ra_m, rb_m, rc_m = delta_from_wye(RPL, RQL, RX_MODERATE)
    ra_m_rw = RQL + RX_MODERATE + (RQL * RX_MODERATE) / RPL
    req_a_b = series_parallel_bridge_shortcut()
    req_b_m = official_bridge(RX_MODERATE, delta_from_wye)
    req_c_m = official_bridge(RX_MODERATE, wye_from_delta)
    nod_m = nodal_bridge(RX_MODERATE)

    ra_g, rb_g, rc_g = delta_from_wye(RPL, RQL, RX_LARGE)
    req_b_g = official_bridge(RX_LARGE, delta_from_wye)
    req_c_g = official_bridge(RX_LARGE, wye_from_delta)
    nod_g = nodal_bridge(RX_LARGE)

    req_b_alt = official_bridge(RX_ALT_LARGE, delta_from_wye)
    req_c_alt = official_bridge(RX_ALT_LARGE, wye_from_delta)
    nod_alt = nodal_bridge(RX_ALT_LARGE)

    print("Synthetic Y-delta / delta-Y reduction toy; two-terminal networks; not a Wheatstone measurement bridge")
    print(
        f"ladder RS1={RS1:.0f}ohm RP1={RP1:.0f}ohm RS2={RS2:.0f}ohm RP2={RP2:.0f}ohm RS3={RS3:.0f}ohm"
    )
    print(
        f"bridge RPL={RPL:.0f}ohm RPR={RPR:.0f}ohm RQL={RQL:.0f}ohm RQR={RQR:.0f}ohm "
        f"RX_LARGE={RX_LARGE:.0f}ohm RX_MODERATE={RX_MODERATE:.0f}ohm"
    )
    print(
        f"seeds={len(SEEDS)} VTEST={VTEST} V clear_frac={CLEAR_FRAC:.4f} "
        f"hand_calc work_plan 30+25+50+40+35=180"
    )
    print("no integration step: algebraic series-parallel and official Y-delta, closed form")
    print(
        f"hand_calc ladder Y_to_delta R1={RS1:.0f} R2={RS2:.0f} R3={RP1:.0f} "
        f"Ra={ra_l:.10f}ohm Rb={rb_l:.10f}ohm Rc={rc_l:.10f}ohm "
        f"Ra_rewritten={ra_l_rw:.10f}ohm"
    )
    print(
        f"hand_calc ladder Req_A={req_a_l:.10f}ohm Req_B={req_b_l:.10f}ohm "
        f"Req_C={req_c_l:.10f}ohm residual_A-B={req_a_l - req_b_l:.3e}ohm "
        f"nodal={nod_l:.10f}ohm residual_B-nodal={req_b_l - nod_l:.3e}ohm"
    )
    print(
        f"hand_calc moderate Y_to_delta R1={RPL:.0f} R2={RQL:.0f} R3={RX_MODERATE:.0f} "
        f"Ra={ra_m:.10f}ohm Rb={rb_m:.10f}ohm Rc={rc_m:.10f}ohm "

**Key points**

- Ohm's law V = I × R: 9 V across 1 kΩ gives I = 9 V / 1000 Ω = 0.009 A = 9 mA.
- Power P = V × I = I² × R = V² / R: 5 V across 100 Ω dissipates 0.25 W, so a 1/2 W part runs it at the recommended 50 % of rating.
- Series resistances add (220 Ω + 330 Ω = 550 Ω) and parallel ones combine as reciprocals (10 kΩ in parallel with 10 kΩ = 5 kΩ).
- Divider Vout = Vin × R2 / (R1 + R2): 5 V with R1 = 10 kΩ and R2 = 4.7 kΩ gives Vout ≈ 1.6 V.
- Loading matters: a 10 kΩ load across R2 drops that output to about 1.2 V, a 25 % fall, so keep the load at least 10× R2.
- Battery into an ADC: 8.4 V with 10 kΩ / 10 kΩ gives 4.20 V and 0.42 mA standing current.
- Thévenin / Norton: the practice divider collapses to `Vth = 1.5986 V` behind `Rth ≈ 3.2 kΩ` (Norton 0.5 mA ∥ 3.2 kΩ), so `V_L = Vth × RL / (Rth + RL)` reproduces 1.21 V at 10 kΩ; when RL ≫ Rth, Thévenin and Norton are a **near-tie / small gap** and `Voc` alone trails by only a small but consistent gap (not a tie) — not a bench lab.
- Superposition: on the shared two-source linear net the joint nodal solve and the sum of single-source responses are a **tie** (identical on every seed) at `3.7142857143 V` (`26/7 V` by hand); 1 mV meter noise is a **near-tie** (`-0.0001±0.0003 V`, 1 of 5 seeds has the opposite sign); a series diode makes superposition lose by a **clear** `0.1750±0.0000 V` residual (same sign on all 5 seeds) — not a breadboard lab.
- Y-Δ / Δ-Y: official Y-Δ then series-parallel **ties** series-parallel-only on a purely series-parallel ladder (`2446.1538+-0.0000 ohm`, identical on every seed); with a very large cross edge the shortcut is a **small but consistent** gap (`24.3794+-0.0000 ohm`, same sign on all 5 seeds); on a moderate five-resistor bridge the official reduction is **clearly better** (`4757.7670+-0.0000 ohm`) and the swapped-direction formulae **lose** (`-258.9083+-0.0000 ohm`, same sign on all 5 seeds) — not the Wheatstone measurement bridge, not a bench lab.

**Common mistakes**

- Mixing mA with A, or kΩ with Ω: 5 V across 220 Ω is 0.0227 A = 22.7 mA, not 22.7 A.
- Swapping R1 and R2: R1 is the top resistor from Vin to the output, R2 is the bottom one to ground.
- Forgetting the power rating: calculate P first and choose a rating of at least twice the dissipation.
- Powering a load from a divider: it is a signal, not a supply.
