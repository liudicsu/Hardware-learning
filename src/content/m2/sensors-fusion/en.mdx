## What a robot can sense

A robot without sensors is a machine that executes a plan and hopes. A robot with sensors can compare what it expected with what actually happened, and that comparison is the beginning of every useful behaviour: stopping before a wall, driving straight, balancing, following a line, or knowing that a wheel is slipping.

Every sensor answers one narrow question. The table below is not a shopping list; it is a map of the questions a small robot usually needs to ask.

| Sensor | Measures | Typical use | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wheel encoder | Rotation counts, and usually direction | Odometry, speed, distance travelled (Lesson 4) | It only knows how far the wheel turned; slipping or a wrong wheel diameter quietly corrupts the result |
| IMU (accelerometer + gyroscope, e.g. MPU-6050) | Acceleration including gravity, and <Term id="angular-velocity">rotation rate</Term> | Tilt, balancing, detecting turns | Noisy and drift-prone, so it needs fusion; it is not a compass |
| Ultrasonic HC-SR04 | Distance from the echo time | Cheap obstacle detection | Wide cone, soft surfaces absorb sound, and the 5 V echo needs a divider on an ESP32 |
| Time-of-flight laser VL53L0X | Distance from the travel time of light, over I2C | Precise short-range distance (up to about 2 m); a narrower field of view (ST specifies about 25°) and no ultrasonic echo confusion | Dark, reflective or transparent targets and strong sunlight reduce the usable range |
| Reflectance sensors (QTR, TCRT5000) | How much infrared light bounces back | Line following (Lesson 6) | Very sensitive to mounting height, ambient light and surface colour; calibrate on the actual track |
| 2D lidar (e.g. RPLIDAR C1) | A 360° ring of distances | Mapping, in later months | Moving parts, more power and data, and still only one horizontal slice of the world |

Two of these measure the robot itself — the encoder and the IMU — while the rest measure the world. That distinction is useful when something goes wrong. An encoder error means your model of your own motion is wrong; an ultrasonic error means your picture of the world is wrong, and the fixes are different.

Prices are rough, because the same chip costs very different amounts from different sellers: an HC-SR04 is about $4, a VL53L0X about $15, an MPU-6050 breakout about $13, and a BNO085 about $30. Do not choose by price alone; choose the sensor whose failure mode you can live with.

The ultrasonic sensor is a good first worked example because the arithmetic is simple and it shows why the round trip matters. Sound travels at about 343 m/s at room temperature, which is 34 300 cm/s, or 0.0343 cm per microsecond. The HC-SR04 sends a short burst, then measures how long the echo takes to come back. The sound went to the object and returned, so the distance is only half of the travelled path.

```text
Speed of sound ≈ 343 m/s = 0.0343 cm/µs
Echo time = 1166 µs (there and back)
Distance = 1166 µs × 0.0343 cm/µs / 2
         = 39.99 cm / 2
         = 20.0 cm
```

The speed of sound changes with temperature (about 0.6 m/s per °C), so 20.0 cm is an estimate, not a laboratory measurement. For a robot deciding whether a chair is close, that is plenty.

## I2C in one page

<Term id="i2c">I2C</Term> (Inter-Integrated Circuit, usually said as I-squared-C) is a two-wire bus: SDA carries data, SCL carries the clock, and both boards must share a ground. The microcontroller is the controller and generates the clock. Each peripheral has a 7-bit address. Every transaction begins with the controller sending that address plus a read/write bit, and only the device that recognises the address answers.

The clever part is the electrical design. The output stage of each device is <Term id="open-drain">open-drain</Term>: it can pull a line LOW, but it cannot drive it HIGH. A pull-up resistor from the line to the supply does the high side. Typical values are 4.7–10 kΩ, and many breakout boards already include them. Because every device can only pull low, two devices can never conflict: if both pull low at once, they are simply connecting the same node to ground, and the line is still at 0 V. There is no high driver to short against.

<Falstad title="Open-drain I2C line with a pull-up" circuit="i2cPullup" height={420} />

The 4.7 kΩ resistor holds the line at 3.3 V when nobody is pulling it down. Each switch is the output transistor inside a device. Click either switch and the line goes to 0 V; click both and it is still just 0 V, with no short and no damage. That is why any device on the bus can talk without damaging any other device on it.

I2C has standard speeds of 100 kHz and 400 kHz. It is not a long-distance bus. Keep the wires short, keep the ground shared, and do not run I2C across a robot chassis next to a motor cable if you can avoid it. On an Arduino Uno, SDA is A4 and SCL is A5. On an ESP32 almost any GPIO can be used; the classic default is 21 for SDA and 22 for SCL, and you can pass your chosen pins to `Wire.begin(sda, scl)`.

Address conflicts are the usual practical problem. Two identical sensors usually share one address, so they cannot simply sit on the same bus. Many chips have an address pin for exactly this reason: on the MPU-6050, AD0 LOW gives 0x68 and AD0 HIGH gives 0x69. If a board has no address pin, you need a second bus or an I2C multiplexer.

I2C is not the only bus you will meet. SPI uses four wires — SCK, COPI (often still called MOSI), CIPO (MISO), and one chip-select per device — with no addresses and much higher speed; it is common for displays, SD cards and some IMUs. UART, the Serial port, is point-to-point and asynchronous, with TX and RX; it is excellent for a GPS or a debug console, but it is not a shared bus. Most robots end up using all three.

## Find it: the I2C scanner

The first program to run with any new I2C device is not a driver. It is a scanner. It walks the 7-bit address range, asks each address whether anyone is there, and prints the ones that answer. This one short sketch saves more time than any library.

<CodeSource path="examples/month-2/sensors-fusion/i2c_scanner/i2c_scanner.ino" />

```cpp
// I2C scanner: try every 7-bit address and report the ones that answer.
#include <Wire.h>

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  Wire.begin();
  Serial.println(F("Scanning..."));
  int found = 0;
  for (uint8_t addr = 1; addr < 127; addr++) {
    Wire.beginTransmission(addr);
    if (Wire.endTransmission() == 0) {      // 0 = the device acknowledged
      Serial.print(F("Found device at 0x"));
      if (addr < 16) Serial.print('0');
      Serial.println(addr, HEX);
      found++;
    }
  }
  Serial.print(found);
  Serial.println(F(" device(s) found"));
}

void loop() {}
```

`beginTransmission(address)` loads the address into the Wire library's buffer. `endTransmission()` actually sends it and returns a status. With no data written in between, the transaction is just the address: it asks "is anyone at this address?" A return value of 0 means the device acknowledged (ACK). A return value of 2 means the address was not acknowledged. The scanner ignores every other value for now; the important distinction is 0 versus everything else.

A scanner can only tell you that something answered. It cannot tell you what the device is; another chip could share the same address. That is what the WHO_AM_I register is for, a few paragraphs from here.

When nothing is found, check in this order: power and ground on the sensor, SDA and SCL swapped, the wrong pins in `Wire.begin()` or in the wiring, missing pull-ups (some bare modules have none), and a 3.3 V-only module powered from the wrong rail. If the scanner hangs instead of printing, one of the lines is probably stuck LOW — often a wiring short or a device holding the bus. Power everything off, fix the wiring, and try again.

## Read the datasheet, not a tutorial

The milestone for this month is not "I copied a library example". It is: give me a sensor, its datasheet, and a microcontroller, and I can wire it and read it myself. Tutorials are useful for orientation, but they go stale, they hide the <Term id="register">register</Term> writes, and they rarely tell you which configuration they assumed.

For the MPU-6050, InvenSense publishes a product specification and a separate register map. The product specification tells you what the chip can do, what the pins mean, and how sensitive each range is. The register map tells you which byte to read or write. You need both.

These are the registers this lesson uses:

| Register | Address | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WHO_AM_I | 0x75 | Always reads 0x68 — proves you are talking to the right chip |
| PWR_MGMT_1 | 0x6B | Bit 6 SLEEP is set at power-up — write 0x00 to wake the chip, otherwise every reading is 0 |
| ACCEL_XOUT_H … | 0x3B–0x40 | Accelerometer X, Y and Z, each 16-bit |
| TEMP_OUT | 0x41–0x42 | Temperature |
| GYRO_XOUT_H … | 0x43–0x48 | Gyroscope X, Y and Z |

Register addresses and values are written in hexadecimal; the [math reference page](/en/math#hex) works through 0x68 = 0b01101000 = 104 bit by bit.

WHO_AM_I is the simplest self-check in embedded work. Read one byte from 0x75 and expect 0x68. If you get 0xFF, the bus is probably not connected or the device is not answering. If you get a different value, you are talking to something else.

The sleep bit is the classic trap. Out of reset, PWR_MGMT_1 has bit 6 set, and the chip is asleep. You can read registers all day and get zeros. Write 0x00 to 0x6B, give it a moment, and the sensor starts producing data.

The data are signed 16-bit numbers in big-endian order: high byte first. One burst read of 14 bytes starting at 0x3B returns accelerometer X, Y, Z, temperature, and gyroscope X, Y, Z in that order. A burst read also means all the values belong to the same sample. Reading one register at a time over several milliseconds can mix two different moments, which is exactly what you do not want when the robot is moving.

At the default ranges, the sensitivity is fixed and simple: the accelerometer is set to ±2 g, which gives 16384 LSB per g; the gyroscope is set to ±250 °/s, which gives 131 LSB per °/s. Both follow from the output format: each axis is a signed 16-bit number, −32768 … +32767, and the selected range is mapped onto it, so 32768 / 2 g = 16384 LSB/g and 32768 / 250 °/s = 131.07, listed in the datasheet as 131 LSB/(°/s). These constants are not universal. Change AFS_SEL or FS_SEL and they change too, so always check which range your code configured.

```text
raw az = 16384  →  16384 / 16384 = 1.00 g
raw gy = −262   →  −262 / 131 = −2.0 °/s
```

The first line is what a board lying flat should show on the vertical axis: one g of gravity. The second is a slow rotation of two degrees per second in the negative direction. If you store the raw bytes in an unsigned 16-bit variable, the negative value turns into a huge positive number; use `int16_t` and combine the bytes in the right order.

**Two's complement.** A signed 16-bit number is stored in <Term id="twos-complement">two's complement</Term>: the top bit has the weight −32768 and the other 15 bits the usual +1 … +16384, so the range is −32768 … +32767. To negate a value, invert every bit and add 1.

```text
262            = 0x0106 = 0000 0001 0000 0110
invert         = 0xFEF9 = 1111 1110 1111 1001
+ 1            = 0xFEFA = 1111 1110 1111 1010  = −262
as unsigned    = 0xFEFA = 65274 = 65536 − 262
high byte 0xFE, low byte 0xFA:  (int16_t)((0xFE << 8) | 0xFA) = −262
```

## Two imperfect sensors

The accelerometer and the gyroscope fail in opposite ways, which is exactly why they are useful together.

The maths in this section is collected on the [math reference page](/en/math): [sine and cosine components](/en/math#trig-components), [radians](/en/math#radians), [accumulating rate × dt](/en/math#rate-accumulation), and [standard deviation](/en/math#stddev-rms) for the noise figures in the script.

An accelerometer measures acceleration, and gravity counts as acceleration. At rest, the only acceleration it feels is gravity, pointing down at 1 g. If the board is pitched by an angle θ around the Y axis, gravity appears on the X and Z axes as `ax = g × sin θ` and `az = g × cos θ`. Therefore:

```text
θ = atan2(ax, az)
```

`atan2` returns radians, so convert with `× 180 / PI` before printing degrees. This estimate is correct on average because gravity is an absolute reference: it never accumulates error. But it is noisy — every sample wobbles — and any real acceleration corrupts it. Vibration, a motor jerk, a bump against the table, or the robot driving forward all add to gravity, and the accelerometer cannot tell the difference. During a hard acceleration the "tilt" reading can be badly wrong.

A gyroscope measures angular rate, not angle. To get angle you integrate: add `rate × dt` every loop. The result is smooth and fast, and it responds immediately to a real rotation. But it is relative: it only knows how much it has turned since you started. A small constant <Term id="gyro-bias">bias</Term> — say 1.5 °/s — integrates into an error that grows without limit.

<Formula label="Gyro drift">angle error = bias × time</Formula>

```text
bias = 1.5 °/s
after 10 s: 1.5 °/s × 10 s = 15°
after 60 s: 1.5 °/s × 60 s = 90°
```

After one minute the gyro-only angle is 90° wrong, even if the robot has not moved at all. The gyro has no internal reference to notice this; it is faithfully integrating its own offset.

<Quiz id="q5" question="A gyro has a 0.5 °/s bias. After 60 s of integrating, how large is the angle error?" answer={30} unit="°" explanation="error = bias × time = 0.5 °/s × 60 s = 30°." />

## The complementary filter

The <Term id="complementary-filter">complementary filter</Term> is the simplest way to combine the two sensors, and it is the one to write before you touch a Kalman filter. The idea is one line:

<Formula label="Complementary filter">angle = α × (angle + rate × dt) + (1 − α) × accelAngle</Formula>

α is close to 1, typically 0.98. The first term takes the previous estimate and adds the gyro's prediction. The second term pulls that prediction toward the accelerometer angle. Because α is close to 1, the gyro dominates over short intervals; because 1 − α is small but nonzero, the accelerometer wins in the long run. It is a <Term id="low-high-pass-filter">high-pass filter</Term> on the gyro and a low-pass filter on the accelerometer, and the two weights add up to 1.

The time constant tells you how fast the accelerometer can correct the gyro:

```text
τ = α × dt / (1 − α)
  = 0.98 × 0.01 s / 0.02
  = 0.49 s
```

What the time constant measures: leave the accelerometer term aside for a moment. Each step keeps a fraction α of the previous estimate, so after n steps a fraction αⁿ of an old error remains. The time constant is the time for that fraction to fall to 1/e:

```text
αⁿ = e^(−1)  →  n = −1 / ln α = −1 / ln 0.98 = 49.5 steps
τ = n × dt = 49.5 × 0.01 s = 0.495 s
```

For α close to 1, −ln α ≈ 1 − α, so τ ≈ dt / (1 − α) = 0.50 s. The formula in the box above, τ = α × dt / (1 − α) = 0.49 s, is not a third approximation of the same decay. It comes from the usual way of choosing α for a desired time constant, α = τ / (τ + dt), which is the form the course's own simulations use (`a = 0.5 / (0.5 + dt_ctrl)` in the balancer model of Your first two robots: line follower & self-balancer); solving that definition for τ gives τ = α × dt / (1 − α). The derivation above gives the exact decay time, 0.495 s, and the boxed formula is the design convention; at α = 0.98 and 100 Hz the three values 0.49 s, 0.495 s and 0.50 s agree to within about 2 %.

At 100 Hz (dt = 0.01 s) and α = 0.98, the correction time constant is about half a second. A larger τ is smoother but slower; a smaller τ is faster but lets more accelerometer noise through.

The Python script below simulates the problem before you wire anything. The board lies flat, is tilted to 30° between t = 3 s and 4 s, held there, and laid flat again between 7 s and 8 s. The simulated gyro reads 1.5 °/s too high, and the accelerometer has noise.

<CodeSource path="tools/sim/models/filter_sim.py" />

```python
"""filter_sim.py: gyro drift, accelerometer noise, and the complementary filter.

The board lies flat, is tilted to 30 degrees between t = 3 s and 4 s, held there,
and laid flat again between 7 s and 8 s. The gyro reads 1.5 deg/s too high (bias);
the accelerometer angle is noisy. Sample rate: 100 Hz.
"""
import math
import random

DT = 0.01
GYRO_BIAS = 1.5      # deg/s
GYRO_NOISE = 0.3     # deg/s, standard deviation
ACCEL_NOISE = 0.02   # g, standard deviation per axis


def true_pitch(t):
    if t < 3:
        return 0.0
    if t < 4:
        return 30.0 * (t - 3)
    if t < 7:
        return 30.0
    if t < 8:
        return 30.0 * (8 - t)
    return 0.0


def simulate(alpha, seconds=12.0, seed=7):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    gyro_only = fused = 0.0
    rows = []
    for i in range(1, int(seconds / DT) + 1):
        t = i * DT
        rate = (true_pitch(t) - true_pitch(t - DT)) / DT
        gyro = rate + GYRO_BIAS + rng.gauss(0, GYRO_NOISE)
        th = math.radians(true_pitch(t))
        ax = math.sin(th) + rng.gauss(0, ACCEL_NOISE)
        az = math.cos(th) + rng.gauss(0, ACCEL_NOISE)
        accel = math.degrees(math.atan2(ax, az))
        gyro_only += gyro * DT
        fused = alpha * (fused + gyro * DT) + (1 - alpha) * accel
        rows.append((t, true_pitch(t), gyro_only, accel, fused))
    return rows


def summary(rows):
    """Error of the fused angle: mean and spread while flat (t > 10 s), and the worst error overall."""
    flat = [r[4] - r[1] for r in rows if r[0] > 10]
    mean = sum(flat) / len(flat)
    spread = math.sqrt(sum((e - mean) ** 2 for e in flat) / len(flat))
    worst = max(abs(r[4] - r[1]) for r in rows)
    return mean, spread, worst


if __name__ == "__main__":
    rows = simulate(alpha=0.98)
    print(" t_s  true  gyro_only  accel_only  fused")
    for t, true, g, a, f in rows:
        if abs(t - round(t)) < 1e-9 and round(t) in (1, 3, 4, 7, 8, 12):
            print(f"{t:4.0f} {true:5.1f} {g:10.1f} {a:11.1f} {f:6.1f}")
    print()
    print("alpha  offset_flat  noise_flat  worst_error")
    for alpha in (0.90, 0.98, 0.995):
        mean, spread, worst = summary(simulate(alpha))
        print(f"{alpha:<6} {mean:10.2f}  {spread:10.2f}  {worst:11.1f}")
```

The first table shows three different estimation behaviours. The gyro-only column climbs by 1.5 °/s forever: 4.6° at t = 3 s while the board is still flat, 40.6° at t = 7 s while the true angle is 30°, and 18.0° at t = 12 s after the board has returned to flat. It is always "30°-ish plus drift" while tilted, and it never comes back to zero.

The accelerometer-only column is right on average but jumpy: 0.5° at t = 1 s, −1.0° at t = 3 s, 31.3° at t = 4 s, and −1.3° at t = 12 s. Individual samples wander by a degree or more, which is enough to make a balancing robot twitch.

The fused column follows the real tilt, settles around 30.8° while the board is held, and returns to about 0.8° when it is flat again. Its worst error over the whole run is 1.0°. That is the whole point: neither sensor is good enough alone, but together they are.

The second table is the α trade-off, from the same simulation:

| α | Offset while flat | Spread while flat | Worst error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.90 | 0.13° | 0.24° | 0.9° |
| 0.98 | 0.72° | 0.07° | 1.0° |
| 0.995 | 2.96° | 0.02° | 3.0° |

A small α lets more accelerometer noise through, but it suppresses the gyro bias quickly. A large α is smoother but leaves a bigger constant offset, because the gyro bias leaks into the estimate. The steady-state offset is approximately:

```text
offset ≈ α / (1 − α) × bias × dt
       = 0.98 / 0.02 × 1.5 °/s × 0.01 s
       = 0.735°
```

The formula follows from the filter equation itself. With the board flat, the accelerometer angle averages 0°, and in the steady state the estimate no longer changes, so the same value `angle` appears on both sides:

```text
angle = α × (angle + bias × dt) + (1 − α) × 0
angle × (1 − α) = α × bias × dt
angle = α × bias × dt / (1 − α) = 0.98 × 1.5 °/s × 0.01 s / 0.02 = 0.735°
```

That matches the measured 0.72° in the table (the small difference is noise and rounding). The formula also explains the other rows: α = 0.90 gives about 0.135°, and α = 0.995 gives about 2.985°.

If the robot heats up or moves to a much colder room, the bias can change; a quick recalibration at startup is cheap.

## On the real chip

Wiring the common GY-521 breakout to an Uno is short: VCC to 5 V (the board has a 3.3 V regulator), GND to GND, SDA to A4, SCL to A5, and AD0 left unconnected for address 0x68. The same breakout has 3.3 V pull-ups on the I2C lines; check your particular board, because some bare MPU-6050 modules are 3.3 V only and have no regulator. The same sketch runs on an ESP32 with different I2C pins — call `Wire.begin(sda, scl)` with the pins you chose, classic default 21 and 22 — but the ESP32 is 3.3 V logic, so never feed it 5 V signals without checking.

<CodeSource path="examples/month-2/sensors-fusion/imu_pitch/imu_pitch.ino" />

```cpp
// MPU-6050 pitch angle three ways: gyro only, accelerometer only, complementary filter.
// Wiring (Uno): VCC -> 5V (the common GY-521 breakout has a regulator), GND -> GND,
// SDA -> A4, SCL -> A5. AD0 left unconnected -> address 0x68.
#include <Wire.h>

const uint8_t MPU = 0x68;
const uint8_t REG_PWR_MGMT_1 = 0x6B;
const uint8_t REG_ACCEL_XOUT_H = 0x3B;
const uint8_t REG_WHO_AM_I = 0x75;

const float ACCEL_LSB_PER_G = 16384.0;   // +-2 g range (datasheet, default)
const float GYRO_LSB_PER_DPS = 131.0;    // +-250 deg/s range (datasheet, default)
const float ALPHA = 0.98;                // complementary filter weight on the gyro
const unsigned long PERIOD_MS = 10;      // 100 Hz loop

float gyroOnly = 0, fused = 0;
unsigned long last = 0, lastPrint = 0;

void writeReg(uint8_t reg, uint8_t value) {
  Wire.beginTransmission(MPU);
  Wire.write(reg);
  Wire.write(value);
  Wire.endTransmission();
}

uint8_t readReg(uint8_t reg) {
  Wire.beginTransmission(MPU);
  Wire.write(reg);
  Wire.endTransmission(false);           // repeated start: keep the bus
  Wire.requestFrom(MPU, (uint8_t)1);
  return Wire.read();
}

int16_t read16() {                       // registers are big-endian: high byte first
  int16_t hi = Wire.read();
  return (hi << 8) | Wire.read();
}

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  Wire.begin();
  writeReg(REG_PWR_MGMT_1, 0x00);        // the chip powers up asleep: clear SLEEP
  Serial.print(F("WHO_AM_I = 0x"));
  Serial.println(readReg(REG_WHO_AM_I), HEX);
  Serial.println(F("t_s,gyro_only,accel_only,fused"));
  last = millis();
}

void loop() {
  unsigned long now = millis();
  if (now - last < PERIOD_MS) return;
  float dt = (now - last) / 1000.0;
  last = now;

  Wire.beginTransmission(MPU);
  Wire.write(REG_ACCEL_XOUT_H);
  Wire.endTransmission(false);
  Wire.requestFrom(MPU, (uint8_t)14);    // accel XYZ, temperature, gyro XYZ
  int16_t ax = read16(), ay = read16(), az = read16();
  read16();                              // temperature, unused
  read16();                              // gyro X, unused
  int16_t gy = read16();
  read16();                              // gyro Z, unused

  float accelPitch = atan2(ax / ACCEL_LSB_PER_G, az / ACCEL_LSB_PER_G) * 180.0 / PI;
  float rate = gy / GYRO_LSB_PER_DPS;    // deg/s around the Y axis

  gyroOnly += rate * dt;                                       // drifts
  fused = ALPHA * (fused + rate * dt) + (1 - ALPHA) * accelPitch;  // does not

  if (now - lastPrint >= 1000) {         // one line per second
    lastPrint = now;
    Serial.print(now / 1000.0, 1);
    Serial.print(',');
    Serial.print(gyroOnly, 1);
    Serial.print(',');
    Serial.print(accelPitch, 1);
    Serial.print(',');
    Serial.println(fused, 1);
  }
}
```

The single 14-byte burst gets the whole sample at one instant.

## Common mistakes and a checklist

The failures below are the ones that cost beginners the most time:

- Never waking the MPU-6050: forget the PWR_MGMT_1 write and every reading is zero.
- Using the wrong address because AD0 is tied high, or because another device already owns 0x68.
- Swapping SDA and SCL, or forgetting that some bare modules have no pull-ups.
- Running I2C over long, loose wires next to motor cables, then blaming the sensor.
- Reading the two bytes of a value as unsigned, or in the wrong order, so a small negative number becomes a huge positive one.
- Mixing degrees and radians: `atan2` returns radians, and forgetting the conversion gives an angle that is wrong by a factor of about 57.
- Assuming `dt` instead of measuring it, especially once Serial prints or other work stretches the loop.
- Using a gyro axis or sign that does not match the accelerometer angle, so the two corrections point opposite ways.
- Mounting the IMU loosely, or right next to a vibrating motor, so the accelerometer measures the frame, not gravity.
- Feeding a 5 V HC-SR04 echo straight into an ESP32 input; use a divider.

Before you move on, you should be able to answer "yes" to these:

- I can find a new I2C device with the scanner and explain what a return value of 0 means.
- I can convert raw MPU-6050 registers to g and °/s using the datasheet sensitivity.
- I can explain why the gyro drifts and why the accelerometer is noisy, in one sentence each.
- I can write the complementary filter from memory and choose α for a given sample rate.
- I know how to measure and subtract the gyro bias at startup.

## Equal-stream KF versus complementary, without a bias oracle

The earlier complementary example stays. This addition compares gyro-only, accel-only, tuned complementary, a **scalar bias-adaptive complementary** variant, and a two-state KF on identical streams. It is not a full Mahony attitude implementation, EKF INS or an Allan-variance lab. Physical IMU characterization: **本页未运行**.

### Declared discrete noise model → Q/R

Units are degrees, deg/s, seconds throughout. Truth tilt is `12 sin(2πt/6) + 3 sin(2πt/2)` degrees,dt=0.02 s, 1500 updates. The synthetic accelerometer angle is `z=theta+na`,white sigma_a=1.5 deg; this **gravity-only angle model excludes translational acceleration**, vibration and scale error. It does not feed oracle truth during dynamic intervals. Gyro samples finite-difference true rate plus the previous sample's bias and independent white noise sigma_g=0.5 deg/s. Initial bias is an unknown draw with sigma_b0=0.5 deg/s; after each gyro reading it random-walks with increment SD `sigma_rw sqrt(dt)`,sigma_rw=0.03 deg/s/√s.

For x=[theta,bias], `F=[[1,−dt],[0,1]]`, control `[dt gyro,0]`, `H=[1,0]`. Discrete sample-held bias makes angle and bias process errors independent here: `Q0=diag((sigma_g dt)², sigma_rw² dt)=diag(0.000100,0.000018)`, `R0=sigma_a²=2.250000`. These are derived from the **declared synthetic noise model**, not guessed from held-out errors or measured on a device; this is not exact continuous-Brownian discretization with cross terms. Prediction is `x−=F x+B gyro`, `P−=F P Fᵀ+Q`; innovation `z−H x−`, gain `K=P−Hᵀ/(H P−Hᵀ+R)`, update `x=x−+K innovation`. The code expands the 2×2 covariance algebra without NumPy.

All filters start angle from the same first noisy accel reading. KF uses `P0=diag(R0,sigma_b0²)` and bias **prior mean 0**, not the sampled bias. **Neither filter knows the true bias**; `estimate()` has no truth/bias input. Truth is available only to the tuning objective and held-out scorer, never in the recursive update.

### Equal tuning effort, then freeze

Plain complementary predicts with gyro and mixes by α; its bias stays0. The adaptive variant uses the same innovation to update `bhat -= kb dt innovation` as well as mixing the angle. It is included so KF is not declared a winner merely because only it can estimate bias. Model capacity still differs and is disclosed.

Each tuned filter gets **9 candidates ×3 tuning seeds ×1500 updates =40500 recursive updates**, the same mean-squared-error objective,streams 201/202/203 and no extra search. Plain complementary α grid is 0.5,0.65,0.8,0.9,0.95,0.97,0.985,0.995,0.999. Adaptive grid is α in 0.9/0.97/0.995 crossed with kb in 0.01/0.05/0.2 s⁻². KF grid multiplies declared Q0 and R0 independently by 0.5/1/2 (3×3); it cannot fit an arbitrary covariance from eval truth. Selected settings are α=**0.95**,adaptive α=**0.97**,kb=**0.2**,KF multipliers **(1.0,1.0)**. This is equal candidate/sample-update budget, not equal floating-point cost or a continuous optimum.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Shared tilt-only sensor stream; no filter receives true gyro bias.
Units: angle deg, rate deg/s, time s. Synthetic gravity-only accelerometer
angle (no translational acceleration), white gyro/accel noise, bias random walk.
During each sample gyro = finite-difference true rate + previous bias + noise;
bias then takes an independent random-walk increment. Model for x=[angle,bias]:
F=[[1,-dt],[0,1]], control=[dt*gyro,0], H=[1,0].
Q0=diag((sigma_g*dt)^2, sigma_rw^2*dt), R0=sigma_a^2.
P0=diag(R0,sigma_b0^2); bias prior MEAN=0, not the sampled true bias.
KF tests 3x3 scale factors of the declared Q0,R0. Plain complementary tests
9 alphas. Bias-adaptive complementary tests 3x3 alpha,bias-gain settings.
Each gets 9 candidates x 3 tuning seeds x 1500 updates=40500 updates, same
squared-error objective. Tuning truth is training-only supervision; held-out
truth scores frozen filters. Model knowledge is shared, not oracle bias.
Model capacity differs: plain complementary has no bias state; adaptive/KF do.
This is not an EKF INS, Allan-variance characterization or MPU6050 measurement.
"""
import itertools
import math
import random
import statistics

DT, N = 0.02, 1500
SIGMA_G, SIGMA_A = 0.5, 1.5
SIGMA_B0, SIGMA_RW = 0.5, 0.03
TUNE = (201, 202, 203)
EVAL = (221, 222, 223, 224, 225)
GRIDS = {
    "complementary": tuple((a, 0.) for a in (.5, .65, .8, .9, .95, .97, .985, .995, .999)),
    "bias_complementary": tuple(itertools.product((.9, .97, .995), (.01, .05, .2))),
    "KF": tuple(itertools.product((.5, 1., 2.), repeat=2)),
}


def angle(t):
    return 12 * math.sin(2 * math.pi * t / 6) + 3 * math.sin(2 * math.pi * t / 2)


def sensors(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    bias = rng.gauss(0, SIGMA_B0)
    truth = tuple(angle(k * DT) for k in range(N + 1))
    accel = [truth[0] + rng.gauss(0, SIGMA_A)]
    gyro = []
    for prev, now in zip(truth, truth[1:]):
        gyro.append((now - prev) / DT + bias + rng.gauss(0, SIGMA_G))
        bias += rng.gauss(0, SIGMA_RW * math.sqrt(DT))
        accel.append(now + rng.gauss(0, SIGMA_A))
    return tuple(gyro), tuple(accel), truth


def estimate(kind, config, gyro, accel):
    # No truth or true-bias argument; all arms initialize from the same z0.
    a, bhat = accel[0], 0.
    out = [a]
    p00, p01, p11 = SIGMA_A**2, 0., SIGMA_B0**2
    for g, z in zip(gyro, accel[1:]):
        pred = a + DT * (g - bhat)
        innovation = z - pred
        if kind == "gyro":
            a = pred
        elif kind == "accel":
            a = z
        elif kind in ("complementary", "bias_complementary"):
            alpha, kb = config
            a = pred + (1 - alpha) * innovation
            bhat -= kb * DT * innovation
        else:
            qscale, rscale = config
            qtheta = qscale * (SIGMA_G * DT)**2
            qbias = qscale * SIGMA_RW**2 * DT
            r = rscale * SIGMA_A**2
            pp00 = p00 - 2 * DT * p01 + DT**2 * p11 + qtheta
            pp01 = p01 - DT * p11
            pp11 = p11 + qbias
            k0, k1 = pp00 / (pp00 + r), pp01 / (pp00 + r)
            a, bhat = pred + k0 * innovation, bhat + k1 * innovation
            p00, p01, p11 = (1-k0)*pp00, (1-k0)*pp01, pp11-k1*pp01
            assert p00 >= 0 and p11 >= 0 and p00*p11-p01*p01 > -1e-12
        out.append(a)
    return out


def mse(estimate_values, truth):
    return statistics.fmean((a-b)**2 for a, b in zip(estimate_values, truth))


def main():
    assert set(TUNE).isdisjoint(EVAL) and all(len(g) == 9 for g in GRIDS.values())
    tuning = [sensors(s) for s in TUNE]
    chosen = {}
    for kind, grid in GRIDS.items():
        chosen[kind] = min(grid, key=lambda cfg: statistics.fmean(
            mse(estimate(kind, cfg, g, z), truth) for g, z, truth in tuning))
    print(f"dt={DT} N={N}; sigma_g={SIGMA_G} deg/s sigma_a={SIGMA_A} deg sigma_b0={SIGMA_B0} deg/s sigma_rw={SIGMA_RW} deg/s/sqrt(s)")
    print(f"Q0_diag={(SIGMA_G*DT)**2:.6f},{SIGMA_RW**2*DT:.6f}; R0={SIGMA_A**2:.6f}; bias_prior_mean=0 (not truth)")
    print("tune_seeds=201,202,203; held_out=221..225; 9 candidates x 3 x 1500 = 40500 updates/filter")
    for kind, cfg in chosen.items():
        print(f"selected {kind} {cfg}")
    arms = ("gyro", "accel", "complementary", "bias_complementary", "KF")
    scores = {a: [] for a in arms}
    print("held_out_seed gyro_RMSE_deg accel_RMSE_deg complementary_RMSE_deg bias_complementary_RMSE_deg KF_RMSE_deg")
    for seed in EVAL:
        g, z, truth = sensors(seed)
        row = [math.sqrt(mse(estimate(a, chosen.get(a), g, z), truth)) for a in arms]
        for a, value in zip(arms, row):
            scores[a].append(value)
        print(str(seed) + " " + " ".join(f"{v:.6f}" for v in row))
    print("arm mean_RMSE_deg population_sd min max")
    for a, values in scores.items():
        print(f"{a} {statistics.fmean(values):.6f} {statistics.pstdev(values):.6f} {min(values):.6f} {max(values):.6f}")
    print("shared data/prior model; neither filter knows true bias; no universal winner; IMU hardware 本页未运行")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

## SPI modes: share the wire, change the sampling edge
SPI sends synchronous bits over SCK, COPI/MOSI and CIPO/MISO, with a CS line selecting a peripheral; CS is not an I2C address. The [Arduino SPI documentation](https://docs.arduino.cc/learn/communication/spi/) gives the following modes. **Leading** means the first transition away from idle; **trailing** returns to idle. CPOL sets idle level; CPHA=0 samples leading and launches on trailing (first bit must already be ready), CPHA=1 launches leading and samples trailing.

<Resource id="arduino-spi-modes" />

| mode | CPOL | CPHA | idle | sample | launch/output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPI_MODE0 | 0 | 0 | low | rising | falling |
| SPI_MODE1 | 0 | 1 | low | falling | rising |
| SPI_MODE2 | 1 | 0 | high | falling | rising |
| SPI_MODE3 | 1 | 1 | high | rising | falling |

A device datasheet, not an assumption that every sensor uses Mode 0, decides mode, CS setup/hold, word length, bit order and allowable clock. **No device MHz is specified here.** `SPISettings` separates clock, MSBFIRST/LSBFIRST and mode; `beginTransaction` configures the bus but does not replace the required CS assertion/deassertion. Devices may require CS to stay active across command, address and data rather than toggling per byte.

### Deterministic shared-edge experiment, not analog timing validation

`spi_mode_mismatch_toy.py` constructs one physical MOSI/SCK stream for each transmitter mode and bit order; **matched**, **CPOL-wrong** and **CPHA-wrong** receivers inspect that **same wire**, same payload and CS. The payload is **A5 00 FF 3C 81 01 96 69**, 64 bits. Both MSB-first and LSB-first are tested. CPOL-wrong (`mode XOR 2`) and CPHA-wrong (`mode XOR 1`) select the same opposite physical edge in this ideal decoder, so those rows coincide; this is not two independent noise realizations.

All times are **synthetic arbitrary units**, not ns or MHz: half-period=1, launch-to-MOSI propagation=0.2, receiver capture at edge+0.1 or edge+0.3. CPHA0 preloads the first bit; CPHA1 starts from declared idle data 0. A matched receiver samples after a stable half-cycle in both cases. A wrong receiver samples near launch: before the data change it may get the old bit, after it may get the next bit. These deterministic aperture cases illustrate a violated timing contract, **not a model of real metastability or a guarantee of a particular wrong byte**. A mismatch can therefore pass some patterns, or even all patterns under one synthetic aperture, without being safe to use.

Report **BER = wrong bits / 64** and the received first byte (sent A5). For MSB-first, TX0 with aperture0.3 yields **27/64=0.421875**, first byte **4A** in both wrong-mode arms (left-shift-like); TX1 with aperture0.1 gives the same BER but first byte **52** (right-shift-like). TX2/3 repeat those edge relationships. Matched BER is 0 for every mode/order/aperture. On isolated bytes at TX1/MSB/aperture0.1/RX0, **00→00** passes but **FF→7F**, **A5→52** and **96→4B** do not: only testing zeros is inadequate. Byte boundaries inside one CS frame carry the preceding bit; do not infer isolated-byte behavior from a continuous stream.

CS-high clocks before/after the frame are ignored. This toy assumes whole 8-bit words within one CS frame and rejects a partial final word; that is a toy framing rule, not a universal SPI protocol. Decode a correctly sampled 96 with the wrong bit order and it becomes 69: a correct edge alone does not fix endianness. Real SPI devices, electrical setup/hold and scope measurements: **本页未运行**. This is not I2C rise-time or a redo of the existing sensor filters.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Deterministic SPI edge surrogate; no device clock rate or analog validation.
Shared physical MOSI/SCK/CS per TX mode/order and aperture case for all RX arms.
Synthetic time units: half period=1, propagation=0.2, apertures=0.1/0.3.
Wrong-edge capture coincides with launch; which bit arrives is timing-dependent,
not a prediction of real metastability. Both clean mismatches and shifted garbage
are intentional. Eight-bit words in one active-low CS frame; COPI/MOSI only.
"""
from bisect import bisect_right

PAYLOAD = bytes((0xA5,0x00,0xFF,0x3C,0x81,0x01,0x96,0x69))
DELAY = .2
APERTURES = (.1,.3)
ORDERS = ("MSB","LSB")


def bits_of(data, order):
    positions=range(7,-1,-1) if order=="MSB" else range(8)
    return tuple((byte>>bit)&1 for byte in data for bit in positions)


def sample_rising(mode):
    cpol,cpha=divmod(mode,2)
    return (cpol==0) == (cpha==0)


def waveform(data, mode, order):
    cpol,cpha=divmod(mode,2)
    bits=bits_of(data,order)
    # Unselected clocks before/after the CS frame must not enter RX framing.
    edges=[(-2.,True,False),(-1.,False,False)]
    changes=[(-10.,0)]
    if cpha==0:
        changes.append((0.,bits[0]))  # First bit preloaded before leading edge at t=1.
    for j,bit in enumerate(bits):
        lead,trail=2*j+1.,2*j+2.
        edges.extend(((lead,cpol==0,True),(trail,cpol==1,True)))
        launch=lead if cpha else trail
        value=bit if cpha else (bits[j+1] if j+1<len(bits) else 0)
        changes.append((launch+DELAY,value))
    edges.extend(((2*len(bits)+1.,True,False),(2*len(bits)+2.,False,False)))
    return tuple(edges),tuple(changes)


def receive(wire, mode, order, aperture):
    edges,changes=wire
    times=tuple(t for t,_ in changes)
    captured=[]
    for time,rising,selected in edges:
        if selected and rising==sample_rising(mode):
            index=bisect_right(times,time+aperture)-1
            captured.append(changes[index][1])
    if len(captured)%8:
        raise ValueError("partial 8-bit CS frame")
    positions=range(7,-1,-1) if order=="MSB" else range(8)
    return bytes(sum(bit<<place for bit,place in zip(captured[j:j+8],positions))
                 for j in range(0,len(captured),8))


def bit_errors(expected, actual):
    if len(expected)!=len(actual):
        raise ValueError("framing length differs")
    return sum((a^b).bit_count() for a,b in zip(expected,actual))


def main():
    print("SPI synthetic edge schedule: half_period=1 prop_delay=0.2 aperture=0.1/0.3 time units")
    print("shared payload=A5 00 FF 3C 81 01 96 69; 64 bits; one active-low CS frame")
    print("TX_mode order aperture RX_arm RX_mode errors BER first_byte")
    for mode in range(4):
        for order in ORDERS:
            wire=waveform(PAYLOAD,mode,order)
            for aperture in APERTURES:
                for name,rx in (("matched",mode),("CPOL_wrong",mode^2),("CPHA_wrong",mode^1)):
```

## Magnetometer hard-iron and soft-iron calibration

Earlier sections already warned that an IMU “is not a compass”: gyro integration drifts, and a raw magnetometer reading is not a heading until the local field is cleaned up. Two non-time-varying distortions dominate indoors and inside a robot frame. **Hard-iron** bias shifts the centre of the measurement cloud (a permanent offset from magnetized material on the same body). **Soft-iron** stretch turns the sphere into an ellipsoid (or the horizontal circle into an ellipse) by deflecting the existing field. This section calibrates those two. It is **not** a redo of the KF-versus-complementary comparison, and it is **not** another “IMU is not a compass” prose paragraph alone.

<Resource id="vn-hsi-calibration" />
<Resource id="wiki-magnetometer" />

**Sources actually opened.**

- **VectorNav — Magnetometer Hard & Soft Iron Calibration:** hard-iron distortions from objects that produce a field cause a **permanent bias** (circle centre shift; their figure uses a 200 / 100 mGauss example). Soft-iron distortions **stretch** the sphere into an ellipsoid. Calibration maps the biased ellipsoid back to a sphere centred at the origin via `m_c = S_I (m̃ − b_HI)`, with 2D (yaw circles) or 3D motion.
- **Wikipedia — Magnetometer:** absolute magnetometers need internal calibration or known constants; relative devices measure against an uncalibrated baseline. Numbers 以原文为准. A real mag turntable / AHRS certification: **本页未运行**.

### Bias versus stretch

In the horizontal plane a clean field of fixed magnitude traces a circle centred at the origin as you yaw. Hard-iron only moves that centre. Soft-iron changes the radii along different axes. Heading from `atan2(my, mx)` on the raw cloud therefore carries a yaw-dependent error until both are removed.

**Scope.** Geographic declination and platform tilt / magnetic dip are out of scope; heading here is measured against the synthetic horizontal `B_true` only.

### Fair setup

`mag_hard_soft_iron_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. Every arm shares the **same** true horizontal field `B_true = (1, 0)`, the **same** 72 yaw samples (5° steps), the **same** diagonal soft stretch `S = (1.35, 0.72)`, the **same** hard bias `b = (0.35, −0.22)`, and the **same** per-seed Gaussian noise (σ = 0.03):

| Arm | Correction on the shared samples |
|---|---|
| A_raw | none |
| B_hard_only | subtract the sample-mean bias |
| C_hard_soft | bias subtract + diagonal soft scale to equalize axis RMS |

Metrics: mean absolute heading error versus the undistorted path, and residual radius RMS after mapping toward a circle. Results are five-seed mean±sd. The toy fits only a diagonal soft matrix; it is not a full 3×3 lab calibration.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Synthetic magnetometer hard/soft-iron calibration toy. Stdlib only.

Shared true field magnitude and shared yaw rotation path for every arm.
Measurement model (VectorNav-style): m_tilde = S_soft @ R(yaw) @ B_true + b_hard + noise.
Arms differ only in post-processing of the SAME samples:
  A_raw          heading from uncorrected XY
  B_hard_only    subtract estimated hard-iron bias (sample mean)
  C_hard_soft    bias subtract + diagonal soft-iron scale (axis std → unit circle)

Metrics: heading absolute error (deg) and residual radius RMS after mapping to
a unit circle. ≥5 noise seeds. Not an AHRS certification. Mag hardware: 本页未运行.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

B_TRUE = (1.0, 0.0)  # horizontal field in body-fixed world frame, synthetic units
# Permanent hard-iron bias (sphere/circle centre shift)
B_HARD = (0.35, -0.22)
# Soft-iron stretch (diagonal ellipsoid → circle stretch); off-diagonals omitted for clarity
S_SOFT = (1.35, 0.72)  # sx, sy
N_YAWS = 72  # 5° steps, full rotation
NOISE_SIGMA = 0.03
SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
ARMS = ("A_raw", "B_hard_only", "C_hard_soft")


def wrap180(deg: float) -> float:
    while deg > 180.0:
        deg -= 360.0
    while deg <= -180.0:
        deg += 360.0
    return deg


def heading_deg(mx: float, my: float) -> float:
    return math.degrees(math.atan2(my, mx))


def true_path() -> list[tuple[float, float, float]]:
    """Return list of (yaw_deg, mx_world_rot, my_world_rot) without distortion."""
    rows = []
    for i in range(N_YAWS):
        yaw = 360.0 * i / N_YAWS
        c = math.cos(math.radians(yaw))
        s = math.sin(math.radians(yaw))
        # rotate true field into sensor frame as yaw changes (2D)
        mx = c * B_TRUE[0] + s * B_TRUE[1]
        my = -s * B_TRUE[0] + c * B_TRUE[1]
        rows.append((yaw, mx, my))
    return rows


def measure(path, seed: int) -> list[tuple[float, float, float]]:
    rng = random.Random(20_000 + seed)
    out = []
    sx, sy = S_SOFT
    bx, by = B_HARD
    for yaw, mx, my in path:
        # soft stretch then hard bias + noise (VectorNav: m_c = S(m - b) form inverted)
        tx = sx * mx + bx + rng.gauss(0.0, NOISE_SIGMA)
        ty = sy * my + by + rng.gauss(0.0, NOISE_SIGMA)
        out.append((yaw, tx, ty))
    return out


def calibrate_bias(samples):
    mx = sum(p[1] for p in samples) / len(samples)
    my = sum(p[2] for p in samples) / len(samples)
    return mx, my


def calibrate_soft(samples, bias):
    """Diagonal soft scale: map demeaned axes to equal RMS (unit-ish circle)."""
    bx, by = bias
    xs = [p[1] - bx for p in samples]
    ys = [p[2] - by for p in samples]
    rx = math.sqrt(sum(v * v for v in xs) / len(xs)) or 1.0
    ry = math.sqrt(sum(v * v for v in ys) / len(ys)) or 1.0
    # target radius = geometric mean
    target = math.sqrt(rx * ry)
    return target / rx, target / ry


def evaluate(samples, arm: str) -> tuple[float, float, float, float]:
    """Return mean |heading err|, sd, residual radius RMS, residual radius sd."""
    bias = calibrate_bias(samples)
    soft = calibrate_soft(samples, bias)
    errs = []
    radii = []
    for yaw, mx, my in samples:
        if arm == "A_raw":
            cx, cy = mx, my
        elif arm == "B_hard_only":
            cx, cy = mx - bias[0], my - bias[1]
        else:
            cx = soft[0] * (mx - bias[0])
            cy = soft[1] * (my - bias[1])
        est = heading_deg(cx, cy)
        # true heading of horizontal field in sensor frame equals yaw for B_TRUE=(1,0)
        # with our rotation convention: atan2(my,mx) of undistorted = -yaw
        true_h = heading_deg(
            math.cos(math.radians(yaw)) * B_TRUE[0] + math.sin(math.radians(yaw)) * B_TRUE[1],
            -math.sin(math.radians(yaw)) * B_TRUE[0] + math.cos(math.radians(yaw)) * B_TRUE[1],
        )
        errs.append(abs(wrap180(est - true_h)))
        radii.append(math.hypot(cx, cy))
    mu_e = sum(errs) / len(errs)
    # radius residual vs mean radius
    mu_r = sum(radii) / len(radii)
    resid = [abs(r - mu_r) for r in radii]
    rms = math.sqrt(sum(r * r for r in resid) / len(resid))
    sd_e = math.sqrt(sum((e - mu_e) ** 2 for e in errs) / (len(errs) - 1))
    mu_abs = sum(resid) / len(resid)
    sd_r = math.sqrt(sum((r - mu_abs) ** 2 for r in resid) / (len(resid) - 1))
    return mu_e, sd_e, rms, sd_r


def mean_sd(xs: list[float]) -> tuple[float, float]:
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    var = sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1)
    return mu, math.sqrt(var)


def main() -> None:
    path = true_path()
    print(
        "Synthetic mag hard/soft-iron toy; shared field/path; not an AHRS certification"
    )
    print(
        f"B_true={B_TRUE} b_hard={B_HARD} S_diag={S_SOFT} "
        f"N_yaws={N_YAWS} noise_sigma={NOISE_SIGMA} seeds={len(SEEDS)}"
    )
    print(
        "model: m_tilde = S_soft * R(yaw)*B_true + b_hard + noise "
        "(VectorNav hard=centre shift, soft=ellipse stretch)"
    )
    print(
        "arm mean_abs_heading_err_deg heading_err_sd_deg "
        "radius_resid_rms radius_resid_sd (mean+-sd over seeds)"
    )
    for arm in ARMS:
        cols_acc = [[] for _ in range(4)]
        for seed in SEEDS:
            samples = measure(path, seed)
            vals = evaluate(samples, arm)
            for i, v in enumerate(vals):
                cols_acc[i].append(v)
        print(
            f"{arm} "
            + " ".join(f"{mean_sd(c)[0]:.4f}+-{mean_sd(c)[1]:.4f}" for c in cols_acc)
        )
    print(
        "No winner declared beyond the defined metric: hard-only recenters; "
        "hard+soft also un-stretches; raw keeps both errors"
    )
    print("Mag turntable / real IMU: 本页未运行; VectorNav/Wikipedia definitions 以原文为准")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

## I²C clock stretching: when the target holds SCL low

<Resource id="nxp-um10204" />

<Resource id="wiki-i2c" />

<Resource id="idf-i2c" />

The primary source is NXP's **I²C-bus specification and user manual, UM10204** (Rev. 7.0, 1 October 2021, §3.1.9 and Table 3; 以文档为准). Clock stretching pauses a transaction by holding SCL **LOW**; the transaction cannot continue until the line is released HIGH again. It is **optional**: most target devices have no SCL driver and cannot stretch at all, and if no target in a system can stretch, the controller need not be designed to handle it. On the byte level a target may hold SCL LOW after receiving and acknowledging a byte, forcing the controller into a wait state until it is ready for the next byte; on the bit level a device can slow the bus clock by extending each clock LOW period; in Hs-mode only the byte level is allowed. UM10204 Table 11 lists the Standard-mode minimums t_LOW = 4.7 µs and t_HIGH = 4.0 µs (以文档为准).

**Clock stretching** is a **flow-control** mechanism for I²C targets (Wikipedia, I²C, kept as a secondary source; 以原文为准). An addressed target may hold **SCL** low after receiving or sending a byte, to say it is not ready yet. Because the lines are open-drain, SCL simply stays low, and the controller **must wait until it observes SCL actually going high**, then a minimal time (4 μs for standard 100 kbit/s I²C) before pulling SCL low again. The same page notes that stretching usually happens around the acknowledgement bit, that some controllers do not support it at all (often labelled *two-wire interface*), and that pure I²C sets **no** limit on how long a target may stretch, while SMBus does.

**On ESP32 with ESP-IDF** (I²C driver page, stable docs; 以文档为准): the master-device field `scl_wait_us` sets the SCL await time in µs; the page warns that it should not be very small because a slave may stretch for a long time (it says even 12 ms is possible), 0 means the default register value, and one example sets `scl_wait_us = 20000`. Each master transfer call (`i2c_master_transmit`, `i2c_master_receive`, `i2c_master_transmit_receive`, `i2c_master_probe`) takes `xfer_timeout_ms`, where -1 means wait forever, and returns `ESP_ERR_TIMEOUT` when the operation takes longer. The ESP32 version of the page also says that the **classic ESP32 I²C controller does not support clock stretching when operating as a slave**, so the application has to match speeds between master and slave itself; the ESP32-S3 version of the page has no such sentence and documents a slave stretch callback (`i2c_slave_stretch_callback_t`) instead. The toy's TIMEOUT_US = 200.0 us is a declared toy parameter, **not** an ESP-IDF default, and no ESP32 was run (本页未运行).

This section is **not** the I²C rise-time / pull-up / bus-capacitance toy in the Arduino I/O lesson: here edges are ideal and the only reason SCL stays low is the target. It is also **not** the I²C scanner above, and **not** RS-485.

<Formula label="Deaf controller, early sample">bit j is early iff j T_BIT + T_HIGH / 2 < stretch</Formula>

<Formula label="Timeout">abort iff stretch > TIMEOUT_US</Formula>

**Worked example — a labelled hand calculation, also printed by the toy.** T_LOW = 5.0 us and T_HIGH = 5.0 us give T_BIT = 10.0 us, so 1 / 10.0 us = 100.0 kHz, and T_HIGH = 5.0 us meets the 4 us standard-mode minimum quoted above (以原文为准); UM10204 Table 11 gives the same 4.0 µs t_HIGH minimum, and T_LOW = 5.0 us also meets its 4.7 µs t_LOW minimum (以文档为准). One unstretched 16-byte read takes 16 × 9 × 10.0 us = 1440.0 us. A deaf controller samples bit j of a stretched byte at j × T_BIT + T_LOW + T_HIGH/2; the target releases SCL at T_LOW + stretch. The comparison is **strict**: a stretch of 2.5000 us gives 0 early bits (the sample lands exactly at the release), 2.5001 us gives 1, 20.0000 us gives 2 and 60.0000 us gives 6. The timeout is strict too: a stretch of exactly 200.0000 us is still accepted (aborts = 0), 200.0010 us aborts (aborts = 1). By hand (手算, not a separate print): an abort on the second byte loses 15 of 16 bytes, 15 / 16 = 0.9375.

**No integration step here.** The toy sums event times; there is no ODE and no step-size stability criterion.

### Fair setup

`i2c_clock_stretch_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** ideal open-drain edges (no RC rise time); the target stretches the first clock of every byte after the first, while it prepares the next byte; a bit sampled strictly before the target releases SCL reads a garbage bit instead of the data bit; a byte with any wrong bit is a byte error; an aborted transaction loses its remaining bytes. T_LOW, T_HIGH and TIMEOUT_US = 200.0 us are declared toy parameters, not values from a controller datasheet.

```python
"""I2C clock stretching: a deaf controller vs one that waits for SCL high.

Synthetic bit-timing model, stdlib only.  Not a logic-analyser capture, not
the I2C rise-time / pull-up signal-integrity toy (arduino-io), not the I2C
scanner sketch and not RS-485.  Real bus / analyser: 本页未运行.
Wikipedia I2C statements 以原文为准.

What the Wikipedia I2C page says (and what this toy keeps):
  Clock stretching is a flow-control mechanism for targets: an addressed
  target may hold SCL low after receiving or sending a byte.  Because the
  lines are open-drain, SCL stays low; the controller must wait until it
  observes SCL actually going high, then a minimal time (4 us for standard
  100 kbit/s) before pulling SCL low again.  Some controllers do not support
  clock stretching (often labelled two-wire interface).  Pure I2C gives no
  stretch limit; SMBus does.
Edges are ideal here: RC rise time is not modelled (that is the arduino-io
rise-time toy), so the only reason SCL stays low is the target.

Common random numbers: per (regime, seed) one byte schedule, one stretch per
byte and one garbage bit stream, shared by every arm.  Arms:
  A_ignore  : releases SCL and samples after T_HIGH/2 on a fixed schedule,
              never reads SCL back (a controller without stretch support)
  B_wait    : waits until SCL is observed high, then samples (legal I2C)
  C_timeout : like B, but aborts the transaction when SCL stays low strictly
              longer than TIMEOUT_US (equality is still accepted)
The arms differ only in how the controller treats SCL.  The target stretches
the first clock of every byte after the first (it is preparing the next
byte, after the ACK).  A bit sampled strictly before the target releases SCL
reads the garbage bit instead of the data bit.  No integration step: event
times are summed, no ODE.

Regimes and the timeout were written before the first run:
  no_stretch       : target never stretches - A, B, C tie
  moderate_stretch : stretch 20..60 us per byte, below the timeout
                     - B consistent fewer byte errors than A; C ties B
  overtime_stretch : stretch 300..600 us per byte, above TIMEOUT_US=200 us
                     - C consistent fail (abort); B succeeds slowly; keep
"""
import math
import random

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9

# Declared model parameters, written before the first run (toy values).
T_LOW_US = 5.0        # us, controller low phase
T_HIGH_US = 5.0       # us, controller high phase (>= 4 us minimum, 以原文为准)
T_BIT_US = T_LOW_US + T_HIGH_US   # 10 us -> 100 kHz
CLOCKS_PER_BYTE = 9   # 8 data bits + ACK
N_BYTES = 16          # bytes per read transaction
N_TX = 20             # transactions per seed
TIMEOUT_US = 200.0    # us, C's SCL-low timeout (toy; pure I2C sets no limit)

REGIMES = {
    "no_stretch": (0.0, 0.0),
    "moderate_stretch": (20.0, 60.0),
    "overtime_stretch": (300.0, 600.0),
}
ARMS = ("A_ignore", "B_wait", "C_timeout")


def shared_bus(regime, seed):
    """Common random numbers: data, stretch and garbage bits, used by every arm."""
    rng = random.Random(f"{regime}/{seed}")
    lo, hi = REGIMES[regime]
    txs = []
    for _ in range(N_TX):
        data = [rng.randrange(256) for _ in range(N_BYTES)]
        stretch = [0.0] + [rng.uniform(lo, hi) if hi > 0.0 else 0.0 for _ in range(N_BYTES - 1)]
        garbage = [rng.randrange(256) for _ in range(N_BYTES)]
        txs.append({"data": data, "stretch": stretch, "garbage": garbage})
    return txs


def early_bits(stretch_us):
    """Bits a deaf controller samples strictly before the target releases SCL.

    Bit j of the byte is sampled at j*T_BIT + T_LOW + T_HIGH/2 after the byte
    starts; SCL is released at T_LOW + stretch.  Early iff sample < release.
    """
    return [j for j in range(8) if j * T_BIT_US + T_HIGH_US / 2.0 < stretch_us]


def read_byte_ignore(true_byte, garbage_byte, stretch_us):
    out = true_byte
    early = early_bits(stretch_us)
    for j in early:
        bit = 7 - j  # MSB first
        mask = 1 << bit
        out = (out & ~mask) | (garbage_byte & mask)
    return out, len(early)


def run_tx(arm, tx, timeout_us=TIMEOUT_US):
    t = 0.0
    errors = early = lost = 0
    aborted = 0
    for k in range(N_BYTES):
        s = tx["stretch"][k]
        if arm == "A_ignore":
            got, e = read_byte_ignore(tx["data"][k], tx["garbage"][k], s)
            early += e
            errors += int(got != tx["data"][k])
            t += CLOCKS_PER_BYTE * T_BIT_US
            continue
        if arm == "C_timeout" and s > timeout_us:   # strict: equality accepted
            t += timeout_us
            lost += N_BYTES - k
            aborted = 1
            break
        t += CLOCKS_PER_BYTE * T_BIT_US + s
    return {"time_us": t, "errors": errors, "early": early, "lost": lost, "aborted": aborted}


def run_seed(regime, seed, arm, timeout_us=TIMEOUT_US):
    agg = {"time_us": 0.0, "errors": 0, "early": 0, "lost": 0, "aborted": 0}
    for tx in shared_bus(regime, seed):
        r = run_tx(arm, tx, timeout_us)
        for key in agg:
            agg[key] += r[key]
    total = N_TX * N_BYTES
    agg["bad_rate"] = (agg["errors"] + agg["lost"]) / total
    agg["bus_ms"] = agg["time_us"] / 1000.0
    return agg


def mean_sd(xs):
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x):
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-4:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def fmt_pair(m, s):
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}"


def verdict(xa, xb):
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


def table(regime, timeout_us=TIMEOUT_US):
    return {arm: [run_seed(regime, seed, arm, timeout_us) for seed in SEEDS] for arm in ARMS}


def col(res, arm, key):
    return [float(r[key]) for r in res[arm]]


def main():
    print("Synthetic I2C clock-stretch toy; shared byte schedule; not an analyser capture")
    print(f"seeds={len(SEEDS)} T_LOW={T_LOW_US:.1f} us T_HIGH={T_HIGH_US:.1f} us T_BIT={T_BIT_US:.1f} us "
          f"CLOCKS_PER_BYTE={CLOCKS_PER_BYTE} N_BYTES={N_BYTES} N_TX={N_TX} TIMEOUT_US={TIMEOUT_US:.1f} (toy)")
    print("No integration step: summed event times, no time-step criterion")
    print("Regimes and TIMEOUT_US were written before the first run")
    print(f"hand_calc bit rate: 1/T_BIT = 1/{T_BIT_US:.1f} us = {1000.0 / T_BIT_US:.1f} kHz; "
          f"T_HIGH={T_HIGH_US:.1f} us >= 4 us standard-mode minimum (以原文为准)")
    print(f"hand_calc unstretched transaction: {N_BYTES}*{CLOCKS_PER_BYTE}*{T_BIT_US:.1f} us = "
          f"{N_BYTES * CLOCKS_PER_BYTE * T_BIT_US:.1f} us")
    print("early_rule: deaf controller samples bit j at j*T_BIT+T_LOW+T_HIGH/2; early iff that is strictly before "
          "release at T_LOW+stretch")
    for s in (2.5, 2.5001, 20.0, 60.0):
        print(f"  stretch={s:.4f} us -> early bits {len(early_bits(s))}")
    print(f"timeout_rule: C aborts iff stretch > TIMEOUT_US (strict); stretch={TIMEOUT_US:.4f} us aborts="
          f"{int(TIMEOUT_US > TIMEOUT_US)}; stretch={TIMEOUT_US + 0.001:.4f} us aborts={int(TIMEOUT_US + 0.001 > TIMEOUT_US)}")
    for regime, (lo, hi) in REGIMES.items():
        res = table(regime)
        print(f"{regime} (stretch {lo:.1f}..{hi:.1f} us per byte after the first; {N_TX}x{N_BYTES} bytes per seed):")
        for arm in ARMS:
            print(f"  {arm} bad_rate {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, arm, 'bad_rate')))} "
                  f"byte_errors {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, arm, 'errors')))} lost {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, arm, 'lost')))} "
                  f"aborts {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, arm, 'aborted')))} early_samples {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, arm, 'early')))} "
                  f"bus_ms {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, arm, 'bus_ms')))}")
        print(f"  paired A-B bad_rate: {verdict(col(res, 'A_ignore', 'bad_rate'), col(res, 'B_wait', 'bad_rate'))}")
        print(f"  paired C-B bad_rate: {verdict(col(res, 'C_timeout', 'bad_rate'), col(res, 'B_wait', 'bad_rate'))}")
        print(f"  paired C-B aborts: {verdict(col(res, 'C_timeout', 'aborted'), col(res, 'B_wait', 'aborted'))}")
        print(f"  paired B-A bus_ms: {verdict(col(res, 'B_wait', 'bus_ms'), col(res, 'A_ignore', 'bus_ms'))}")
        print(f"  paired A-B early_samples: {verdict(col(res, 'A_ignore', 'early'), col(res, 'B_wait', 'early'))}")
    for regime, alt in (("overtime_stretch", 1000.0), ("moderate_stretch", 50.0)):
        base = table(regime)
        res = table(regime, alt)
        print(f"alternative TIMEOUT_US={alt:.1f} on {regime} (same seeds):")
        print(f"  C_timeout bad_rate@alt {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, 'C_timeout', 'bad_rate')))} "
              f"aborts@alt {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(res, 'C_timeout', 'aborted')))} "
              f"(default bad_rate {fmt_pair(*mean_sd(col(base, 'C_timeout', 'bad_rate')))})")
        print(f"  paired C-B bad_rate@alt: {verdict(col(res, 'C_timeout', 'bad_rate'), col(res, 'B_wait', 'bad_rate'))}")
    print("No winner declared: without stretching all three tie; with stretching only a controller that waits for SCL "
          "reads correctly, and a timeout shorter than the target's stretch turns a slow read into a failed one")
    print("Real bus / analyser: 本页未运行; Wikipedia statements 以原文为准")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### Read the regimes

How the labels are used on this page, in this order: **tie: identical on every seed** only when the paired per-seed difference is exactly zero on every seed; **numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie** when |paired mean| and its sd are both below 1e-9 (no sign count is printed on such a line); **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** when |paired mean| ≤ sd; otherwise a **consistent** gap (|paired mean| > sd), called **small but consistent gap, not a tie** when |m| is under 20 % of the smaller arm mean and **clear gap, not a tie** otherwise. Every consistent line carries a same-sign count over the 5 seeds.

- **no_stretch.** Every paired line is a **tie: identical on every seed**: no early samples, no bad bytes, and all three arms spend 28.8000 ms on the bus. Without stretching, a controller that ignores SCL is indistinguishable from one that waits.
- **moderate_stretch.** A samples early 1281.0000±15.3134 times per seed and gets a bad-byte rate of 0.8681±0.0111. Paired A−B bad_rate is 0.8681±0.0111, a **clear gap, not a tie** (same sign on all 5 seeds). B reads every byte correctly and pays for it with bus time: paired B−A bus_ms is 12.0501±0.1307 (40.8501 ms against 28.8000 ms), also **clear** (same sign on all 5 seeds). C never reaches its timeout here, so paired C−B bad_rate is a **tie: identical on every seed**.
- **overtime_stretch.** Every stretch is longer than the 200.0 us timeout. C aborts all 20 transactions per seed: paired C−B bad_rate is 0.9375±0.0000 and paired C−B aborts 20.0000±0.0000, both a **clear gap, not a tie** (same sign on all 5 seeds). **Keep** this failure. B still reads every byte, but paired B−A bus_ms grows to 135.1400±0.7771; A's paired A−B bad_rate is 0.9338±0.0041 (**clear**, same sign on all 5 seeds) because it samples all 8 data bits of every stretched byte early.

The equal-budget held-out IMU comparison derives KF Q/R from its declared noise model, tunes complementary alternatives separately, and gives no arm the true bias.

**Key points**

- I2C: SDA and SCL, 7-bit addresses, open-drain lines with 4.7–10 kΩ pull-ups, 100 or 400 kHz; on the Uno SDA is A4 and SCL is A5.
- MPU-6050 at 0x68 (0x69 with AD0 high): WHO_AM_I (0x75) reads 0x68, and writing 0x00 to PWR_MGMT_1 (0x6B) wakes it.
- Raw data are signed 16-bit two's complement, high byte first: ±2 g gives 32768 / 2 = 16384 LSB/g, ±250 °/s gives 32768 / 250 ≈ 131 LSB/(°/s).
- Accelerometer pitch θ = atan2(ax, az) is absolute but noisy; the integrated gyro is smooth but drifts by bias × time (1.5 °/s → 90° in 60 s).
- Complementary filter: angle = α × (angle + rate × dt) + (1 − α) × accelAngle; α = 0.98 at 100 Hz gives τ ≈ 0.49 s and a 0.735° offset from a 1.5 °/s bias.
- Remove the bias at the source: average the gyro for 1–2 s at rest and subtract it before integrating.
