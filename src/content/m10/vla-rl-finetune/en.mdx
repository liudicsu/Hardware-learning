## Why RL after SFT

SFT copies the data, mistakes included, and stops at the level of the demos. RL can push past them, but a multi-billion-parameter VLA is expensive to explore with, and a sparse reward gives it little to go on. Four families of answers have appeared; they differ in **what is trained** and **where the signal comes from**.

| family | example | what is trained | learning signal | where the paper ran it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| outcome-reward RL on the whole VLA | SimpleVLA-RL | all VLA weights | binary task success per rollout | simulation benchmarks, then real-world tests |
| <Term id="advantage-conditioning">advantage conditioning</Term> | π*0.6 / RECAP | the VLA, conditioned on "was this better than expected" | a learned value function | real robots |
| <Term id="residual-policy">residual policy</Term> on a frozen base | ResiP, Policy Decorator | a small correction network | sparse reward, PPO or SAC | mostly simulation |
| reward-model-weighted imitation | LeRobot SARM + RA-BC | the policy, with per-frame weights | predicted task progress | your own dataset |

## Outcome-reward RL: SimpleVLA-RL

SimpleVLA-RL (Li et al., 2025) runs large-scale RL on the whole VLA, built on the veRL framework, with a GRPO-style objective and a binary reward: the rollout succeeded or it did not. The base comes from the OpenVLA-OFT family; for sampling convenience, however, SimpleVLA-RL switches to discrete action tokens + cross-entropy (a LLaMA2 output head, single view), not the official L1 continuous-action OpenVLA-OFT (§4.1). Official OpenVLA-OFT (parallel decoding, action chunks, continuous actions, an L1 loss) reports in its abstract that LIBERO average success rises from 76.5 % to 97.1 % with 26× faster action generation. The SimpleVLA-RL abstract reports state-of-the-art results on LIBERO, beating π0 on RoboTwin 1.0 and 2.0, and a new behaviour during RL that the authors call "pushcut" — for example pushing an object on RoboTwin2.0 tasks such as move can pot and place a2b, instead of the grasp the demos showed. The paper reports that on 5 RoboTwin2.0 tasks, a base without trajectory fine-tuning has 0 success, and outcome-reward RL alone cannot improve it (§6.2/Tab.7); after fine-tuning with 1 demo per task, RL then lifts performance sharply (LIBERO-Long 17.3 %→91.7 %, §5.1). Per the paper. All measured on the authors' GPU cluster, not run here.

Intuition for GRPO: sample a group of rollouts for the same prompt, update the policy with group-normalised advantages; if a group is all-success or all-fail, advantages are zero, there is no gradient, and that group is dropped. Related methods include ConRFT, RIPT-VLA and RLinf (one-line reading links below; no numbers).

## Advantage conditioning: π*0.6 and RECAP

Physical Intelligence's π*0.6 post describes RECAP, "RL with Experience & Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies". A value function predicts how many steps remain until the task is done; from it, each step in the data gets an advantage (better or worse than expected). Instead of throwing bad data away, the VLA is trained on **all** of it, conditioned on the advantage, and at run time is asked for the "better than expected" behaviour. Data comes from demonstrations, the robot's own autonomous attempts and expert corrections. The post reports that this more than doubles throughput on some of the hardest tasks and can cut failure rates by 2× or more, with examples such as making espresso from 5:30 am to 11:30 pm and folding laundry. π0.6 itself uses a 5B vision-language backbone with an action expert. These are the company's reported results, not run here.

## Residual policies: ResiP and Policy Decorator

Here the big model stays frozen and a small network learns a correction on top of its action:

- **ResiP** (Ankile et al., 2024) keeps a chunked behaviour-cloning model (a diffusion policy) fixed and trains a closed-loop, per-step residual with PPO on a sparse reward, keeping the residual small. On its simulated furniture-assembly tasks it reports large gains from 50 demos (for example one_leg rising from 54 % to 98 % under low randomization ±1.5 cm, best checkpoint, simulation; medium randomization 26 %→76 %); per the paper.
- **Policy Decorator** (Yuan et al., 2024) makes the residual model-agnostic and trains it with SAC; it bounds the residual's size and gradually widens how often the residual is used during exploration, so the base policy's good behaviour is not destroyed early. Its experiments use ManiSkill and Adroit with Behaviour Transformer and Diffusion Policy bases.

Residuals are attractive for hobby hardware because the trained part is small, but the papers' results are mostly in simulation.

## Reward-model-weighted imitation: SARM + RA-BC

The cheapest step on your own data is not RL at all. LeRobot's SARM page describes a stage-aware reward model that predicts task progress from 0 to 1 per frame, and Reward-Aligned Behaviour Cloning (RA-BC), which weights each training sample by its progress change: full weight above a threshold κ, a soft weight between 0 and κ, and **zero weight when progress goes backwards**. It is advantage-weighted imitation with a learned progress signal. Not run on this page (本页未运行，以当期文档为准): it needs a GPU, and the commands (for example `python -m lerobot.rewards.sarm.compute_rabc_weights`) are on the main docs and may differ in the LeRobot v0.6.x release you install.

## Optional: run the advantage filter toy

`advantage_filter_toy.py` reuses the 10.6 lock chain offline. A mediocre behaviour policy logs 40 episodes: right 90 % of the time, but worse than a coin flip in states 2 and 5. A value V(s) ("minus steps to finish", the flavour RECAP describes) is averaged from the log, each step gets A = −1 + V(s′) − V(s) (i.e. per-step reward −1, undiscounted TD error), and three counted policies are compared: plain BC, BC on successful episodes only, and BC on steps with A > 0. A second log with no successes shows what happens when there is nothing to rank.

```python
"""advantage_filter_toy.py: behaviour cloning that keeps only steps with A > 0 (stdlib).

Same 10-state lock chain as 10.6 (wrong action -> back to state 0, goal at
state 9, at most 30 steps). A mediocre behaviour policy (think: an SFT'd
VLA, or teleop plus autonomous rollouts) records 40 episodes. It gets most
states right but is worse than a coin flip in states 2 and 5.

1. Value from the log: V(s) = mean of "minus steps to finish" over every
   visit to s (a timed-out episode counts its remaining steps plus a
   30-step penalty). This is the flavour of value pi*0.6 / RECAP describes
   (predicting how far you are from done); here it is a plain average.
2. Advantage per logged step: A = -1 + V(s') - V(s), with V(goal) = 0.
3. Three tabular "policies" built by counting logged actions per state:
     bc            every step, equal weight
     success_only  only steps from episodes that reached the goal
     adv_filter    only steps with A > 0 (binary advantage weight)
   Each is evaluated by sampling actions in proportion to the counts,
   2000 rollouts with a fixed seed.
4. Same recipe on a log with no successes at all: V has nothing to rank,
   so the filter keeps noise (outcome-reward RL also needs a policy that
   already succeeds sometimes).
No VLA, no GPU: only the reweighting idea.
"""

import random

N = 10
H = 30
LOCK = [1, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0]     # same right actions as rlpd_chain_toy.py
GOOD = [0.9, 0.9, 0.35, 0.9, 0.9, 0.4, 0.9, 0.9, 0.9]   # P(right action) of the behaviour policy
EPISODES = 40
EVAL = 2000


def step(s, a):
    if a != LOCK[s]:
        return 0, False
    s2 = s + 1
    return s2, s2 == N - 1


def collect(p_right, episodes, seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    log = []   # list of episodes; each a list of (s, a, s2, done)
    for _ in range(episodes):
        ep, s = [], 0
        for _ in range(H):
            a = LOCK[s] if rng.random() < p_right[s] else 1 - LOCK[s]
            s2, done = step(s, a)
            ep.append((s, a, s2, done))
            if done:
                break
            s = s2
        log.append(ep)
    return log


def values(log):
    tot = [0.0] * N
    cnt = [0] * N
    for ep in log:
        success = ep[-1][3]
        L = len(ep)
        for t, (s, _, _, _) in enumerate(ep):
            g = -(L - t) if success else -(L - t) - H
            tot[s] += g
            cnt[s] += 1
    v = [tot[s] / cnt[s] if cnt[s] else -2.0 * H for s in range(N)]
    v[N - 1] = 0.0
    return v


def count_policy(steps):
    c = [[0, 0] for _ in range(N - 1)]
    for s, a in steps:
        c[s][a] += 1
    return c


def p_right(c, s):
    n = c[s][0] + c[s][1]
    return c[s][LOCK[s]] / n if n else 0.5


def evaluate(c, seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    wins = lens = 0
    for _ in range(EVAL):
        s = 0
        for t in range(1, H + 1):
            a = LOCK[s] if rng.random() < p_right(c, s) else 1 - LOCK[s]
            s, done = step(s, a)
            if done:
                wins += 1
                lens += t
                break
    return wins / EVAL, (lens / wins if wins else float("nan"))


def build(log, v):
    all_steps, succ_steps, adv_steps = [], [], []
    for ep in log:
        success = ep[-1][3]
        for s, a, s2, done in ep:
            all_steps.append((s, a))
            if success:
                succ_steps.append((s, a))
            if -1.0 + v[s2] - v[s] > 0:
                adv_steps.append((s, a))
    return {
        "bc": (count_policy(all_steps), len(all_steps)),
        "success_only": (count_policy(succ_steps), len(succ_steps)),
        "adv_filter": (count_policy(adv_steps), len(adv_steps)),
    }


def report(title, log, seed):
    v = values(log)
    wins = sum(ep[-1][3] for ep in log)
    print(title)
    print(f"  logged episodes {len(log)}, successes {wins}, steps {sum(len(ep) for ep in log)}")
    print("  V(s): " + " ".join(f"{x:.1f}" for x in v))
    pols = build(log, v)
    print("  policy        kept_steps  P(right) in states 2 / 5  success  mean_steps_if_success")
    for name, (c, kept) in pols.items():
        sr, ml = evaluate(c, seed)
        mls = f"{ml:.1f}" if sr else "n/a"
        print(f"  {name:<12} {kept:>11}  {p_right(c, 2):>12.2f} / {p_right(c, 5):.2f}  {100 * sr:>6.1f} %  {mls:>8}")
    print()


def main():
    print("lock chain: wrong action -> state 0, goal at 9, at most 30 steps; A = -1 + V(s') - V(s)")
    print()
    report("A) mediocre log (right action 90 % of the time, 35 % in state 2, 40 % in state 5)",
           collect(GOOD, EPISODES, seed=3), seed=11)
    report("B) log with no successes (coin-flip policy, 6 episodes)",
           collect([0.5] * (N - 1), 6, seed=4), seed=11)
    print("in A the filter drops the wrong actions that BC copies; in B nothing ever")
    print("finished, so V cannot tell good steps from bad and the filter keeps noise.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

How to read it:

- **Plain BC** copies the behaviour policy: in state 2 it picks the right action with probability **0.35**, and succeeds **49.6 %** of the time.
- **Success-only BC** keeps 357 of 957 steps and reaches **89.4 %**; successful episodes still contain the wrong moves made before a reset.

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting RL to rescue a policy that never succeeds.** Outcome rewards and advantage filters both need some successes in the data.
- **Throwing away failures.** RECAP keeps them and conditions on the advantage; a filter that discards them also discards what "worse" looks like.
- **Unbounded residuals.** A residual allowed to be large can undo the base policy; ResiP keeps it small and Policy Decorator bounds it.
- **Quoting paper numbers as your own.** Every percentage above is the authors' measurement on their setup.

## Offline Cal-ConRFT: calibrate the critic, not just the label

This is distinct from the existing `advantage_filter_toy.py` and **10.6 HIL-SERL**. Filtering ranks logged actions; here a critic and actor are actually optimized from a fixed offline MDP batch, not from-scratch real-world RL.

<Resource id="calql-paper" />

The opened ConRFT paper (arXiv2502.05450, offline method section) separates **Cal-ConRFT offline** from **HIL-ConRFT online**. Offline, a Cal-QL-style critic accompanies a consistency-policy head trained with **BC + Q guidance** on a small demonstration set. Online, the same BC/Q structure uses interactions and human interventions; weights can change. Our categorical actor **does not reproduce the consistency policy head**, diffusion/consistency training, Octo backbone or Franka experiment. GPU/VLA/real robot: **本页未运行**. Paper numbers such as **96.3 %** are **以论文为准**, not toy measurements.

### The lower bound belongs inside the conservative regularizer

The opened Cal-QL paper (arXiv2303.05479, calibration definition and method) explains that a conservative critic can lie far below the return of a valid behavior policy. Its practical change uses **max(Q, V_MC)** inside the push-down term while retaining compensation on dataset actions. V_MC(s) here is the average discounted return-to-go from the **same offline behavior demonstrations** visiting s, not an evaluation return or optimal-value oracle.

Our discrete temperature-logsumexp version is:

- TD: `0.5 E_D[(Q(s,a) − (r + γ E_π Q_old(s',·)))²]`; terminal target r.
- CQL: `α [T logsumexp_a(Q(s,a)/T) − E_D[Q(s,a) | s]]`.
- Cal-QL: replace Q **only inside logsumexp** by `max(Q(s,a), V_MC(s))`. Below the reference, the push-down derivative is zero; dataset compensation is unchanged. V_MC is stop-gradient.
- Actor: `β CE(behavior,π) − η E_π Q`, categorical logits with critic fixed; **not advantage-filtering**.

The floor is on the regularizer's argument, **not a post-hoc clamp of learned Q or Bellman targets**. As the paper cautions, function approximation and finite optimization do not certify every raw Q or every state-average as a lower bound. We print `min_state_Q_minus_V` to expose residual under-calibration. Online-unlearning/fine-tuning-dip mitigation is **reading only**: this toy has **no online phase**, so it does not demonstrate that benefit.

### One MDP, shared data and equal update counts

A four-state lock chain has three actions:0 advances with probability0.9 (reward1 only on the last successful advance),1 is a wrong key and terminates for0,2 aborts for0.2 but is **not goal success**. γ=0.95. Behavior probabilities are0.75/0.10/0.15;48 logged episodes per seed include failures and aborts. All declared seeds cover all states. The learner sees transitions, not labelled correct actions.

Seeds **1071–1075** each generate one immutable batch reused by **BC_only / CQL_BC / CalQL_BC**. Every arm gets **1000 full-batch actor updates**, identical state-balanced weights, zero initial logits and update schedule. β=1 for all; η=0(BC),η=2(both RL). Critic TD weight1,α=1,T=0.1,learning rates0.03(Q)/0.06(actor); both RL critics start at zero and receive1000 updates. BC has **no critic**, Q=NA: equal outer/actor updates do **not** mean equal FLOPs/parameter counts. No tuning on held-out evaluation results occurs within the run.

Default critic: **Q(s,a)=w_s·features[a]**, features=(1.0,1.2,1.4). This deliberately **misspecified representation** shares action effects: positive w ranks abort above advance and cannot fit all true values. Both RL arms have the same restriction. This is a selected diagnostic fixture, not an unbiased algorithm leaderboard. It exposes conservative-gradient coupling, not an assertion that CQL always under-scales. The `--tabular` control removes this restriction without changing the MDP, data, updates or losses.

Each seed's **1000 evaluation trajectories** share action-sampling and transition uniforms across arms. Every policy is sampled categorically. Report goal success, discounted return and steps including failures. `policy_Q` is E_π Q averaged **uniformly over the four dataset states**; `MC_behavior_V` uses the same state weighting for behavior MC references. Neither is the start-state evaluation return; their difference is a calibration diagnostic, not same-policy prediction error.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Offline discrete lock-chain; BC vs CQL+BC vs Cal-QL+BC (stdlib).
No consistency head, VLA backbone, online phase or HIL-SERL reproduction.
Tabular actor, restricted shared-action-feature linear critic (misspecified).
CQL uses a temperature-logsumexp conservative penalty; Cal-QL masks its
push-down via max(Q, V_MC) INSIDE that penalty, not a post-hoc Q clamp.
V_MC is behavior return-to-go from the SAME demos, never an eval oracle.
Equal full-batch actor updates; BC has no critic (Q scale NA), not equal FLOPs.
All settings synthetic; paper numbers 以论文为准; GPU/robot 本页未运行.
"""
import math
import random
import statistics
import sys

FEATURES = (1., 1.2, 1.4)  # Deliberately restricted critic: Q(s,a)=w_s*FEATURES[a].
SEEDS = (1071, 1072, 1073, 1074, 1075)
STATES, ACTIONS, DEMOS, UPDATES, EVAL_N = 4, 3, 48, 1000, 1000
GAMMA, ALPHA, TEMP, LR_Q, LR_PI = .95, 1., .1, .03, .06
BC_WEIGHT, Q_WEIGHT = 1., 2.
ARMS = ("BC_only", "CQL_BC", "CalQL_BC")


def softmax(xs):
    m = max(xs)
    es = [math.exp(x-m) for x in xs]
    return [x/sum(es) for x in es]


def transition(s, a, noise):
    # 0: advance with 90% probability; 1: wrong key, fail; 2: abort for .2.
    if a == 1:
        return 0., STATES, False
    if a == 2:
        return .2, STATES, False
    if noise >= .9:
        return 0., STATES, False
    return (1. if s == STATES-1 else 0.), s+1, s == STATES-1


def dataset(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    by_state = [[] for _ in range(STATES)]
    for _ in range(DEMOS):
        episode, s = [], 0
        while s < STATES:
            u = rng.random()
            a = 0 if u < .75 else (1 if u < .85 else 2)
            r, ns, _ = transition(s,a,rng.random())
            episode.append((s,a,r,ns)); s = ns
        ret = 0.
        for s,a,r,ns in reversed(episode):
            ret = r+GAMMA*ret
            by_state[s].append((a,r,ns,ret))
    assert all(by_state), "declared seeds must cover all four states"
    return tuple(tuple(rows) for rows in by_state)


def train(arm, data, tabular=False):
    weights, features = [0.]*STATES, FEATURES
    q = [[0.]*ACTIONS for _ in range(STATES)]
    logits = [[0.]*ACTIONS for _ in range(STATES)]
    refs = [statistics.fmean(row[3] for row in rows) for rows in data]
    beta = [[sum(row[0]==a for row in rows)/len(rows) for a in range(ACTIONS)] for rows in data]
    masked, proposals = 0, 0
    for _ in range(UPDATES):
        pi = [softmax(z) for z in logits]
        grads = [[0.]*ACTIONS for _ in range(STATES)]
        for s,rows in enumerate(data):
            if arm != "BC_only":
                scores = [max(x,refs[s]) if arm=="CalQL_BC" else x for x in q[s]]
                proposal = softmax([x/TEMP for x in scores])
                td = [0.]*ACTIONS
                for a,r,ns,_ in rows:
                    target = r if ns==STATES else r+GAMMA*sum(p*x for p,x in zip(pi[ns],q[ns]))
                    td[a] += (q[s][a]-target)/len(rows)
                for a in range(ACTIONS):
                    active = arm!="CalQL_BC" or q[s][a] > refs[s]
                    masked += not active; proposals += 1
                    grad = td[a]+ALPHA*(proposal[a]*active-beta[s][a])
                    grads[s][a] = grad
            # CE gradient plus gradient of -E_pi Q; critic held fixed here.
            meanq = sum(p*x for p,x in zip(pi[s],q[s]))
            eta = 0. if arm=="BC_only" else Q_WEIGHT
            for a in range(ACTIONS):
                grad = BC_WEIGHT*(pi[s][a]-beta[s][a])-eta*pi[s][a]*(q[s][a]-meanq)
                logits[s][a] -= LR_PI*grad
        if arm != "BC_only":
            if tabular:
                q = [[x-LR_Q*g for x,g in zip(qs,gs)] for qs,gs in zip(q,grads)]
            else:
                for s in range(STATES):
                    weights[s] -= LR_Q*sum(g*f for g,f in zip(grads[s],features))
                q = [[w*f for f in features] for w in weights]
    pi = [softmax(z) for z in logits]
    # Uniform weighting over the four covered dataset states (not start return).
    # Same dataset-state weighting for learned policy Q and MC behavior V.
    qscale = statistics.fmean(sum(p*x for p,x in zip(ps,qs)) for ps,qs in zip(pi,q))
    reference = statistics.fmean(refs)
    gaps = [sum(p*x for p,x in zip(ps,qs))-ref for ps,qs,ref in zip(pi,q,refs)]
    return pi,qscale,reference,min(gaps),masked/max(proposals,1)


def evaluate(pi, draws):
    successes, returns, lengths = 0, [], []
    for trajectory in draws:
        s, ret = 0, 0.
        for t,(u,noise) in enumerate(trajectory):
            cdf = 0.
            for a,p in enumerate(pi[s]):
                cdf += p
                if u < cdf:
                    break
            r,s,success = transition(s,a,noise)
            ret += GAMMA**t*r
            if s == STATES:
                successes += success; lengths.append(t+1); break
        returns.append(ret)
    return successes/EVAL_N,statistics.fmean(returns),statistics.fmean(lengths)


def main():
    assert sys.argv[1:] in ([], ["--tabular"]), "optional representation control: --tabular"
    tabular = "--tabular" in sys.argv
    print(f"offline lock-chain: states={STATES} actions={ACTIONS} demos={DEMOS} updates={UPDATES} eval={EVAL_N}/seed")
    print(f"actor beta_BC={BC_WEIGHT} eta_Q=0(BC)/{Q_WEIGHT}(RL); critic TD=1 alpha={ALPHA} LSE_temperature={TEMP}; gamma={GAMMA}")
    print("critic=full tabular representation control" if tabular else
          f"critic Q(s,a)=w_s*features[a], features={FEATURES}; model misspecification intentionally retained")
    print("same full demo batch/actor updates and eval action+transition uniforms; BC critic=NA, not equal FLOPs")
    print("seed arm transitions success mean_return mean_steps policy_Q MC_behavior_V min_state_Q_minus_V masked_fraction")
    scores = {a:[] for a in ARMS}
    for seed in SEEDS:
        data = dataset(seed)
        rng = random.Random(seed+10000)
        draws = tuple(tuple((rng.random(),rng.random()) for _ in range(STATES)) for _ in range(EVAL_N))
        for arm in ARMS:
            pi,q,ref,gap,masked = train(arm,data,tabular)
            success,ret,steps = evaluate(pi,draws)
            scores[arm].append((success,ret,q,ref,gap))
            mechanism = f"{q:.6f} {ref:.6f} {gap:.6f} {masked:.4f}" if arm!="BC_only" else f"NA {ref:.6f} NA NA"
            print(f"{seed} {arm} {sum(map(len,data))} {success:.3f} {ret:.6f} {steps:.3f} {mechanism}")
    print("arm success_mean population_sd min max return_mean policy_Q_mean MC_behavior_V_mean")
    for arm,rs in scores.items():
        success = [r[0] for r in rs]
        q = f"{statistics.fmean(r[2] for r in rs):.6f}" if arm!="BC_only" else "NA"
        print(f"{arm} {statistics.fmean(success):.4f} {statistics.pstdev(success):.4f} {min(success):.3f} {max(success):.3f} "
              f"{statistics.fmean(r[1] for r in rs):.6f} {q} {statistics.fmean(r[3] for r in rs):.6f}")
    print("Cal-QL masks conservative push-down below MC reference; learned Q is NOT hard-clipped or a universal per-action lower bound")
    print("offline only: online unlearning dip is reading, NOT demonstrated; no universal winner")
    print("distinct from advantage filtering/10.6 HIL-SERL; consistency head not reproduced; paper 以论文为准; GPU/robot 本页未运行")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```
