The policies in the last two lessons were trained from scratch for one task on one robot. A vision-language-action model (VLA) starts instead from a vision-language model, a network already trained on huge amounts of images and text, and is trained further on robot demonstrations from many robots, so that it maps a camera image plus an instruction such as "put the red cube in the bin" to robot actions. You rarely train one yourself; you download one and **fine-tune** it on a few dozen demonstrations of your task. This lesson covers how actions get in and out of such a model, which models you can actually download, and under what licence.

## What a VLA is

Three parts. A **vision encoder** turns each camera image into a sequence of feature vectors. A **language model** reads those together with the tokenised instruction (and, in most models, the robot's joint state, fed into the language model or the action head). An **action output** turns the language model's result into numbers for the motors. The first two come from a pretrained vision-language model (in every model below except Octo). OpenVLA reports that, after that initialization, most of the adaptation to robot actions happens in the action tokeniser / output path and in fine-tuning on robot trajectories rather than by training the vision-language backbone from scratch (Kim et al., arXiv:2406.09246; similarly RT-2, Brohan et al., arXiv:2307.15818). Treat "mostly in the third part and in the fine-tuning" as that attributed claim, not as a result measured in this lesson.

There are two ways to build the action output, and they decide much of a model's behaviour:

- **Actions as tokens.** Extend the language model's vocabulary with action tokens and let it generate action tokens autoregressively, one token after another. RT-2 and OpenVLA work like this, one action step per call (8 and 7 tokens); π0-FAST does too, but its tokeniser, FAST, compresses a whole chunk first (see below).
- **A separate action head.** Feed the language model's features into a smaller network that produces a whole chunk of continuous actions, by diffusion or flow matching (a close relative of the diffusion you met in the [previous lesson](/en/month/month-6/act-diffusion)). π0, SmolVLA and GR00T work like this. π0.5 is pretrained on FAST tokens only, gets a flow-matching action expert in post-training and uses that expert at run time. Octo also has a diffusion head but no vision-language model: it is a small transformer trained from scratch, with a frozen T5 text encoder.

The joint state goes to different places. SmolVLA projects it into one token that joins the image and text tokens in the language model ([arXiv:2506.01844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01844), §3.1). π0 gives it to the action expert's weights together with the noisy actions ([arXiv:2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164), §IV). GR00T N1 encodes it and passes it with the language model's features to its diffusion transformer ([arXiv:2503.14734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14734), §2.1). Octo's authors report that policies trained with proprioceptive observations "seemed generally worse" ([arXiv:2405.12213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12213), Appendix E), and the released Octo checkpoints take only camera images and a task, given as a language instruction, a goal image or both ([octo-base-1.5 model card](https://huggingface.co/rail-berkeley/octo-base-1.5)).

The first approach reuses the language model unchanged but has to decide how to write actions as tokens. That is the part you can run and inspect without a GPU.

## Actions as tokens: 256 bins

RT-2 and OpenVLA give each action dimension 256 tokens: the range of each dimension is cut into 256 equal bins, and a value becomes the index of its bin. The count 256 is a vocabulary budget, not a physics constant: OpenVLA overlays the continuous-action bins onto the 256 least-used tokens in the Llama tokenizer vocabulary so the pretrained language model keeps its text tokens (§3.2 of Kim et al., arXiv:2406.09246). RT-2 uses the whole min/max range of the data. OpenVLA instead sets the range with the dataset's 1st and 99th percentiles and clips values beyond them to the end bins. Percentiles spend the 256 bins on the mass of the data; min/max spends many of them on rare extremes and makes every ordinary step coarser. This lesson follows OpenVLA.

<Formula label="Bin token for one action value">token = floor(256 × (clip(a, p1, p99) − p1) / (p99 − p1)),   from 0 to 255</Formula>

The program below makes a dataset shaped like teleoperation of an SO-101: 50 episodes of 6 joint targets in degrees at 30 Hz. It then tokenises a 1-second chunk from a new episode both ways:

```python
"""action_tokens.py: how a VLA turns robot actions into tokens, per-step bins versus a DCT of the whole chunk.

A language model outputs tokens, so an autoregressive VLA needs a way to write actions as tokens. Two schemes:
  bins  (OpenVLA): each action dimension at each step becomes one of 256 tokens, bins spread evenly
        between that dimension's 1st and 99th percentile over the dataset (RT-2 spreads them over the full range).
  DCT   (the idea behind FAST, used by pi0-FAST): normalise each dimension to -1..1 with the same percentiles,
        take a discrete cosine transform of each dimension over the chunk, multiply by a scale and round.
        Smooth motion puts almost everything into a few low-frequency coefficients; the rest round to zero.
        (FAST then compresses the integers with byte-pair encoding; this program counts nonzero coefficients.)
The actions are synthetic but shaped like a teleoperated SO-101: 6 joint targets in degrees at 30 Hz, chunks of
1 s (30 steps). Standard library only, fixed seeds.
"""
import math
import random

HZ, CHUNK, JOINTS, BINS = 30, 30, 6, 256


def episode(rng, seconds=10):
    """Smooth joint targets: a few slow sine components per joint, like a person moving a leader arm."""
    comps = [[(rng.uniform(5, 40), rng.uniform(0.1, 0.8), rng.uniform(0, 2 * math.pi)) for _ in range(3)]
             for _ in range(JOINTS)]
    offset = [rng.uniform(-30, 30) for _ in range(JOINTS)]
    return [[offset[j] + sum(a * math.sin(2 * math.pi * f * t / HZ + ph) for a, f, ph in comps[j])
             for j in range(JOINTS)] for t in range(seconds * HZ)]


def percentile(values, q):
    v = sorted(values)
    k = (len(v) - 1) * q
    lo = int(k)
    return v[lo] + (v[min(lo + 1, len(v) - 1)] - v[lo]) * (k - lo)


def to_bins(x, lo, hi):
    u = (min(max(x, lo), hi) - lo) / (hi - lo)
    return min(int(u * BINS), BINS - 1)


def from_bins(token, lo, hi):
    return lo + (token + 0.5) * (hi - lo) / BINS


def dct(x):
    """DCT-II with orthonormal scaling."""
    n = len(x)
    return [math.sqrt((1 if k == 0 else 2) / n) * sum(x[i] * math.cos(math.pi * k * (2 * i + 1) / (2 * n))
                                                      for i in range(n)) for k in range(n)]


def idct(c):
    n = len(c)
    return [sum(math.sqrt((1 if k == 0 else 2) / n) * c[k] * math.cos(math.pi * k * (2 * i + 1) / (2 * n))
                for k in range(n)) for i in range(n)]


def errors(chunk, rebuilt):
    diffs = [abs(a - b) for row, rrow in zip(chunk, rebuilt) for a, b in zip(row, rrow)]
    return math.sqrt(sum(d * d for d in diffs) / len(diffs)), max(diffs)


def main():
    rng = random.Random(6)
    data = [episode(rng) for _ in range(50)]
    lo = [percentile([s[j] for ep in data for s in ep], 0.01) for j in range(JOINTS)]
    hi = [percentile([s[j] for ep in data for s in ep], 0.99) for j in range(JOINTS)]
    print(f'Dataset: 50 episodes of 10 s, {JOINTS} joints at {HZ} Hz; 1st..99th percentile per joint (deg):')
    print('  ' + '  '.join(f'j{j} {lo[j]:+.0f}..{hi[j]:+.0f}' for j in range(JOINTS)))
    print(f'  bin width = range / {BINS}: ' + ', '.join(f'{(hi[j] - lo[j]) / BINS:.2f}' for j in range(JOINTS)) + ' deg')

    chunk = episode(random.Random(99))[:CHUNK]          # a new episode, not in the dataset
    print(f'\nOne {CHUNK}-step chunk (1 s) from a new episode: {CHUNK} x {JOINTS} = {CHUNK * JOINTS} numbers')

    tokens = [[to_bins(chunk[t][j], lo[j], hi[j]) for j in range(JOINTS)] for t in range(CHUNK)]
    rebuilt = [[from_bins(tokens[t][j], lo[j], hi[j]) for j in range(JOINTS)] for t in range(CHUNK)]
    rms, worst = errors(chunk, rebuilt)
    clipped = sum(1 for t in range(CHUNK) for j in range(JOINTS) if not lo[j] <= chunk[t][j] <= hi[j])
    print(f'bins: {CHUNK * JOINTS} tokens, error rms {rms:.3f} deg, max {worst:.3f} deg '
          f'({clipped} values outside the 1st..99th percentile, clipped)')
    print('  joint 0 tokens, first 12 steps: ' + ' '.join(str(tokens[t][0]) for t in range(12)))
    same = sum(1 for t in range(1, CHUNK) for j in range(JOINTS) if tokens[t][j] == tokens[t - 1][j])
    near = sum(1 for t in range(1, CHUNK) for j in range(JOINTS) if abs(tokens[t][j] - tokens[t - 1][j]) <= 2)
    total = (CHUNK - 1) * JOINTS
    print(f'  token equal to the previous step: {same}/{total}; within 2 of it: {near}/{total}')

    print('\nDCT of each joint over the chunk, normalised to -1..1, then scale and round:')
    norm = [[2 * (chunk[t][j] - lo[j]) / (hi[j] - lo[j]) - 1 for j in range(JOINTS)] for t in range(CHUNK)]
    coeffs = [dct([norm[t][j] for t in range(CHUNK)]) for j in range(JOINTS)]
    for scale in (5, 10, 30, 100):
        q = [[round(c * scale) for c in coeffs[j]] for j in range(JOINTS)]
        nonzero = sum(1 for j in range(JOINTS) for v in q[j] if v != 0)
        back = [idct([v / scale for v in q[j]]) for j in range(JOINTS)]
        rebuilt = [[(back[j][t] + 1) / 2 * (hi[j] - lo[j]) + lo[j] for j in range(JOINTS)] for t in range(CHUNK)]
        rms, worst = errors(chunk, rebuilt)
        print(f'  scale {scale:>3}: {nonzero:>3} nonzero coefficients of {CHUNK * JOINTS}, '
              f'error rms {rms:.3f} deg, max {worst:.3f} deg')
    q = [round(c * 10) for c in coeffs[0]]
    print('  joint 0 at scale 10: ' + ' '.join(str(v) for v in q))


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
```

## Compressing a chunk with the DCT

FAST, the tokeniser behind π0-FAST (Pertsch et al., arXiv:2501.09747), tokenises the whole chunk at once. It normalises each dimension to −1..1 with the same percentiles, takes a discrete cosine transform (DCT) of each dimension over the chunk, multiplies by a scale and rounds to integers. Why low frequencies dominate: a teleoperated joint trajectory varies slowly from step to step, so it is well approximated by a few slow cosines; the DCT is an orthogonal change of basis onto those cosines, and energy compaction puts most of the ℓ₂ mass in the first coefficients. Rounding to integers then zeros any coefficient whose absolute value is below 1/(2 · scale) — raising the scale keeps more small coefficients and lowers reconstruction error, at the cost of a longer nonzero sequence (see the table). FAST then compresses the integer sequence with byte-pair encoding. The program counts the nonzero coefficients as a stand-in for the final tokens:

## SmolVLA and asynchronous chunk inference

SmolVLA (Shukor et al., arXiv **2506.01844**) is the small model in the table above. Two things in its paper are worth reading closely even if you never fine-tune it: how it stays small, and how its **asynchronous inference** stack keeps the arm moving while the next chunk is computed. Everything in this section about SmolVLA's architecture and results is **per the paper**; no weights, GPU server or SO-100 arm were run for this page.

<Resource id="smolvla-paper" />

### How SmolVLA stays small (per the paper)

- **About 450M parameters in total**, of which roughly 100M are the action expert. The backbone is the SmolVLM-2 vision-language model, and the VLM is kept frozen while the action expert trains.
- **Skipped VLM layers.** The action expert reads features from an intermediate layer instead of the last one; the paper uses the first half of the language model's layers (the first 16) and drops the rest, which roughly halves that part of the compute.
- **Few visual tokens:** the global image only, with pixel shuffle, giving 64 visual tokens per frame.
- **Action expert with interleaved attention.** The expert's blocks alternate between **cross-attention** (action tokens attend to the VLM features) and **causal self-attention** (action tokens attend to earlier action tokens), rather than using only one kind.
- **Flow matching.** The expert is trained with a <Term id="flow-matching">flow-matching</Term> objective to output chunks of n = 50 actions, with 10 integration steps at inference.
- **Community data:** pretraining used fewer than 30k episodes from public, community-contributed LeRobot datasets.

### Async inference: when to ask for the next chunk

A chunked policy can run in two ways. In the **sync** (sequential) way, the robot executes the whole <Term id="action-chunk">action chunk</Term>, then sends an observation and waits for inference with an empty queue. In SmolVLA's **async** stack, a **RobotClient** pops one action per control tick from a queue; when the remaining fraction `|A| / n` falls below a threshold **g**, it captures a new observation and sends it to a **PolicyServer** without stopping. When the new chunk arrives, the client aggregates it with what is left of the old one on the overlapping steps. g = 0 reproduces sync; g = 1 asks for a new chunk at every tick (one forward pass per tick). With inference latency ℓ_S and control period Δt, the paper derives that the queue does not run dry when

<Formula label="No-idle condition for async inference (SmolVLA §3.3)">g ≥ (E[ℓ_S] / Δt) / n</Formula>

8.3 already shows sync idle frames against one async threshold. What it does not show is that async inference is a **trade-off**, which is what the toy below measures. It also has to be kept apart from two neighbouring ideas:

| Idea | Lesson | What it fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Async chunk inference (this section) | 6.3, and the loop in 8.3 | Idle gaps while the next chunk is computed; when to trigger it (g) |
| Real-time chunking (RTC) soft mask | 10.2 | The jump at the seam: the new chunk is guided towards the old one during denoising |
| Causal timestamp alignment of sensor streams | 8.3 | Which camera / joint samples belong together, without looking ahead |

Async here is **not** RTC: the toy's new chunk is not conditioned on the old one and there is no soft mask. It is **not** stamp alignment either: there is one observation per request and no stream matching.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""vla_async_chunk_toy.py: sync vs async action-chunk inference, as a tradeoff (stdlib).

SmolVLA (arXiv 2506.01844, 3.3) runs a RobotClient that pops one action per
control tick from a queue and, when the remaining fraction |A|/n drops below a
threshold g, captures an observation and asks a PolicyServer for a new chunk
without stopping. g = 0 is the sequential limit (drain, then wait), g = 1 the
compute-intensive limit (a request as soon as the last one returns).

Every arm here shares:
  - the same chunk predictor (a function of the observation only),
  - the same chunk size n and control tick dt,
  - the same latency draws l_S: the k-th request of every arm takes LAT[k],
  - the same target path (piecewise-constant velocity, seeded).
The robot tracks a moving target; a chunk is the target extrapolated from the
observation, so old observations give worse actions after a velocity change.

Metrics per arm: idle_s (ticks with an empty queue x dt), infer_calls,
underruns (separate empty-queue episodes), staleness = age of the observation
behind each executed action (mean / max, s), switches = new chunks spliced
into a non-empty queue and their mean |jump| (overlap thrash), track_rmse.
Aggregation f here: actions carry the executed-step index they belong to; on
arrival the new chunk's already-executed steps are dropped and overlapping
steps take the new value (one simple f; the paper only says "aggregate").
This is NOT 10.2 RTC (no soft mask, the new chunk is not conditioned on the
old one) and NOT 8.3 timestamp alignment of sensor streams. Async need not
win every metric. SmolVLA weights, a GPU server and an SO-100 arm are not run
on this page (本页未运行); paper latency / success numbers are 以论文为准.
usage: python3 vla_async_chunk_toy.py
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random

SEED = 1844
DT = 1 / 30             # 30 Hz control, as in the paper's real-world setting
N_CHUNK = 50            # chunk size n (the paper trains n = 50)
HORIZON = 900           # ticks = 30 s
LAT_MEAN, LAT_SD = 0.30, 0.10   # l_S in seconds (lesson-chosen, not measured)
LAT_MIN = 0.10
ARMS = (("sync", 0.0), ("async_g0.1", 0.1), ("async_g0.3", 0.3), ("async_g0.7", 0.7), ("async_g1.0", 1.0))


def target_path(rng):
    """Target position per tick: velocity changes every 1-3 s."""
    pos, vel, out, t_next = 0.0, 0.0, [], 0
    for t in range(HORIZON + N_CHUNK + 200):
        if t == t_next:
            vel = rng.uniform(-0.3, 0.3)
            t_next = t + rng.randint(30, 90)
        out.append((pos, vel))
        pos += vel * DT
    return out


def latencies(rng, k=2000):
    return [max(LAT_MIN, rng.gauss(LAT_MEAN, LAT_SD)) for _ in range(k)]


def predict_chunk(obs_step, obs_tick, path):
    """Same predictor for all arms: extrapolate the observed target n steps ahead.

    Actions are indexed by executed-step count (not wall time): a chunk from an
    observation taken after step k covers steps k+1 .. k+n.
    """
    p, v = path[obs_tick]
    return {obs_step + 1 + i: p + v * (i + 1) * DT for i in range(N_CHUNK)}


def run(g, path, lat):
    queue = predict_chunk(0, 0, path)   # initial chunk, as in Algorithm 1 line 2
    source = {k: 0 for k in queue}      # step -> wall tick of the observation behind it
    inflight = None                     # (arrival_tick, obs_step, obs_tick)
    step, calls, idle, underruns, was_empty = 0, 1, 0, 0, False
    ages, jumps, errs, held = [], [], [], 0.0
    for t in range(1, HORIZON + 1):
        if inflight and inflight[0] <= t:          # chunk arrives: aggregate f(A, A~)
            _arr, obs_step, obs_tick = inflight
            live = {k: a for k, a in predict_chunk(obs_step, obs_tick, path).items() if k > step}
            overlap = [k for k in live if k in queue]
            if overlap:
                k0 = min(overlap)
                jumps.append(abs(live[k0] - queue[k0]))
            queue.update(live)
            source.update({k: obs_tick for k in live})
            inflight = None
        if step + 1 in queue:                      # pop and execute the next step
            step += 1
            held = queue.pop(step)
            ages.append((t - source[step]) * DT)
            was_empty = False
        else:                                      # empty queue: the arm waits
            idle += 1
            underruns += not was_empty
            was_empty = True
        errs.append(held - path[t][0])
        if inflight is None and (not queue or len(queue) / N_CHUNK < g):
            inflight = (t + max(1, math.ceil(lat[calls] / DT)), step, t)
            calls += 1
    return {
        "idle_s": idle * DT, "calls": calls, "underruns": underruns,
        "age_mean": sum(ages) / len(ages), "age_max": max(ages),
        "switches": len(jumps), "jump_mean": sum(jumps) / len(jumps) if jumps else 0.0,
        "rmse": math.sqrt(sum(e * e for e in errs) / len(errs)),
    }


def main() -> None:
    path = target_path(random.Random(SEED))
    lat = latencies(random.Random(SEED + 1))
    mean_lat = sum(lat[:200]) / 200
    print(f"seed={SEED} dt={DT:.4f}s n={N_CHUNK} horizon={HORIZON * DT:.0f}s "
          f"l_S~N({LAT_MEAN},{LAT_SD}) min {LAT_MIN}s, shared draws (mean of first 200 = {mean_lat:.3f}s)")
    print(f"no-idle rule of thumb (paper 3.3): g >= E[l_S]/(n*dt) = {mean_lat / (N_CHUNK * DT):.3f}")
```

- `idle_s`: seconds the arm spent with an empty queue. Sync waits 4.17 s out of 30 s. `async_g0.1` still waits 2.57 s because 0.1 is below the no-idle threshold 0.176 printed above. From g = 0.3 up, it is 0.

- `stale_mean_s` / `stale_max_s`: **action staleness**, the age of the observation behind each executed action. Sync executes actions computed from an observation up to 2.133 s old; g = 0.7 brings the mean from 1.101 s to 0.559 s.
- `switches` / `mean_jump`: how often a new chunk was spliced into a non-empty queue, and the mean size of the jump at the splice (overlap thrash). ## What a VLA does not do for you

A VLA does not remove the work of the previous lessons; it relocates it. You still need a good dataset of your robot, with the variation and recoveries you want it to handle. You still need to evaluate it on a fixed set of conditions. And "zero-shot" claims only hold for robots and scenes like those in the pretraining data: OpenVLA's own model card says it does not generalise to unseen robot embodiments without fine-tuning. What you gain is language conditioning (one model, many instructions) and a better starting point, so fewer demonstrations per task.

## Dual-system VLAs: a slow planner and a fast, seeing controller

Everything above is a **single trunk**: one forward path from image and instruction to actions, whether as tokens or through an action head. A **dual-system** VLA splits that into two parts running at different rates. The OpenHelix survey ([arXiv:2505.03912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03912), [project page](https://openhelix-robot.github.io/)) describes a slower **System 2**, typically a multimodal LLM, that updates less often and makes the deliberate, high-level decision, and a faster **System 1**, a lightweight policy, that updates at a higher rate to produce the low-level actions; the slow system's information arrives with a delay. The survey adds one criterion that decides the label: **System 1 must take real-time perception inputs (such as RGB images) itself.** By that criterion it says π0, GR00T N1 and similar models cannot properly be classified as dual-system — so this page does not call π0 dual-system, even though it has a separate action expert. It lists LCB, DP-VLA, HiRT, RoboDual and others in the family.

OpenHelix's own model, per the paper: a frozen-or-tuned LLaVA as System 2 whose output `<ACT>` token is the latent goal (in its prompt-tuning variant only the new token's head is trained), a linear projector, and a pre-trained 3D Diffuser Actor as System 1 that also sees RGB-D views and proprioception. Two of its findings are worth keeping: training the projector and the MLLM together from the start can collapse training, and its analysis suggests the MLLM's action token mostly carries instruction semantics with little adaptation to the image. Its CALVIN success rates, including the moving-object CALVIN-D variant, are **以论文为准**; no weights, GPU or CALVIN run here. This is a different topic from TwinVLA's bimanual coordination in 6.2 and from the async chunking above.

### Fair cadence toy

`dual_system_vla_cadence_toy.py` is **synthetic**: a 2-D point follows one of two moving targets (red, blue) at 50 ticks per second for 300 ticks; the instruction switches from red to blue at a random tick between 100 and 200. Every arm gets **the same compute budget of 1.0 unit per tick on average**, the same targets, perception noise and switch ticks (common random numbers per seed), the same speed limit and gain. What each arm **sees** differs and is declared:

| Arm | Model calls | Sees instruction | Sees the current image every tick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `A_slow_single` | big model every 10 ticks (10 units), 10-tick latency, output held | yes (when called) | no |
| `B_fast_single` | small model every tick (1 unit) | only once at t = 0 (not charged) | yes |
| `C_dual_rt` | System 2 every 20 ticks (10 units, 10-tick latency) + System 1 every tick (0.5 units) | System 2 only | yes (System 1) |
| `D_dual_no_rt` | as C | System 2 only | no — System 1 gets only System 2's target snapshot |

Metrics after the switch: `rmse` to the instructed target, `on_target_frac`, `react_ticks` (ticks until within 0.05 of the new target), `never_reached`, `underrun` (fraction of ticks without a fresh model output) and `goal_age_ticks` (age of the goal information in use). Eight switch-time seeds, mean±sd. A second scenario holds both targets still and never switches.

### Reading the result

- **Moving targets with a switch:** `C_dual_rt` reacts in about 28 ticks and is on target 80 % of the time. `B_fast_single` sees perfectly well but never learns the instruction changed (6 of 8 seeds never reach blue). `A_slow_single` is right about the goal but steers to 10–20-tick-old images and has no fresh output on 90 % of ticks. `D_dual_no_rt` has the same cadence and compute as C but no real-time perception in System 1, and it does about as badly as A — the toy version of OpenHelix's criterion.
- **Static targets, no switch:** every arm stays on target; B and C tie at the lowest error and A and D lag only slightly. The dual split buys nothing when nothing changes.

The benefit depends on how fast the world and the instruction change relative to the slow model's rate; the toy does not pick a winner, and real System 2 latency, goal-embedding quality and training stability are exactly what it leaves out. **Real VLA weights, GPUs and robots: 本页未运行.**

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""dual_system_vla_cadence_toy.py: slow planner / fast controller vs single-rate arms (stdlib).

Synthetic 2-D reaching: two moving targets (red, blue); the instruction says
which one to follow and switches from "red" to "blue" at a random tick.
A second scenario holds both targets still and never switches (scored over the
same tick window), to show when a single-rate arm loses nothing. Control
runs at 50 ticks/s for 300 ticks. Compute is counted in units; every arm gets
the SAME 1.0 unit per tick on average, the SAME targets, perception noise and
switch times (common random numbers per seed), the SAME speed limit and gain.
What each arm sees:
  A slow_single   big model (instruction + image) every 10 ticks, 10 units/call,
                  result after 10 ticks of latency; action held in between
  B fast_single   small model (image only) every tick, 1 unit/call; the goal is
                  the instruction parsed once at t=0 (that parse is not charged)
                  and never refreshed
  C dual_rt       System 2 (instruction + image) every 20 ticks, 10 units/call,
                  10-tick latency -> goal = which target; System 1 every tick,
                  0.5 units, uses the latest goal AND the current image
  D dual_no_rt    like C, but System 1 only gets System 2's target snapshot,
                  no current image (fails OpenHelix's real-time criterion)
Not a VLA, not trained, no GPU. Numbers depend on the lesson-chosen rates.
usage: python3 dual_system_vla_cadence_toy.py
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
import statistics

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7)
TICKS = 300
HZ = 50
VMAX = 0.02            # max move per tick
GAIN = 0.3
PERC = 0.005           # perception noise on target positions
REACH = 0.05           # "on target" radius
COST = {"A": (10, 10.0, 0.0), "B": (1, 1.0, 0.0), "C": (20, 10.0, 0.5), "D": (20, 10.0, 0.5)}
LATENCY = 10           # ticks for a big-model call (A, and System 2 in C/D)
ARMS = {"A": "slow_single", "B": "fast_single", "C": "dual_rt", "D": "dual_no_rt"}


def targets(t, phase, moving=True):
    w = 2 * math.pi * 0.25 / HZ * t if moving else 0.0
    red = (0.5 + 0.25 * math.cos(w + phase), 0.5 + 0.25 * math.sin(w + phase))
    blue = (0.5 - 0.25 * math.cos(1.3 * w + phase), 0.5 + 0.20 * math.sin(1.3 * w))
    return {"red": red, "blue": blue}


def step_to(p, goal):
    dx, dy = GAIN * (goal[0] - p[0]), GAIN * (goal[1] - p[1])
    n = math.hypot(dx, dy)
    if n > VMAX:
        dx, dy = dx * VMAX / n, dy * VMAX / n
    return (p[0] + dx, p[1] + dy)


def episode(arm, seed, scenario="moving_switch"):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    switch = rng.randint(100, 200)
    moving = scenario == "moving_switch"
    score_from = switch
    if not moving:  # static targets, no instruction switch; score the same window
        switch = TICKS + 1
    phase = rng.uniform(0, 2 * math.pi)
    noise = [(rng.gauss(0, PERC), rng.gauss(0, PERC), rng.gauss(0, PERC), rng.gauss(0, PERC)) for _ in range(TICKS)]
    every, big, small = COST[arm]

    def seen(t):  # noisy image at tick t (shared draws)
        tg, n = targets(t, phase, moving), noise[t]
        return {"red": (tg["red"][0] + n[0], tg["red"][1] + n[1]), "blue": (tg["blue"][0] + n[2], tg["blue"][1] + n[3])}

    def instr(t):
        return "red" if t < switch else "blue"

    p = (0.5, 0.5)
    compute = 0.0
    held = None            # A: held action target
    goal_name, goal_snap, goal_age_t = "red", seen(0)["red"], 0
    pending = []           # (ready_tick, payload)
    err, fresh, ages, reached = [], 0, [], None
    for t in range(TICKS):
        if t % every == 0 and arm != "B":
            compute += big
            obs = seen(t)
            pending.append((t + LATENCY, (instr(t), obs[instr(t)], t)))
        if arm == "B":
            compute += big
        for item in [x for x in pending if x[0] <= t]:
            pending.remove(item)
            goal_name, goal_snap, goal_age_t = item[1]
            held = goal_snap
            if arm == "A":
                fresh += 1
        now = seen(t)
        if arm == "A":
            target = held if held is not None else p
            age = t - goal_age_t
        elif arm == "B":
            target, age = now["red"], 0     # instruction parsed once at t=0
            fresh += 1
        else:
            compute += small
            fresh += 1
            target = now[goal_name] if arm == "C" else goal_snap
            age = t - goal_age_t
        p = step_to(p, target)
        true = targets(t + 1, phase, moving)[instr(t)]
        d = math.hypot(p[0] - true[0], p[1] - true[1])
        if t >= score_from:
            err.append(d)
            if reached is None and d < REACH:
                reached = t - score_from
        ages.append(age)
    follow = sum(e < REACH for e in err) / len(err)
    return {"rmse": math.sqrt(statistics.mean(e * e for e in err)), "follow": follow,
            "react": reached if reached is not None else TICKS - score_from, "miss": reached is None,
            "underrun": 1 - fresh / TICKS, "age": statistics.mean(ages), "unit": compute / TICKS}


def ms(v, f):
    return f"{statistics.mean(v):{f}}±{statistics.pstdev(v):{f}}"


def main():
    print(f"Synthetic cadence: {TICKS} ticks at {HZ} Hz, {len(SEEDS)} seeds (switch tick 100..200), "
          f"big call latency {LATENCY} ticks, vmax {VMAX}/tick, reach {REACH}")
    for scenario in ("moving_switch", "static_no_switch"):
        print(f"[{scenario}] arm units/tick rmse on_target_frac react_ticks never_reached underrun goal_age_ticks")
        for arm, name in ARMS.items():
            rs = [episode(arm, s, scenario) for s in SEEDS]
            print(f"{arm}_{name} {ms([r['unit'] for r in rs], '.2f')} {ms([r['rmse'] for r in rs], '.3f')} "
                  f"{ms([r['follow'] for r in rs], '.2f')} {ms([r['react'] for r in rs], '.1f')} "
                  f"{sum(r['miss'] for r in rs)}/{len(SEEDS)} {ms([r['underrun'] for r in rs], '.2f')} "
                  f"{ms([r['age'] for r in rs], '.1f')}")
    print("Same targets, noise, switch ticks and compute per tick; what each arm sees differs and is declared.")
    print("No winner is declared; real VLA weights, GPUs and robots: 本页未运行.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

## RDT2's three-stage recipe: tokens, a flow-matching expert, then one step

Everything so far turns actions into tokens **or** generates them with an action head. RDT2 does both, one after the other, and then shrinks the generator. This section covers how the actions are **represented and sampled**. It is not the dual-system cadence above: there are no rates or latencies here. It is not the VQ-BeT codebook for a multimodal BC head in [6.1](/en/month/month-6/behaviour-cloning), and it is not the OneDP-style one-step surrogate in [10.1](/en/month/month-10/flow-matching-policies).

<Resource id="rdt2-paper" />

**What the paper does (opened at arXiv 2602.03310; numbers 以论文为准).** RDT2 (Liu et al.) is built on a 7B Qwen2.5-VL and trained on over 10,000 hours of demonstrations collected with a re-designed, embodiment-agnostic UMI in more than 100 households.

- **Stage 1:** a Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) tokenizer turns each continuous action chunk into a few discrete tokens. The 1024 least frequent vocabulary entries are reserved for them, and the VLM is trained with ordinary cross-entropy (128K iterations). The paper argues this converges faster and does not damage the VLM's discrete knowledge.
- **Stage 2:** the VLM is **frozen**, and a 400M action expert (a variant of RDT-1B using grouped-query attention) cross-attends to the VLM's per-layer features and is trained with a **flow-matching** loss. Sampling a chunk takes five forward passes of the expert.
- **Stage 3:** that multi-step expert is **distilled** into a single-step generator G(A⁰) = A⁰ + v_θ′(0, A⁰, ·), initialised from the expert and regressed onto the expert's multi-step output for the same noise.
- **Ablation:** at the same discretisation error, RVQ needs up to about two-thirds fewer tokens than FAST, while uniform binning gives the lowest error with far more tokens.

None of this was reproduced here. The 7B training, the GPUs and the robots are **本页未运行**.

### Fair setup

`rdt2_rvq_fm_distill_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. An action chunk is 2 steps × (dx, dy) = 4 numbers, drawn from four heading modes with speed, heading and turn noise. Every arm within a part gets the same chunks and the same noise draws (common random numbers), over five seeds.

| Part | Arm | What it is | Budget held equal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | bins_4x4 | 4 uniform bins per number, 4 tokens | 8 bits per chunk |
| 1 | single_vq_256 | one token from a 256-entry k-means codebook | 8 bits per chunk |
| 1 | rvq_2x16 | two tokens, a 16-entry codebook per residual stage | 8 bits per chunk |
| 1 | rvq_4x4 | four tokens, a 4-entry codebook per residual stage | 8 bits per chunk |
| 2 | fm_1 / fm_2 / fm_5 | Euler sampling of the flow-matching expert with 1, 2 or 5 NFE | the same noise draws |
| 2 | distill_1_nn | lookup student: the stored 5-step output for the nearest of 400 stored noise draws | 1 online NFE |
| 2 | distill_1_avg3 | the same pairs, averaging the 3 nearest stored outputs | 1 online NFE |

**The expert is not trained.** Its velocity is the exact flow-matching field of a lightly smoothed copy of the training chunks (what a perfectly fitted expert would converge to), on the paper's path A^τ = (1 − τ)ε + τA. Nothing conditions it on images or language. The students are lookups, not RDT2's trained G, and their 400 pairs cost 2000 offline expert calls. Part 1 reports held-out MSE, tokens, codebook entries and unused entries. Part 2 reports precision (distance to the nearest held-out chunk), recall (distance from each held-out chunk to the nearest sample), the off-manifold share (precision above 0.25) and distinct outputs. All results are mean±sd over five seeds.

```python
     share, distinct outputs, online NFE and offline expert NFE.

usage: python3 rdt2_rvq_fm_distill_toy.py

from __future__ import annotations

import math
import random
import statistics

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
N_TOK, N_TRAIN, N_TEST, N_GEN = 768, 256, 200, 200
LLOYD_ITERS = 8
SMOOTH = 0.05          # expert smoothing (std of the Gaussian around each train chunk)
DISTILL_PAIRS = 400
OFF_MANIFOLD = 0.25    # distance to the nearest held-out chunk that counts as off-manifold
TOKENIZERS = (("bins_4x4", [4, 4, 4, 4]), ("single_vq_256", [256]), ("rvq_2x16", [16, 16]), ("rvq_4x4", [4, 4, 4, 4]))
GENERATORS = ("fm_1", "fm_2", "fm_5", "distill_1_nn", "distill_1_avg3")


def sample_chunk(rng):
    mode = rng.randrange(4)
    head = mode * math.pi / 2 + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.12)
    speed = 1.0 + rng.gauss(0.0, 0.08)
    turn = rng.gauss(0.0, 0.3)
    v1 = (speed * math.cos(head), speed * math.sin(head))
    v2 = (speed * math.cos(head + turn), speed * math.sin(head + turn))
    return [v1[0], v1[1], v2[0], v2[1]]


def d2(a, b):
    return sum((x - y) ** 2 for x, y in zip(a, b))


def nearest(c, pts):
    best, bi = float("inf"), 0
    for i, p in enumerate(pts):
        d = d2(c, p)
        if d < best:
            best, bi = d, i
    return bi, best


def kmeans(data, k, rng):
    cent = [list(p) for p in rng.sample(data, k)] if k <= len(data) else [list(p) for p in data]
    for _ in range(LLOYD_ITERS):
        sums = [[0.0] * 4 for _ in cent]
        cnt = [0] * len(cent)
        for p in data:
            i, _ = nearest(p, cent)
            cnt[i] += 1
            for j in range(4):
                sums[i][j] += p[j]
        for i in range(len(cent)):
            if cnt[i]:
                cent[i] = [s / cnt[i] for s in sums[i]]
    return cent


def train_rvq(data, stages, rng):
    books, resid = [], [list(p) for p in data]
    for k in stages:
        book = kmeans(resid, k, rng)
        books.append(book)
        resid = [[r[j] - book[nearest(r, book)[0]][j] for j in range(4)] for r in resid]
    return books


def encode_decode(x, books):
    rec, resid, codes = [0.0] * 4, list(x), []
    for book in books:
        i, _ = nearest(resid, book)
        codes.append(i)
        rec = [rec[j] + book[i][j] for j in range(4)]
        resid = [resid[j] - book[i][j] for j in range(4)]
    return rec, codes


def velocity(x, tau, train):
    """Exact FM field for a mixture of N(a_i, SMOOTH^2 I) under A^tau = (1-tau) eps + tau A."""
    var = (1.0 - tau) ** 2 + (tau * SMOOTH) ** 2
    logw = [-d2(x, [tau * v for v in a]) / (2.0 * var) for a in train]
    m = max(logw)
    w = [math.exp(l - m) for l in logw]
    z = sum(w)
    gain = tau * SMOOTH ** 2 / var
    ea = [0.0] * 4
    for wi, a in zip(w, train):
        for j in range(4):
            ea[j] += wi * (a[j] + gain * (x[j] - tau * a[j]))
    ea = [v / z for v in ea]
    return [(ea[j] - x[j]) / (1.0 - tau) for j in range(4)]


def euler(eps, steps, train):
    x, h = list(eps), 1.0 / steps
    for s in range(steps):
        v = velocity(x, s * h, train)
        x = [x[j] + h * v[j] for j in range(4)]
    return x


def knn_student(pairs, eps, k):
    """Average of the stored 5-step outputs for the k nearest stored noise draws."""
    near = sorted(pairs, key=lambda p: d2(p[0], eps))[:k]
    return [sum(p[1][j] for p in near) / k for j in range(4)]


def gen_metrics(samples, test):
    prec = [math.sqrt(nearest(s, test)[1]) for s in samples]
    rec = [math.sqrt(nearest(t, samples)[1]) for t in test]
    off = 100.0 * sum(p > OFF_MANIFOLD for p in prec) / len(prec)
    return statistics.mean(prec), statistics.mean(rec), off


def ms(xs, f="{:.3f}"):
    return (f + "±" + f).format(statistics.mean(xs), statistics.stdev(xs))


def main():
    tok = {n: {"mse": [], "dead": []} for n, _ in TOKENIZERS}
    gen = {n: {"prec": [], "rec": [], "off": [], "uniq": []} for n in GENERATORS}
    for seed in SEEDS:
        rd = random.Random(1000 + seed)
        tok_data = [sample_chunk(rd) for _ in range(N_TOK)]
        train = tok_data[:N_TRAIN]                   # the expert sees a subset of the same chunks
        test = [sample_chunk(rd) for _ in range(N_TEST)]
        for name, stages in TOKENIZERS:
            if name.startswith("bins"):
                lo = [min(p[j] for p in tok_data) for j in range(4)]
                hi = [max(p[j] for p in tok_data) for j in range(4)]
                codes = [[min(3, max(0, int((p[j] - lo[j]) / (hi[j] - lo[j]) * 4))) for j in range(4)] for p in tok_data]
                tok[name]["dead"].append(100.0 * sum(4 - len({c[j] for c in codes}) for j in range(4)) / 16)
                rec = lambda t: [lo[j] + (min(3, max(0, int((t[j] - lo[j]) / (hi[j] - lo[j]) * 4))) + 0.5) * (hi[j] - lo[j]) / 4 for j in range(4)]
                tok[name]["mse"].append(statistics.mean(d2(rec(t), t) / 4 for t in test))
                continue
            books = train_rvq(tok_data, stages, random.Random(2000 + seed))
            used = [set() for _ in books]
            for p in tok_data:
                for s, c in enumerate(encode_decode(p, books)[1]):
                    used[s].add(c)
            tok[name]["dead"].append(100.0 * sum(len(b) - len(u) for b, u in zip(books, used)) / sum(len(b) for b in books))
            tok[name]["mse"].append(statistics.mean(d2(encode_decode(t, books)[0], t) / 4 for t in test))
        rn = random.Random(3000 + seed)
        noise = [[rn.gauss(0.0, 1.0) for _ in range(4)] for _ in range(N_GEN)]
        rp = random.Random(4000 + seed)
        pair_eps = [[rp.gauss(0.0, 1.0) for _ in range(4)] for _ in range(DISTILL_PAIRS)]
        pairs = []
        for e in pair_eps:
            pairs.append((e, euler(e, 5, train)))
        for name in GENERATORS:
            if name.startswith("distill"):
                samples = [knn_student(pairs, e, 1 if name.endswith("nn") else 3) for e in noise]
            else:
                samples = [euler(e, int(name[3:]), train) for e in noise]
            p, r, o = gen_metrics(samples, test)
            gen[name]["prec"].append(p)
            gen[name]["rec"].append(r)
            gen[name]["off"].append(o)
            gen[name]["uniq"].append(100.0 * len({tuple(round(v, 9) for v in x) for x in samples}) / N_GEN)
    print("Synthetic RDT2-style toy: 4-number action chunks, four heading modes; not RDT2, no VLM, no GPU")
    print(f"seeds={len(SEEDS)} tokenizer_train={N_TOK} expert_train={N_TRAIN} test={N_TEST} generated={N_GEN} lloyd_iters={LLOYD_ITERS} distill_pairs={DISTILL_PAIRS} (mean±sd over seeds)")
    print("[part1 tokenizer, equal 8-bit budget per chunk]")
    print("arm tokens_per_chunk codebook_entries heldout_mse dead_entries_%")
    for name, stages in TOKENIZERS:
        print(f"{name} {len(stages)} {sum(stages)} {ms(tok[name]['mse'], '{:.4f}')} {ms(tok[name]['dead'], '{:.1f}')}")
    print("[part2 generator, shared noise draws; expert = exact FM field of smoothed train chunks]")
    print("arm online_nfe offline_expert_nfe precision recall off_manifold_% distinct_outputs_%")
    for name in GENERATORS:
        dist = name.startswith("distill")
        nfe = 1 if dist else int(name[3:])
        off_nfe = DISTILL_PAIRS * 5 if dist else 0
        g = gen[name]
        print(f"{name} {nfe} {off_nfe} {ms(g['prec'])} {ms(g['rec'])} {ms(g['off'], '{:.1f}')} {ms(g['uniq'], '{:.1f}')}")
    print("RDT2 recipe (per the paper): Stage 1 RVQ tokens + cross-entropy, Stage 2 frozen VLM + flow-matching expert, Stage 3 one-step distillation")
    print("No winner is declared; RDT2 task scores 以论文为准; 7B training / GPU / robots: 本页未运行")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### Read the trade-off

**Tokens.** At the same 8 bits, the single 256-entry codebook has the lowest held-out MSE (**0.0024**), but it needs 256 entries. RVQ 2×16 is close (**0.0037**) with 32 entries and 2 tokens. RVQ 4×4 (**0.0060**) uses only 16 entries but 4 tokens. Uniform bins with the same 16 entries and 4 tokens are about four times worse (**0.0261**), because they spend bits on empty corners of the space. No entries went unused at this scale, so the collapse fixes the paper lists were not needed here.

**Steps.** One Euler step from noise lands every sample on the dataset mean: precision **1.062**, 100 % off-manifold, and only 0.5 % distinct outputs (one chunk). Two steps are still mostly between modes (**75.5 %** off-manifold). Five steps reach **0.109** precision and **6.8 %** off-manifold.

**Students.** The nearest-pair student matches the 5-step expert at one online NFE (**0.110**, **7.1 %**). However, it only replays stored outputs (**77.2 %** distinct), and it cost 2000 offline expert calls. Averaging 3 neighbours restores variety (**96.7 %** distinct) but blurs across modes (**37.0 %** off-manifold).

**The toy does not pick a winner.** The fewest tokens cost codebook size. The fewest steps cost either quality or an offline distillation budget and a student that must be re-made whenever the expert changes. Which trade matters depends on your vocabulary, your latency budget and how often you retrain.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating "open source" as "open weights" as "free to use commercially".** Read the licence of the weights, not only of the code. GR00T N1.6's code is Apache-2.0 while its weights are research-only.
- **Forgetting the normalisation statistics.** A VLA outputs normalised actions. Converting them back needs the 1st/99th percentiles (or mean and standard deviation) of the dataset it was fine-tuned on; the wrong statistics give the right shape at the wrong size.
- **Expecting zero-shot on your arm.** Without fine-tuning on your robot, a VLA's joint or end-effector conventions, camera position and gripper may all differ from what it learned. Plan for about 50 demonstrations.
- **Ignoring inference time.** A 7B model needs a large GPU to run fast enough. Measure actions per second on the computer that will drive the robot, and pick a smaller model or chunked execution if it cannot keep up.
- **Calling async inference "free".** It removes idle time only when g is above (E[ℓ_S] / Δt) / n, and it costs more inference calls and more chunk splices (16 → 57 calls at g = 0.7 in the toy). Pick g for your latency and your server budget.
- **Mixing up async inference, RTC and timestamp sync.** Async decides when to request the next chunk (here); RTC smooths the seam with a soft mask (10.2); stream alignment matches sensor samples by time (8.3).
- **Quoting SmolVLA's paper numbers as your own.** The 450M size, the 30 % speed-up and the 19 vs 9 cycles are per the paper; measure on your own arm.
- **Picking bins without looking at the range.** If your task includes motions bigger than anything in the dataset, bin tokenisation clips them, as the 6.2-degree error above shows.

## CoT-VLA: draw the subgoal first, then act

Every VLA above maps the current image and the instruction straight to actions (or to a chunk of them). **CoT-VLA** inserts one intermediate step, a **visual chain of thought**: the model first generates a **subgoal image** of the scene a few frames ahead, and then generates a short action chunk conditioned on the current image **and** that subgoal. The subgoal step needs no action labels, so it can also learn from action-less video. This section is **not** DreamGen's inverse-dynamics labelling of generated videos and **not** a WorldVLA attention mask (both in [world models](/en/month/month-9/world-models-overview)), **not** TraceVLA's drawn traces ([visual servoing](/en/month/month-9/visual-servoing)), **not** MAP-VLA's stage memory prompts ([intervention logging](/en/month/month-10/intervention-logging)) and **not** the text-only chain of thought of a language model.

<Resource id="cotvla-html" />

<Resource id="cotvla-pdf" />

**Sources actually opened (body read).** **CoT-VLA (Qingqing Zhao et al., arXiv 2503.22020, LaTeXML HTML and the PDF via pdftotext, v1).** The base is the 7B VILA-U model, which encodes a 256 × 256 image as 16 × 16 × 4 discrete tokens. A vanilla VLA samples `â_t ~ P(a_t | s_t, l)` (Eq. 1). CoT-VLA first samples a subgoal `ŝ_{t+n} ~ P(s_{t+n} | s_t, l)` (Eq. 2), `n` frames ahead with `n` drawn from a dataset-specific range, and then a chunk `{â_t … â_{t+m}} ~ P(· | s_t, l, ŝ_{t+n})` (Eq. 3). Training sums `L_action + L_visual` (Eq. 6); the subgoal step trains on robot demonstrations **and** action-less video (EPIC-KITCHENS, Something-Something V2), the action step on robot demonstrations only. Image and text use causal attention; the action tokens (256 bins per dimension, chunk size 10) use full attention. Test time (Algorithm 1) is closed loop: subgoal, chunk, execute, observe again. Reported numbers: LIBERO average 81.13 % against 76.5 % for fine-tuned OpenVLA (Table 1); +17 % real-world and +6 % simulation over the best earlier VLA (abstract); on Bridge-V2, CoT-VLA is **below** OpenVLA on the visual (65 % vs 75 %) and language (70 % vs 75 %) categories (Table 2); on Franka-Tabletop, Diffusion Policy is best on single-instruction tasks; ground-truth subgoal images instead of generated ones raise two out-of-distribution tasks from 20 % / 0 % to 60 % / 40 % (Table 3); and generating 256 image tokens before the actions makes inference about **7× slower** with a chunk of 10 (Section 5). All **以论文为准**; the model, LIBERO and the robots: **本页未运行**.

### Where the subgoal helps and what it costs

<Formula label="CoT-VLA: visual chain of thought (Eq. 2–3)">ŝ(t+n) ~ P(s(t+n) | s(t), l),   [â(t) … â(t+m)] ~ P(a(t) … a(t+m) | s(t), l, ŝ(t+n))</Formula>

Splitting the policy this way moves the **task-specific** part into the subgoal model, which learns from video, and leaves the action model with a mostly **task-agnostic** question: how do I get from this image to that one? When a task has plenty of action-labelled demonstrations, a direct policy already has what it needs and the subgoal only adds cost. When a task has few action labels but many videos, the subgoal model can know the way even though the direct policy does not. If the subgoal is not refreshed, the robot reaches it and then waits.

**Worked example: the token bill.**

```text
action: 7 tokens, chunk M = 4 actions -> 28 tokens per chunk (A_direct)
subgoal image: 256 tokens -> 256 + 28 = 284 tokens per chunk (B_cot), 10.1x
C_cot_stale refreshes every 2nd chunk -> (256 + 2 x 28) / 2 = 156 tokens per chunk on average
paper (chunk m = 10): 256 + 70 = 326 against 70 tokens = 4.7x, reported ~7x wall-clock (以论文为准)
```

The token count is a **cost count, not a timing**: a real model's latency depends on how image and action tokens are decoded (CoT-VLA decodes actions in parallel). The ratio still shows why the subgoal is never free.

**No integration step here.** The track is a grid of cells and an action moves the robot by −1, 0 or +1 cell; nothing is stepped in continuous time, so no step-size stability criterion applies. The independent tests check that the toy has no time-step constant and that every arm reads the same task, data and start cells.

### Fair setup

`cot_vla_subgoal_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:** a 1-D track of 24 cells; a task is an ordered list of waypoints (3 in the multi-stage regimes, 1 in `single_reach`), and an episode succeeds if every waypoint is reached in order within 60 steps. The observation that stands in for an image is `(cell, stage)`, where stage counts the waypoints already reached; the subgoal "image" is a future `(cell, stage)`, not pixels and not a video model. All data come from one noisy demonstrator that takes a random action 20 % of the time. Policies are **count tables, not networks**: A maps `(cell, stage)` to the most common next 4 actions in the task's action demos; B maps `(cell, stage)` to the most common state 4 steps later in the task's action demos **and** videos, cut at the first stage change so that a subgoal never skips a waypoint, and a task-agnostic table maps the direction to the subgoal to an action, learned from the task's action demos plus the 30 demos of other tasks on the track. Unseen keys fall back to the task's most common demo action for both A and B. Chunks are 4 actions for every arm, executed open loop and followed by a new observation. Metrics: success (%), steps used (capped at 60, so a failing arm's steps are a lower bound) and decoded tokens per episode.

**Design iterations, disclosed.** Three choices were made after seeing intermediate numbers, and each applies to every regime and arm alike. (1) A first draft let the subgoal look 4 steps ahead even past a stage change, so the direction → action table walked straight past waypoints and B failed everywhere; the **cut at the first stage change** fixed that bug and is now a stated assumption. (2) The same draft gave B a "stay" fallback on unseen keys while A had the task's most common demo action; that was unequal, so both now use the same prior. (3) **`STALE = 2` was chosen after seeing the results** for 2 and 3: with 3, C fails every multi-stage episode on every seed through the step budget alone, so the toy prints **both** — the main table uses `STALE = 2`, and a second block reruns C with `STALE_ALT = 3` against the same B.

| Arm | Chunk chosen from |
|---|---|
| A_direct | `(cell, stage)` → next 4 actions, from the task's action demos |
| B_cot | `(cell, stage)` → subgoal, from action demos and videos; then direction → action; subgoal refreshed every chunk |
| C_cot_stale | B's tables, but the subgoal is refreshed only every 2nd chunk (`STALE = 2`; the `STALE_ALT = 3` block reruns C every 3rd chunk) |

**Common random numbers:** for each (regime, seed) the task, the action demos of the task, the 30 demos of other tasks, the videos and the 40 test start cells are drawn once and shared by all three arms. The arms differ **only** in how a chunk is chosen. Five seeds; reported as mean±sd over seeds.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""CoT-VLA-style visual chain of thought: predict a subgoal state, then a short action chunk.

Synthetic, stdlib only.  Not DreamGen's inverse-dynamics labelling (Month 9),
not TraceVLA's drawn traces, not MAP-VLA memory prompts (Month 10), not
WorldVLA.  Real CoT-VLA (7B VILA-U, 256 image tokens per subgoal) / GPU:
本页未运行.  Paper numbers are 以论文为准 (arXiv 2503.22020).

World: a 1-D track with cells 0..W-1 and a task = an ordered list of waypoints.
The observation (the stand-in "image") is (cell, stage); stage counts waypoints
already reached.  An episode succeeds if every waypoint is reached in order
within T_MAX steps.  Actions are -1, 0, +1, executed as chunks of M actions;
after each chunk the policy observes again (closed loop).  Discrete grid moves:
there is no time-step integration.
Data, all from one noisy demonstrator (with probability EPS it takes a random action):
    action demos D_r   (cell, stage, actions) for the test task: K_ACT of them,
                       plus REST demos of other tasks on the same track
    action-less videos D_v  (cell, stage) sequences of the test task: K_VID of them
Arms (each predicts one chunk of M actions per observation):
    A_direct      count table (task, cell, stage) -> next M actions, from D_r of the task
    B_cot         subgoal table (task, cell, stage) -> state N_AHEAD steps later (cut at the first
                  stage change, so a subgoal never skips a waypoint), from D_r and D_v of the task; then a task-agnostic action table (direction to subgoal) -> action,
                  learned from all D_r on the track; the subgoal is refreshed every chunk
    C_cot_stale   B's two tables, but the subgoal is refreshed only every STALE-th chunk
STALE = 2 was chosen after seeing the runs with 2 and 3; both are printed (the main
table uses STALE = 2, a second block repeats C with STALE_ALT = 3 against the same B).
Unseen keys: both fall back to the task's most common demo action: A repeats it
for the chunk, B/C put the subgoal N_AHEAD cells that way.
Common random numbers: per (regime, seed) the task, the demos, the videos and
the start cells are drawn once and shared by all three arms.
Tokens per episode count the decoded tokens: an action is 7 tokens and a
subgoal image 256 tokens, the paper's sizes; this is a cost count, not a timing.
"""
import math
import random
from collections import Counter

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
W = 24                    # track cells
M = 4                     # action chunk length
N_AHEAD = 4               # subgoal horizon in steps
STALE = 2                 # C refreshes its subgoal every 2nd chunk (picked after seeing 2 and 3)
STALE_ALT = 3             # the other value that was run; printed as a second block
EPS = 0.2                 # demonstrator random-action rate
T_MAX = 60                # step budget per episode
TESTS = 40                # test episodes per seed (shared start cells)
REST = 30                 # action demos of other tasks on the same track
TOK_ACTION, TOK_IMAGE = 7, 256
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9           # |m| and sd both below this: floating-point residue, not a gap

# name: (waypoints per task, action demos of the test task K_ACT, videos of the test task K_VID)
REGIMES = {
    "multi_stage_video": (3, 1, 30),
    "multi_stage_no_video": (3, 1, 0),
    "multi_stage_action_rich": (3, 30, 30),
    "single_reach": (1, 30, 30),
}
ARMS = ("A_direct", "B_cot", "C_cot_stale")


def sign(x):
    return (x > 0) - (x < 0)


def make_task(rng, n_way):
    pts, prev = [], rng.randrange(W)
    for _ in range(n_way):
        nxt = prev
        while abs(nxt - prev) < 5:
            nxt = rng.randrange(W)
        pts.append(nxt)
        prev = nxt
    return tuple(pts)


def demo(rng, task, start):
    """Noisy demonstrator; returns states (cell, stage) and the action taken in each state."""
    cell, stage, states, acts = start, 0, [], []
    for _ in range(T_MAX):
        if stage == len(task):
            break
        a = sign(task[stage] - cell) if rng.random() >= EPS else rng.choice((-1, 0, 1))
        states.append((cell, stage))
        acts.append(a)
        cell = min(W - 1, max(0, cell + a))
        if cell == task[stage]:
            stage += 1
    states.append((cell, stage))
    return states, acts


def shared_draws(regime, seed):
    """Common random numbers: one task, one data set, one list of start cells for all arms."""
    n_way, k_act, k_vid = REGIMES[regime]
    rng = random.Random(f"{regime}/{seed}")
    task = make_task(rng, n_way)
    own = [demo(rng, task, rng.randrange(W)) for _ in range(k_act)]
    others = []
    for _ in range(REST):
        other = make_task(rng, n_way)
        others.append(demo(rng, other, rng.randrange(W)))
    videos = [demo(rng, task, rng.randrange(W))[0] for _ in range(k_vid)]
    starts = [rng.randrange(W) for _ in range(TESTS)]
    return {"task": task, "own": own, "others": others, "videos": videos, "starts": starts}


def fit(d):
    chunk = {}
    for states, acts in d["own"]:
        for i in range(len(acts)):
            seq = tuple(acts[i:i + M]) + (0,) * max(0, i + M - len(acts))
            chunk.setdefault(states[i], Counter())[seq] += 1
    prior = Counter(a for _, acts in d["own"] for a in acts)
    fallback = (prior.most_common(1)[0][0] if prior else 0,) * M
    sub = {}
    for states in [s for s, _ in d["own"]] + d["videos"]:
        for i in range(len(states) - 1):
            j = min(i + N_AHEAD, len(states) - 1)
            for jj in range(i + 1, j + 1):      # never look past the first stage change
                if states[jj][1] != states[i][1]:
                    j = jj
                    break
            sub.setdefault(states[i], Counter())[states[j]] += 1
    toward = {}
    for states, acts in d["own"] + d["others"]:
        for i, a in enumerate(acts):
            j = min(i + N_AHEAD, len(states) - 1)
            toward.setdefault(sign(states[j][0] - states[i][0]), Counter())[a] += 1
    act_of = {k: c.most_common(1)[0][0] for k, c in toward.items()}
    return ({k: c.most_common(1)[0][0] for k, c in chunk.items()}, fallback,
            {k: c.most_common(1)[0][0] for k, c in sub.items()}, act_of)


def run(regime, seed, arm, stale=None):
    stale = STALE if stale is None else stale
    d = shared_draws(regime, seed)
    task = d["task"]
    chunk, fallback, sub, act_of = fit(d)
    wins, steps_used, tokens = 0, 0, 0
    for start in d["starts"]:
        cell, stage, t, k, goal = start, 0, 0, 0, None
        while stage < len(task) and t < T_MAX:
            obs = (cell, stage)
            if arm == "A_direct":
                plan = chunk.get(obs, fallback)
                tokens += TOK_ACTION * M
            else:
                if goal is None or arm == "B_cot" or k % stale == 0:
                    goal = sub.get(obs, (min(W - 1, max(0, cell + N_AHEAD * fallback[0])), stage))
                    tokens += TOK_IMAGE
                tokens += TOK_ACTION * M
                plan = []
                c = cell
                for _ in range(M):
                    a = act_of.get(sign(goal[0] - c), 0) if c != goal[0] else 0
                    plan.append(a)
                    c = min(W - 1, max(0, c + a))
            k += 1
            for a in plan:
                if stage == len(task) or t >= T_MAX:
                    break
                cell = min(W - 1, max(0, cell + a))
                t += 1
                if cell == task[stage]:
                    stage += 1
        wins += stage == len(task)
        steps_used += t
    return {"succ": 100.0 * wins / TESTS, "steps": steps_used / TESTS, "tok": tokens / TESTS}


def mean_sd(xs):
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x):
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-4:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def fmt_pair(m, s):
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}"


def verdict(xa, xb):
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{fmt_pair(m, s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


def cell_str(xs):
    mu, sd = mean_sd(xs)
    return f"{num(mu)}+-{num(sd)}"
```

## MolmoAct: reason in space, then act

The CoT-VLA section above predicts one subgoal image before acting, and the ThinkAct section in Month 10 (real-world-rl) reasons in a visual plan latent rewarded by RL. **MolmoAct** takes a third route. It decodes three token chains in order: depth perception tokens, a short **visual reasoning trace** drawn on the image, and then the action tokens. Because the trace is an explicit set of image points, a user can edit it to steer the robot. This section tests that idea on a small synthetic stand-in. It is **not** a redo of CoT-VLA, **not** ThinkAct, and **not** TraceVLA (which draws a tracker's history onto the input image as a prompt).

  - **Depth perception tokens** (eqs. 1–2): a VQVAE codebook of N = 128 codes, M = 100 tokens per image, distilled from a depth specialist trained on Depth Anything V2 depth maps.
  - **Visual reasoning trace** (eq. 3): a polyline of 1 to 5 image points, coordinates in 0–255; the first point is the current gripper position and the rest are later gripper positions subsampled from the episode. Labels come from Molmo pointing at the gripper in every frame.

<Formula label="Action reasoning factorisation (MolmoAct eq. 4)">p(d, τ, a ∣ I, T) = ∏ p(d_i ∣ I, T, d_before_i) · ∏ p(τ_j ∣ I, T, d, τ_before_j) · ∏ p(a_k ∣ I, T, d, τ, a_before_k)</Formula>

<Formula label="Visual reasoning trace (MolmoAct eq. 3)">τ = (p1, …, pL),  1 ≤ L ≤ 5,  pi = (ui, vi)</Formula>

The trace is only 2-D. It says where the gripper should appear in the image, not how far it is from the camera; depth has to come from somewhere else, and in MolmoAct that is the depth token chain. That split is exactly what the toy's last regime probes.

**Worked example.** The toy quantises depth into `N_DEPTH = 16` levels, a coarse stand-in for the paper's 128 codes. A bin at depth `z = 0.43` falls in level `int(0.43 · 16) = 6`, whose token depth is `(6 + 0.5) / 16 = 0.40625`: an error of 0.024, under the worst case `1 / (2 · 16) = 0.03125`. With the 0.06 placing tolerance, quantisation alone never fails a placement, but it adds to the perception noise.

**No integration step here.** The gripper is kinematic: each step moves it at most `V = 0.05` towards the commanded point. **Correctness criterion for the wall.** Every step's segment is checked for the wall at `SUB = 10` points, and the toy asserts `V / SUB = 0.005 < WALL_T = 0.06`, so a step cannot pass through the wall between two checks. A test checks that a segment through the wall is caught and one above it is not.

### Fair setup

`molmoact_reasoning_trace_toy.py` is **synthetic** and stdlib only. **Model assumptions:**
- A front camera sees the image plane (`u` left-right, `v` height); `z` is depth. The gripper must pick an object from the table and place it in a bin within 80 steps.
- In the wall regimes a wall (height 0.45) stands between object and bin, so the path needs a lift; touching the wall fails the episode.
- Every arm sees the same scene with the same pixel noise (sd 0.01) and depth noise (sd 0.02).
- The trace is built by a fixed rule from the perceived scene. It stands in for a trace head trained on plenty of trace data, so the toy measures what a correct, editable trace buys and what a wrong one costs, **not** how hard a trace is to predict.

| Arm | Model |
|---|---|
| A_direct | k-nearest-neighbour policy (k = 3) from observation features straight to the action, learned from the regime's in-task demonstrations |
| B_trace | depth tokens, then a trace of at most 5 points (gripper, object, lift, over, bin), then a step towards the next trace point at the depth read from the depth token under it |
| C_wrong | B with the trace mirrored left-right (`u → 1 − u`); in the steering regimes, the frozen trace to the other bin |

**Common random numbers:** for each (regime, seed) the demonstrations, test scenes, perception noise, start points and intended bins are drawn once and shared by all three arms; 40 test episodes per seed, five seeds.

| Regime | Wall | In-task demos | Steering | Expected before the run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| multi_stage_few | yes | 5 | none | B consistent win over A |
| multi_stage_many | yes | 200 | none | near-tie (A has enough data) |
| single_step | no | 5 | none | near-tie; B may lose a little to depth quantisation |
| steer_in_plane | no | 5 | two bins left and right, edited trace | B beats A and the frozen C |
| steer_depth | no | 5 | two bins at the same image point, one behind the other | B ties or loses: a 2-D edit cannot choose along depth |

`single_step` means no wall, so there is no lift stage; it is still a pick followed by a place.

**Choices made after seeing results.** No parameter, regime or seed was changed. Two code changes came after a run and are disclosed here:
- The first invocation stopped with an error inside the first regime, before any arm's numbers were printed: C's trace looked up a bin index that only exists in the steering regimes. The fix gives C the mirrored trace to the intended bin, as the docstring already described.
- After the first full run the neighbour search was rewritten with an early exit (`nearest`) to cut the run time from about 57 s to about 22 s. The output is byte-identical (compared with `cmp`), and a test checks `nearest` against a full sort.
- Alternatives not taken: keeping the slower `heapq` search; renaming `single_step`.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""MolmoAct-style action reasoning: depth tokens -> editable 2-D visual trace -> action, vs a direct policy.

Synthetic, stdlib only.  Not ThinkAct (language reasoning plans), not CoT-VLA (one predicted
subgoal image), not TraceVLA (trace drawn as a visual prompt from a tracker).  Real MolmoAct
(7B, 100 depth tokens from a VQVAE, LIBERO / SimplerEnv) / GPU: 本页未运行.  Paper numbers are
以论文为准 (arXiv 2508.07917).

World: a front camera sees the image plane (u = left-right, v = height); z is depth (distance
from the camera).  Coordinates are in [0, 1].  A gripper starts at a random point and must pick an
object resting on the table (v = V_TABLE) and place it in a bin, within T_MAX steps.  In wall
regimes a wall (thickness WALL_T, height WALL_H, all depths) stands between object and bin, so
the gripper must lift over it; touching the wall fails the episode.  A step moves the gripper by
at most V towards the commanded point.  No integration step: the gripper is kinematic; every step's
segment is checked for the wall at SUB points, and V / SUB < WALL_T is asserted so a step cannot
tunnel through the wall.
Perception, shared by every arm (common random numbers): the object, bin and wall positions are
seen with pixel noise PIX_SD in u, v and depth noise Z_SD in z.
Arms:
  A_direct     k-nearest-neighbour policy (K_NN = 3) from observation features straight to the
               action, learned from DEMOS in-task demonstrations (state-action pairs of a noisy
               demonstrator on random scenes of the same regime).
  B_trace      MolmoAct-style: depth perception tokens (observed depth quantised to N_DEPTH
               levels), then a visual reasoning trace of at most 5 image-plane points (gripper,
               object, lift, over, bin, as in the paper's 1 <= L <= 5 polyline), then a
               trajectory-conditioned action: step towards the next trace point, at the depth
               read from the depth token under that point.  The trace is built by a fixed rule
               from the perceived scene: it stands in for a trace head trained on plenty of
               trace data, so this toy measures what a correct, editable trace buys and what a
               wrong one costs, not how hard a trace is to predict.
  C_wrong      B with the trace mirrored left-right (u -> 1 - u): a wrong / frozen trace.
Steering regimes: the instruction names no bin and there are two bins.  The intended bin is
drawn per episode.  A cannot be steered (its features see both bins).  B's trace is edited by
the user to end at the intended bin (the edit is 2-D: image-plane points only).  C keeps the
trace to the other bin.
Regimes and expectations, written before the first run:
  multi_stage_few     wall, DEMOS = 5      B consistent win over A
  multi_stage_many    wall, DEMOS = 200    near-tie (A has enough in-task data)
  single_step         no wall, DEMOS = 5   near-tie (B may lose a little to depth quantisation)
  steer_in_plane      two bins left / right, edited trace: B beats A and the frozen C
  steer_depth         two bins at the same (u, v), one behind the other: a 2-D edit cannot say
                      which; B ties or loses (the paper's out-of-plane limitation)
Metrics per seed (TESTS episodes): success %, final 3-D distance to the intended bin (x100), and
for B / C the 2-D distance from the trace end to the intended bin (x100).
"""
import math
import random

SEEDS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
V = 0.05            # max step length
SUB = 10            # wall check points per step
R = 0.06            # pick / place tolerance (3-D)
R_TRACE = 0.04      # advance to the next trace point within this distance
T_MAX = 80
V_TABLE = 0.05
WALL_T = 0.06
WALL_H = 0.45
LIFT = 0.12         # demonstrator / trace clearance above the wall
PIX_SD = 0.01
Z_SD = 0.02
N_DEPTH = 16        # depth token levels (the paper uses a 128-code VQVAE; a coarse stand-in)
A_SD = 0.005        # demonstrator action noise
K_NN = 3
TESTS = 40
NUM_ZERO = 1e-9
ARMS = ("A_direct", "B_trace", "C_wrong")
# name: (wall, in-task demos, steering: None / "plane" / "depth")
REGIMES = {
    "multi_stage_few": (True, 5, None),
    "multi_stage_many": (True, 200, None),
    "single_step": (False, 5, None),
    "steer_in_plane": (False, 5, "plane"),
    "steer_depth": (False, 5, "depth"),
}


def sub(a, b):
    return [x - y for x, y in zip(a, b)]


def norm(a):
    return math.sqrt(sum(x * x for x in a))


def scene(rng, wall, steer):
    """True scene: object, bins (one or two), wall u; the object is left of the bin."""
    obj = [rng.uniform(0.1, 0.3), V_TABLE, rng.uniform(0.3, 0.7)]
    if steer == "plane":
        bins = [[rng.uniform(0.45, 0.6), V_TABLE, rng.uniform(0.3, 0.7)], [rng.uniform(0.75, 0.9), V_TABLE, 0.0]]
        bins[1][2] = rng.uniform(0.3, 0.7)
    elif steer == "depth":
        u, z = rng.uniform(0.6, 0.9), rng.uniform(0.25, 0.4)
        bins = [[u, V_TABLE, z], [u, V_TABLE, z + 0.3]]
    else:
        bins = [[rng.uniform(0.7, 0.9), V_TABLE, rng.uniform(0.3, 0.7)]]
    wall_u = rng.uniform(0.45, 0.55) if wall else None
    return {"obj": obj, "bins": bins, "wall": wall_u}


def hits_wall(p, q, wall_u):
    if wall_u is None:
        return False
    for i in range(SUB + 1):
        u = p[0] + (q[0] - p[0]) * i / SUB
        v = p[1] + (q[1] - p[1]) * i / SUB
        if abs(u - wall_u) <= WALL_T / 2 and v <= WALL_H:
            return True
    return False


def step_to(g, target):
    d = sub(target, g)
    n = norm(d)
    if n <= V:
        return list(target)
    return [x + V * y / n for x, y in zip(g, d)]


def waypoints(sc, b, wall_u):
    """Demonstrator / trace points after the object: lift, over, bin (lift only with a wall)."""
    o, k = sc["obj"], sc["bins"][b]
    if wall_u is None:
        return [k]
    top = WALL_H + LIFT
    return [[o[0], top, o[2]], [k[0], top, k[2]], k]


def features(g, held, sc, wall_u):
    """A's observation features, relative to the gripper; with two bins both are seen."""
    f = [float(held)]
    if not held:
        f += sub(sc["obj"], g)
    else:
        f += [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    for k in sc["bins"]:
        f += sub(k, g) if held else [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    if wall_u is not None:
        f += [wall_u - g[0], WALL_H - g[1]]
    return f


def demos(rng, wall, steer, n):
    """State-action pairs from a noisy demonstrator; in steering regimes the bin is 50/50."""
    data = []
    for _ in range(n):
        sc = scene(rng, wall, steer)
        b = rng.randrange(len(sc["bins"]))
        g = [rng.uniform(0.05, 0.35), rng.uniform(0.2, 0.4), rng.uniform(0.3, 0.7)]
        plan = [sc["obj"]] + waypoints(sc, b, sc["wall"])
        held, i = 0, 0
        for _ in range(T_MAX):
            nxt = step_to(g, plan[i])
            a = [x - y + rng.gauss(0.0, A_SD) for x, y in zip(nxt, g)]
            data.append((features(g, held, sc, sc["wall"]), a))
            g = [x + y for x, y in zip(g, a)]
            if norm(sub(g, plan[i])) <= R / 2:
                if i == 0:
                    held = 1
                i += 1
                if i == len(plan):
                    break
    return data


def perceive(sc, rng):
    def p(x):
        return [x[0] + rng.gauss(0.0, PIX_SD), x[1] + rng.gauss(0.0, PIX_SD), x[2] + rng.gauss(0.0, Z_SD)]
    return {"obj": p(sc["obj"]), "bins": [p(k) for k in sc["bins"]], "wall": None if sc["wall"] is None else sc["wall"] + rng.gauss(0.0, PIX_SD)}


def depth_token(z):
    lvl = min(N_DEPTH - 1, max(0, int(z * N_DEPTH)))
    return (lvl + 0.5) / N_DEPTH


def depth_under(seen, uv):
    """Depth token at an image point: the nearest surface (smallest z) among scene items there."""
    zs = [x[2] for x in [seen["obj"]] + seen["bins"] if abs(x[0] - uv[0]) < 0.05 and abs(x[1] - uv[1]) < 0.05]
    return depth_token(min(zs)) if zs else None


def trace(seen, g, b, mirror):
    pts = [seen["obj"]] + waypoints(seen, b, seen["wall"])
    uv = [[p[0], p[1]] for p in [g] + pts][:5]
    if mirror:
        uv = [uv[0]] + [[1.0 - u, v] for u, v in uv[1:]]
    return uv


def run_trace(sc, seen, g, b_trace, mirror):
    """B / C: follow the 2-D trace; depth from the depth token under each point (else hold depth)."""
    uv = trace(seen, g, b_trace, mirror)
    zt = depth_token(seen["obj"][2])
    pts = []
    for i, (u, v) in enumerate(uv[1:]):
        z = depth_under(seen, (u, v))
        if z is None:
            z = depth_token(seen["obj"][2]) if i == 0 else zt
        zt = z
        pts.append([u, v, z])
    return pts, uv[-1]


def episode(arm, sc, seen, g, want, wall, table, mirror_other):
    held, i = 0, 0
    if arm != "A_direct":
        b_trace = mirror_other if (arm == "C_wrong" and mirror_other is not None) else want
        pts, end = run_trace(sc, seen, g, b_trace, arm == "C_wrong" and mirror_other is None)
    for _ in range(T_MAX):
        if arm == "A_direct":
            f = features(g, held, seen, seen["wall"])
            near = nearest(table, f)
            a = [sum(r[j] for r in near) / K_NN for j in range(3)]
            n = norm(a)
            q = [x + y * (min(1.0, V / n) if n > 0 else 0.0) for x, y in zip(g, a)]
        else:
            q = step_to(g, pts[i])
            if norm(sub(q, pts[i])) <= R_TRACE and i < len(pts) - 1:
                i += 1
        if hits_wall(g, q, sc["wall"]):
            return 0.0, norm(sub(g, sc["bins"][want])), None if arm == "A_direct" else norm(sub(end, sc["bins"][want][:2]))
        g = q
        if not held and norm(sub(g, sc["obj"])) <= R:
            held = 1
        if held and norm(sub(g, sc["bins"][want])) <= R:
            return 1.0, norm(sub(g, sc["bins"][want])), None if arm == "A_direct" else norm(sub(end, sc["bins"][want][:2]))
    return 0.0, norm(sub(g, sc["bins"][want])), None if arm == "A_direct" else norm(sub(end, sc["bins"][want][:2]))


def shared_draws(regime, seed):
    """Common random numbers: demos, scenes, perception noise, starts and intended bins drawn once."""
    wall, n, steer = REGIMES[regime]
    rng = random.Random(1000 * seed + sorted(REGIMES).index(regime))
    table = demos(rng, wall, steer, n)
    tests = []
    for _ in range(TESTS):
        sc = scene(rng, wall, steer)
        seen = perceive(sc, rng)
        g = [rng.uniform(0.05, 0.35), rng.uniform(0.2, 0.4), rng.uniform(0.3, 0.7)]
        want = rng.randrange(len(sc["bins"]))
        tests.append((sc, seen, g, want))
    return table, tests


def evaluate(arm, regime, draw):
    wall, _, steer = REGIMES[regime]
    table, tests = draw
    succ, err, terr = [], [], []
    for sc, seen, g, want in tests:
        other = None if steer is None else 1 - want
        s, e, t = episode(arm, sc, seen, list(g), want, wall, table, other)
        succ.append(s)
        err.append(e)
        if t is not None:
            terr.append(t)
    out = {"succ": 100.0 * sum(succ) / len(succ), "err": 100.0 * sum(err) / len(err)}
    out["trace"] = 100.0 * sum(terr) / len(terr) if terr else None
    return out


def nearest(table, f):
    """The K_NN rows nearest to f, ordered by (distance, row index): same as heapq.nsmallest, with early exit."""
    best = []
    worst = float("inf")
    for idx, (row, act) in enumerate(table):
        d = 0.0
        for x, y in zip(row, f):
            d += (x - y) * (x - y)
            if d >= worst:
                break
        else:
            best.append((d, idx, act))
            best.sort(key=lambda b: (b[0], b[1]))
            if len(best) > K_NN:
                best.pop()
            if len(best) == K_NN:
                worst = best[-1][0]
    return [b[2] for b in best]


def mean_sd(xs):
    mu = sum(xs) / len(xs)
    return mu, math.sqrt(sum((x - mu) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1))


def num(x):
    if x != 0.0 and abs(x) < 5e-5:
        return f"{x:.3e}"
    return f"{x:.4f}"


def sign_note(diffs, m):
    n, opp, zero = len(diffs), sum(1 for d in diffs if d * m < 0), sum(1 for d in diffs if d == 0)
    if opp == 0 and zero == 0:
        return f"same sign on all {n} seeds"
    if opp == 0:
        return f"no seed has the opposite sign, {zero} of {n} equal"
    note = f"{opp} of {n} seeds {'has' if opp == 1 else 'have'} the opposite sign"
    return note + (f", {zero} of {n} equal" if zero else "")


def verdict(xa, xb):
    diffs = [a - b for a, b in zip(xa, xb)]
    if all(d == 0.0 for d in diffs):
        return "0.0000+-0.0000: tie: identical on every seed"
    m, s = mean_sd(diffs)
    if abs(m) < NUM_ZERO and s < NUM_ZERO:
        return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie"
    if abs(m) <= s:
        label = "near-tie / small gap, within seed noise (|m| <= sd)"
    elif abs(m) < 0.2 * min(abs(mean_sd(xa)[0]), abs(mean_sd(xb)[0])):
        label = "small but consistent gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    else:
        label = "clear gap, not a tie (|m| > sd)"
    return f"{num(m)}+-{num(s)}: {label}; {sign_note(diffs, m)}"


def main():
    assert V / SUB < WALL_T
    print("Synthetic MolmoAct-style depth tokens -> 2-D trace -> action vs a direct kNN policy vs a mirrored trace; not a 7B model")
    print(f"V={V} SUB={SUB} R={R} T_MAX={T_MAX} WALL_T={WALL_T} WALL_H={WALL_H} PIX_SD={PIX_SD} Z_SD={Z_SD} N_DEPTH={N_DEPTH} K_NN={K_NN} tests={TESTS} seeds={len(SEEDS)}")
    print(f"no integration step: kinematic steps of at most V, wall checked at {SUB} points per step (V/SUB={V / SUB:.3f} < WALL_T={WALL_T})")
    print("regime arm success_% final_err_x100 trace_end_err_x100  (mean+-sd over seeds)")
    for regime, (wall, n, steer) in REGIMES.items():
        print(f"{regime} (wall {'yes' if wall else 'no'}; in-task demos {n}; steering {steer or 'none'})")
        draws = [shared_draws(regime, s) for s in SEEDS]
        res = {arm: [evaluate(arm, regime, d) for d in draws] for arm in ARMS}
        for arm in ARMS:
            cols = ["{}+-{}".format(*map(num, mean_sd([r[k] for r in res[arm]]))) for k in ("succ", "err")]
            cols.append("n/a" if res[arm][0]["trace"] is None else "{}+-{}".format(*map(num, mean_sd([r["trace"] for r in res[arm]]))))
            print(f"  {arm} " + " ".join(cols))
        for x, y in (("B_trace", "A_direct"), ("C_wrong", "B_trace")):
            for key in ("succ", "err"):
                print(f"  paired {x[0]}-{y[0]} {key}: {verdict([r[key] for r in res[x]], [r[key] for r in res[y]])}")
    print("No winner declared: a correct, editable trace helps where the path has stages; a 2-D edit cannot choose along depth")
    print("MolmoAct-7B / LIBERO / SimplerEnv / GPU: 本页未运行; paper numbers 以论文为准")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### Read the regimes

How the labels are used on this page, in this order: **tie: identical on every seed** only when the paired per-seed difference is exactly zero on every seed; **numerically zero (floating-point residue), effectively a tie** when `|paired mean|` and its sd are both below 1e-9 (no sign count is printed on such a line); **near-tie / small gap, within seed noise** when `|paired mean| ≤ sd`; otherwise a **consistent** gap (`|paired mean| > sd`), called **small** if `|m|` is under 20 % of the smaller arm mean and **clear** otherwise; the 20 % rule only chooses between those two words. Every consistent line carries a same-sign count over the 5 seeds, and any seed with the opposite sign is reported; a sign count alone never makes a gap consistent. Gaps are computed from unrounded values. Paired differences are B−A and C−B. `succ` is success in %, `err` the final 3-D distance to the intended bin (×100).

- **multi_stage_few:**

  - The mirrored trace loses with a clear gap: C−B success −92.5000±3.5355, same sign on all 5 seeds.
- **multi_stage_many:**
